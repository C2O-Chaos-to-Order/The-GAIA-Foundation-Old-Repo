# Report 5 — Consciousness

## Definition

Consciousness concerns subjective experience: there being something it is like to undergo an experience. It is distinct from wakefulness, attention, self-report, intelligence, or behavioral performance, though these can be related.

## Evidence

The Cogitate Consortium’s 2025 adversarial comparison of global neuronal workspace theory (GNWT) and integrated information theory (IIT), published in *Nature* (doi:10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1), used preregistered predictions, multimodal neural measurements (fMRI, MEG, iEEG), and n=256 participants. Reported results aligned with some predictions of both theories (e.g., content decoding in relevant cortices) while substantially challenging key tenets of each (absence of sustained posterior synchronization for core IIT claims; incomplete support for full prefrontal broadcasting and offset ignition for certain GNWT claims).

## Perspectives

Neural theories seek mechanisms associated with experience; philosophy of mind asks whether and how mechanism explains subjectivity; spiritual interpretations may assign additional meaning to awareness. The adversarial design illustrates a productive way to test competing theories without assuming either is correct a priori.

## Relationships

Bodily signals (including interoception), attention, memory, perception, and report are relevant to consciousness research. None alone is a complete proxy for subjective experience. The unified framework treats these as typed relationships requiring separate evidence.

## Uncertainty

A visual-stimulus study cannot settle all questions about dreams, anesthesia, meditation, animal consciousness, artificial systems, or the metaphysical status of awareness. Full methods, data, code, and independent replications require audit before any stronger claims.

## Verification

Future work should audit full methods, preregistration (OSF), data, alternative analyses, and independent replication. A system’s self-description alone is not sufficient evidence of consciousness. Status remains Documented / Preliminary; not Verified.

## Conclusions

Consciousness remains an active, unresolved research and philosophical problem. Competing theories should be tested using discriminating, preregistered predictions. The 2025 Cogitate results neither confirm nor refute either major theory in full.
