## Purpose

Pause intake of new numbered reports while the existing research corpus is audited and strengthened. The aim is not to force all reports into empirical science: empirical claims need empirical evidence; mathematical models need declared assumptions and reproducible analysis; historical claims need primary-source grounding; philosophical and spiritual interpretations need clear scope, fair representation, and safeguards against category errors.

The audit must preserve the author's conceptual architecture while improving truthfulness, traceability, internal consistency, and evidential discipline. No prior version is silently deleted or overwritten. No repository visibility changes, repository creation, issues, merges, deployments, or implementation actions are authorized by this document.

## Global completion criteria

Every canonical report must have:

1. **Canonical identity and provenance:** stable path, report number, title, version history, and source-of-truth designation.
2. **Claim register:** material claims classified as empirical, mathematical/model-based, historical, interpretive, philosophical/theological, or speculative; each claim linked to evidence or explicitly marked unsupported/unresolved.
3. **Source audit:** full bibliographic identity; accessible primary source where possible; source type and publication status; exact support for the associated claim; date-sensitive checks where relevant. A citation is not sufficient merely because its topic is related.
4. **Technical accuracy:** equations, quantities, definitions, units, causal claims, and terminology checked; corrections and unresolved disagreements recorded.
5. **Counterarguments and falsifiability:** strongest relevant alternative explanations, boundary conditions, failure cases, and operational criteria. Interpretive claims are not assigned fake numerical tests.
6. **Cross-report consistency:** shared vocabulary, definitions, dependencies, and tensions checked against Reports 1–19 and the Transformative Intelligence architecture. Symbolic analogy, ordinary causal coupling, information-theoretic dependence, and physical quantum entanglement remain distinct.
7. **Reproducibility:** any computation/simulation has model/code, data provenance, parameters, baselines, seeds where relevant, outputs, failures, and reproduction instructions. Proposed simulations remain design-only until actually executed.
8. **Risk and use boundaries:** assumptions, potential misuse, uncertainty, limitations, and claims not established by the report.
9. **Independent review readiness:** concise questions and evidence package prepared for appropriate specialists; internal review is not described as external peer review.
10. **Readiness decision:** Pass / Fail / Indeterminate for each applicable gate. Indeterminate is never Pass. No unsupported “complete” status.

## Audit sequence

### Gate A — Inventory and provenance
- Establish the canonical file for each report and architecture revision.
- Record missing report numbers, placeholder folders, duplicate historical copies, and README/index inconsistencies.
- Preserve all files until any cleanup is explicitly reviewed.

### Gate B — Claim and source audit
- Extract material claims and source citations.
- Locate primary sources, verify bibliographic details and publication status, and check claim-level support.
- Mark unavailable or mismatched sources as unresolved rather than inferring their contents.
- Prioritize high-impact claims involving consciousness, quantum physics, Earth-system science, geophysics, chemistry, medicine/biology, and quantitative information-processing estimates.

### Gate C — Scientific and conceptual correction
- Correct demonstrable factual errors and category mistakes.
- Separate observations, established theory, contested hypotheses, model assumptions, metaphor, and metaphysical interpretation.
- Retain original arguments and version history where practical; document material changes.

### Gate D — Cross-report integration
- Normalize terms and claim types across Reports 1–19.
- Build an explicit dependency/cross-reference map.
- Identify contradiction, duplicated concepts, missing prerequisites, and claims that exceed the evidence in upstream reports.
- Check the Unified Framework and Transformative Intelligence architecture against corrected source reports.

### Gate E — Reproducibility and validation
- Audit existing synthetic smoke tests and their limits.
- Execute only well-specified, bounded tests with available valid data and clear acceptance criteria.
- Never substitute toy data for empirical validation or report an unrun simulation as completed.
- Keep specialist review, numerical validation, empirical testing, and implementation as separate gates.

### Gate F — Release decision
- Publish a final report-by-report status matrix and an overall completion decision.
- New reports resume only after the corpus audit reaches a documented checkpoint and the user agrees the existing foundation is ready.
