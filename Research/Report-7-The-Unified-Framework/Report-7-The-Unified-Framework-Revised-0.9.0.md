## Purpose

This report contributes **Cross-report synthesis, the five-phase cycle, and recursive meta-wisdom governance** to the Transformative Intelligence governance architecture. The five phases now function as governance operations:

1. **Divergence — generation:** preserve relevant perspectives and record claim type, evidence, provenance, and uncertainty.
2. **Insurgence — contestation:** challenge core commitments using predeclared criteria; retain rival positions and document the outcome.
3. **Allegiance — stabilization:** adopt provisional working standards with review dates, anomaly triggers, and revision conditions.
4. **Convergence — integration:** connect compatible findings without silently erasing meaningful differences; record information lost or left unresolved.
5. **Ascendence — meta-evaluation:** evaluate the process and specify how conclusions reconnect to first-order evidence or accountable review.

## Report-specific governance rule

Govern the seven-report framework through Divergence, Insurgence, Allegiance, Convergence, and Ascendence. Meta-wisdom applies generation records, challenge protocols, commitment reviews, a loss ledger, and re-entry conditions, while remaining itself revisable. These are proposed controls, not yet validated performance claims.

## Minimum claim record

For consequential claims, record the exact claim; claim type; source and provenance; method and scope; uncertainty; counterevidence and rival explanations; decision relevance; and review or revision trigger. A source supporting one claim type does not automatically support a stronger causal, normative, or metaphysical conclusion.

## Controls

| Control | Requirement | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|
| Generation record | Each included perspective declares its evidential basis and limits | Inclusion or exclusion lacks a traceable rationale |
| Challenge protocol | Define success and potential disconfirmation before evaluation | Criteria shift after results to protect a preferred position |
| Commitment review | Record owner, scope, review date, and anomaly triggers | A commitment persists without review or is retired arbitrarily |
| Loss ledger | Document distinctions merged, omitted, or judged incomparable | Synthesis implies equivalence while concealing material differences |
| Re-entry condition | Meta-level recommendations connect to a checkable observation or review | Meta-level conclusions cannot be affected by first-order evidence |
| Dissent and appeal | Preserve minority reports and reasoned challenges | Governance suppresses disagreement instead of adjudicating it |

1. **Do not collapse different evidence classes.** Reports 1–2 concern biological/cognitive mechanisms and bounded models; Reports 3–4 include philosophical, cultural, phenomenological, and theological interpretations; Report 5 concerns an unsettled empirical/theoretical problem; Report 6 combines geophysics with metaphor; Report 7 is a synthesis architecture. Separate primary studies, systematic reviews, philosophical sources, preprints, models, and author interpretation.

3. **Treat simulations as conditional demonstrations.** A model result supports a claim only under the implemented equations, parameters, initial/boundary conditions, data-generating assumptions, and tested distributions. Reproducibility requires code or full specification, seeds where applicable, dependencies, data provenance, sensitivity analysis, and a baseline. A design document or simulated scenario is not an executed simulation.

4. **Do not infer across levels without a bridge.** Brain activity does not by itself establish metaphysical soul claims; physical coupling does not establish quantum entanglement; geomagnetic dynamics do not establish Earth consciousness; a useful analogy does not validate the mapped mechanism. Each cross-domain inference needs explicit premises and a falsifiable consequence if it is presented as empirical.

5. **Verification states must remain separate.** “Documented,” “source-checked,” “formally tested,” “simulated,” “reproduced,” and “independently reviewed” are different statuses. A report may be updated while still awaiting full source audit or external peer review.
