## 1. Purpose, scope, and governing rule

The Unified Framework is a **plural-level conceptual architecture** connecting biological organization, cognition, identity and meaning, spiritual interpretation, subjective experience, and planetary context. It maps relationships among these domains without claiming that they are one experimentally established substance or one universally accepted causal system.

**Integration means making relationships explicit; it does not mean erasing distinctions.** The framework must preserve the difference between empirical mechanisms, historical or textual descriptions, philosophical arguments, spiritual or theological commitments, metaphors, and hypotheses. Each claim is assessed according to the type of claim it actually makes.

This is a synthesis of the seven-report architecture and the evidence described in the supplied reports. It is not a new experimental result, a peer-reviewed theory, or authorization to implement the framework in software or repositories beyond the documented report. Its conclusions are limited by the sources and source excerpts available for this revision.

## 2. The architecture to preserve

### 2.1 The two layers

The framework retains the two layers exactly as supplied:

1. **Body – Mind – Soul**
2. **Soul – Spirit – Consciousness**

Soul appears in both layers and functions as an **interpretive bridge** within this design. This is a structural choice of the framework, not evidence that soul is a measurable entity, a single mechanism, or a universally defined concept. Its meanings vary across traditions and philosophical systems.

The Earth-centered context surrounds both layers. Living organisms participate in Earth's systems; “life as Earth's soul” and “the core as its heart” are attributed philosophical or spiritual interpretations. Neither metaphor should be mistaken for a demonstrated planetary essence or anatomical equivalence.

The older repository's fourfold alchemy model remains a **separate historical formulation**. It must not be silently merged into this architecture or represented as the same model without an explicit, documented mapping. [S01–S06; U01; G02]

### 2.2 Domain descriptions and evidence boundaries

| Domain | Working role in this framework | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Body** | Living, sensing, regulating organism; physiological and environmental coupling | Anatomy, physiology, and bodily processes can be empirically investigated; individual claims require domain-specific evidence. |
| **Mind** | Cognition, memory, emotion, attention, interpretation, planning, and action | Cognitive capacities can be studied through behavioral, neural, computational, and other methods; a model does not explain every aspect of mind by definition. |
| **Soul** | Interpretive concept for identity, personhood, continuity, meaning, or sacred significance | Definitions and claims vary historically and culturally; no single definition should be presented as universal or empirically settled. |
| **Spirit** | Interpretive vocabulary for vitality, inspiration, sacred relation, transcendence, or spiritual orientation | Spiritual practices and reported experiences can be studied; neural correlates do not settle the metaphysical interpretation of experience. |
| **Consciousness** | Primarily subjective experience, distinguished from access, wakefulness, attention, and report | Neural correlates and theory-specific predictions are investigable; no universally accepted complete explanation follows from the reviewed evidence. |
| **Earth context** | Biosphere, ecosystems, atmosphere, oceans, land, ice, and deep geophysical processes | Earth-system interactions and geophysical mechanisms are scientific topics; planetary soul, heart, or unified subject remain separate interpretive claims. |

These definitions are **working framework vocabulary**, not a claim that every religion, philosophical school, or scientific discipline uses these words in the same way.

## 3. Evidence synthesis across the seven reports

### 3.1 Body and mind

The first two reports describe bodily regulation, sensory systems, neural activity, cognition, memory, emotion, action, and environmental interaction. Embodied and 4E approaches emphasize that cognition can depend on bodily and environmental relationships. The strength and scope of such claims differ by task and theory; embodied accounts do not eliminate neural explanation, and not every cognitive process must be assumed to depend equally on every environmental feature. [S01–S03]

**Synthesis:** a biological–cognitive map may represent environmental input, bodily sensing, neural processing, interpretation, action, and environmental consequences as interacting processes. Each arrow must be backed by a specific claim and source rather than assumed from the diagram.

### 3.2 Soul and spirit

The third and fourth reports document different historical, philosophical, religious, and theological uses of “soul” and “spirit.” Those terms do not have one universal translation. Some traditions posit a persisting self or soul; others analyze personal identity without an enduring independent self. Buddhist philosophical accounts, for example, include detailed analyses of consciousness and cognition while challenging the notion of a permanent, substantial self. [S04–S06]

Neurotheology research can study neural and physiological correlates of prayer, meditation, ritual, and reported spiritual experiences. A 2026 systematic review synthesized 105 studies across several traditions and practice types; its breadth is useful, but heterogeneous practices and methods limit any one universal inference. Such evidence can support claims about measured brain–practice relationships without proving or disproving the external reality of a theological interpretation. [S07]

**Synthesis:** the framework can preserve spiritual language as meaningful and attributable while separating (a) the experience, (b) the person's or tradition's interpretation, (c) empirical findings about relevant processes, and (d) claims about ultimate reality.

### 3.3 Consciousness

The fifth report distinguishes phenomenal consciousness from access, wakefulness, attention, self-awareness, and reporting. The Cogitate Consortium's preregistered 2025 comparison of GNWT and IIT involved 256 participants across three neuroimaging modalities. It reported findings consistent with some predictions and substantial challenges to key tenets of both theories. The primary paper also acknowledges that its empirical tests focused on proposed biological implementations and that the theories' computational cores are difficult to test directly. [S08]

The supplied 2026 critique argues that some tested predictions may not fully capture the theories' core commitments. That is a methodological argument to evaluate against the preregistration, methods, and theory statements; it is not itself a consensus verdict. A mixed result should neither be described as decisive proof nor dismissed automatically. The correct question is: **which precise prediction was tested, what result occurred, what auxiliary assumptions connected the theory to the measurement, and what conclusion is licensed by that test?**

No reviewed evidence establishes that a functional AI architecture, memory, self-description, information routing, or an integration score by itself demonstrates subjective experience. A simulation of a function and evidence of phenomenal consciousness are different claims. [S08–S10]

### 3.4 Earth, life, and the core

The sixth report connects established Earth-system interactions with the interpretive metaphors “life as Earth's soul” and “the core as its heart.” The Gaia hypothesis and Earth-system science have related histories but should not be treated as interchangeable. A 2026 historical and conceptual analysis explicitly argues that they address distinct questions and practices. [S11]

Geodynamo simulations and research on core–mantle coupling strengthen the scientific account of Earth's deep dynamics. These results concern specific physical models, observations, and uncertainties; they do not demonstrate a conscious planetary subject or turn the core into a literal biological heart. The inner-core differential-rotation interpretation remains disputed in the supplied source set. [S12–S14]

**Synthesis:** life and environmental systems influence one another; deep-interior dynamics contribute to particular geophysical processes; people interpret their place in the living world. The links do not entail “core = heart = soul = consciousness.”

## 4. Claim-type vocabulary and status schema

The following labels are **local documentation proposals**, not an approved replacement for GAIA's existing evidence vocabulary. Their purpose is to prevent unlike claims from being compressed into one confidence score.

- **EMPIRICAL:** a claim about observable processes, measurements, or findings.
- **HISTORICAL/TEXTUAL:** a claim about what a text, thinker, institution, or community said or practiced.
- **PHILOSOPHICAL:** a definition, argument, interpretation, or normative proposal assessed by reasoning.
- **SPIRITUAL/THEOLOGICAL:** a tradition-specific or personal claim concerning sacred or ultimate reality, recorded with attribution.
- **METAPHORICAL:** a correspondence intended to illuminate meaning, not an asserted identity of mechanisms.
- **HYPOTHETICAL:** a proposed claim whose evidence, definitions, or discriminating tests remain incomplete.

A claim may involve more than one type. Record the type and evidence status separately.

### 4.1 Evidence-status values

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ESTABLISHED_WITHIN_SCOPE** | Multiple appropriate lines of evidence support the claim within explicitly stated conditions. This does not mean universal certainty. |
| **SUPPORTED_BUT_SCOPED** | Evidence supports a narrower claim, method, population, model, or dataset than a broad summary might imply. |
| **PRELIMINARY** | Early, limited, preprint, or otherwise insufficiently replicated evidence. |
| **DISPUTED** | Credible sources or models disagree on the specific claim. |
| **UNRESOLVED** | Evidence does not currently settle the question. |
| **NOT_ASSESSED** | The necessary evidence has not been reviewed for this report. |
| **NOT_APPLICABLE_AS_STATED** | The proposition is interpretive or normative as written and is not directly an empirical hypothesis; a more precise empirical subclaim may still be testable. |
| **UNVERIFIED_SOURCE** | The cited source identity or the claim attributed to it could not be reliably confirmed. |

These values must not be treated as one linear ladder. For example, a spiritual claim can be faithfully documented as a tradition's teaching without being assigned an empirical truth status; an empirical claim can be disputed; and a metaphor can be useful without being a mechanism.

## 5. Relationships and causal discipline

Every cross-domain relation should contain:

- **Source concept** and **target concept**
- **Relation type** and, where relevant, direction
- **Exact claim** in a single interpretable sentence
- **Claim type** and **evidence status**
- **Source(s)** with stable identifiers and relevant passages
- **Scope**, including system boundary, conditions, and timescale where applicable
- **Alternative explanation(s)**
- **Uncertainty and limitations**
- **Review status** and reviewer competence, where applicable

Candidate relation types:

- `PART_OF` — membership or composition
- `INTERACTS_WITH` — interaction without presuming a single causal direction
- `CAUSES` — causal claim requiring appropriate evidence
- `SUPPORTS_CAPACITY` — contributes to a capacity without claiming sufficiency
- `CORRELATES_WITH` — measured association, not automatically causal
- `INTERPRETED_AS` — meaning assigned by a person, tradition, or framework
- `ANALOGOUS_TO` — similarity in a stated respect, not identity
- `CONFLICTS_WITH` — incompatibility under specified definitions or assumptions
- `CONDITIONS` — a factor changes the circumstances under which another process occurs
- `UNKNOWN_RELATION` — a relation is proposed but not adequately characterized

### 5.1 Illustrative relation records

**Example A — Body and cognition**
- Claim: bodily signals can contribute to perception, emotion, and action.
- Relation: `SUPPORTS_CAPACITY` / `INTERACTS_WITH`
- Type: EMPIRICAL
- Status: SUPPORTED_BUT_SCOPED
- Scope: specific physiological and cognitive processes; cite evidence appropriate to each claimed pathway.

**Example B — Core and magnetic field**
- Claim: electrically conducting fluid motion in Earth's outer core contributes to generation and maintenance of Earth's intrinsic magnetic field.
- Relation: `CAUSES` / `SUPPORTS_CAPACITY`
- Type: EMPIRICAL
- Status: ESTABLISHED_WITHIN_SCOPE, with model-specific uncertainties
- Scope: geodynamo mechanisms; do not infer consciousness or intentionality from this mechanism.

**Example C — Core as heart**
- Claim: “The core is Earth's heart” expresses the core's perceived centrality in the framework.
- Relation: `ANALOGOUS_TO`
- Type: METAPHORICAL
- Status: NOT_APPLICABLE_AS_STATED
- Scope: an interpretive analogy, not a biological or geophysical identity.

**Example D — Earth as one conscious subject**
- Claim: Earth has one unified subjective point of view.
- Relation: proposed, but not established by ecological interdependence or geophysical coupling alone.
- Type: HYPOTHETICAL or SPIRITUAL/THEOLOGICAL, depending on the claim's intended meaning.
- Status: UNRESOLVED / NOT_ASSESSED as a distinct empirical hypothesis.
- Requirement: define the proposed subject and specify evidence that would distinguish the claim from metaphor or distributed Earth-system interaction.

**Example E — AI consciousness**
- Claim: a system's ability to route information, use memory, or describe itself establishes subjective experience.
- Type: EMPIRICAL/HYPOTHETICAL, depending on formulation.
- Status: NOT_ESTABLISHED by those functional abilities alone.
- Requirement: state a theory, operational predictions, alternatives, and the scope of any inference. Do not present a function benchmark as a direct detector of experience.

## 6. Feedback loops: evidence and status

1. **Body–mind loop:** bodily signals contribute to perception and cognition; emotion and action can affect physiological states. The direction and mechanism of each link must be specified. **Status:** empirical in particular pathways; not one universal loop.
2. **Mind–meaning–action loop:** interpretations of identity and values may shape choices and practices, which can in turn alter later interpretations. **Status:** plausible research relationship and framework proposal; causal effects are not quantified here.
3. **Experience–spiritual-interpretation loop:** experiences are described and interpreted through personal, cultural, and religious concepts; practices may also shape attention and experience. **Status:** mixed empirical, historical, and interpretive claims; a neural correlate does not settle metaphysics.
4. **Life–environment loop:** organisms and physical environments influence one another. **Status:** empirical, but any claim of stabilization or planetary regulation must specify variable, mechanism, boundary, and timescale.
5. **Core–field relationship:** outer-core fluid dynamics support the geodynamo; deep-layer coupling may influence rotation. **Status:** empirically and model supported within defined geophysical scope; not a demonstrated channel from Earth's core to human meaning or consciousness.

A diagram may represent these relations, but visual proximity or a connecting arrow must never be treated as evidence by itself.

## 7. Uncertainty, disagreement, and inference controls

No reviewed source establishes one causal equation linking body, mind, soul, spirit, consciousness, and Earth. These domains differ in meaning, method, and explanatory scale. Risks include treating meaning as a physical quantity, equating complexity with subjectivity, projecting human anatomy onto a planet, treating a tradition's statement as a universal doctrine, or confusing a model of experience with experience itself.

The framework must avoid both **false positives** and **false negatives**:

- Do not infer a literal mechanism from a metaphor.
- Do not infer a single planetary subject from planetary interdependence.
- Do not infer a complete explanation from a neural correlate.
- Do not infer AI subjective experience from functional similarity alone.
- Do not infer a decisive verdict between theories from mixed adversarial-collaboration results.
- Do not infer that every philosophical or spiritual claim is false simply because it is not directly operationalized as an empirical hypothesis.
- Do not treat unresolved disagreement as proof that all positions are equally supported.
- Do not protect a theory from any possible counterevidence by allowing unrestricted post-hoc changes to its core commitments or auxiliary assumptions.
- Do not treat one failed implementation as automatically refuting every version of a broad theory.

For each empirical claim, identify what evidence would strengthen it, weaken it, or leave it unchanged. For interpretive claims, record attribution and reasoning rather than inventing a laboratory pass/fail test that does not fit the claim.

### C. Simulation 1 — Geodynamo / length-of-day coupling toy model

**Purpose.** Test whether an explicit minimal angular-momentum exchange model can reproduce pre-specified qualitative and quantitative features of multidecadal ΔLOD under plausible parameter constraints.

**Starting balance (schematic):**

\[
\frac{dL_m}{dt}=\Gamma_{grav}-\Gamma_{em}-\Gamma_{topo}
\]

This balance is not executable until torque laws and state definitions are supplied. Required model specification:

1. Define mantle/core angular momenta, moments of inertia, relative rotation, and the exact conversion from Δ angular momentum to ΔLOD.
2. Define each torque's functional form and units; state how conductance, CMB topography, inner-core mass anomaly, and relative rotation enter.
3. Declare initial conditions, forcing, boundary conditions, solver, time step, simulation duration, parameter distributions, and calibration dataset.
4. Separate externally constrained parameters from fitted parameters; report uncertainty and sensitivity.
5. Compare predicted spectra, amplitudes, and phase relationships against an identified observational series using a baseline model and out-of-sample checks.

The suggested 10–30-year spectral peak, several-millisecond amplitude, and parameter sweep are **hypotheses to test**, not results. Failure criteria include unstable/no oscillatory solution over justified parameter ranges, requiring unsupported conductance/topography, or failing out-of-sample observations. A toy model cannot validate a metaphor or independently settle the inner-core rotation dispute.

### D. Simulation 2 — Consciousness prediction ledger and adversarial scoring

**Purpose.** Make theory comparisons auditable before data are inspected.

Each preregistered row must contain: theory; claim role (content, access, report, confidence, control use, state capacity); exact prediction; operational measure; critical control; falsifier; core-vs-import flag; rival predictions; sample/data scope; scoring rule; missingness policy; and evidence status.

**Proposed scoring gate:** award theory-discriminating credit only when (a) the prediction and falsifier were specified in advance, (b) the prediction is core or the relation of the implementation to the core is explicit, (c) at least one serious rival makes a different prediction, (d) the relevant measure and controls were actually collected, and (e) the preregistered scoring rule is followed. Report support by prediction domain; do not collapse all domains into one “winner.”

**Application to Cogitate:** this is a proposed re-analysis rubric, not a completed re-score. To classify Cogitate's core-level outcome, first map each actual preregistered prediction to theory statements and implementation assumptions, then have independent reviewers agree on the map before scoring. The assertion that both theories are “inconclusive at core level” should be reported as a rubric-dependent assessment, not as a raw experimental result.

**Falsifiers:** a theory's pre-specified, discriminating core prediction fails under the conditions it stipulated; the measure fails validity checks; or results do not distinguish the theory from rivals. Distinguish theory failure from failure of an auxiliary implementation or measurement instrument.

### E. Simulation 3 — Embodied–interoceptive active-inference model

**Purpose.** Test whether a minimal model with cardiac and respiratory channels can generate specified changes in perceptual confidence and affective bias under interoceptive manipulations.

**Proposed model elements:** latent arousal; cardiac and respiratory phase; external stimulus intensity; noisy interoceptive and exteroceptive observations; precision-weighted prediction errors; belief updating; and a stated decision/report rule. Allen et al. (2022) provides a formal interoceptive-inference precedent; Monti and Aglioti (2026) provides a review-level empirical motivation. [S41; S49]

**Pre-registered manipulations:** (1) increase cardiac-channel precision; (2) set interoceptive precision to zero as an abstract lesion; (3) vary respiratory-phase coupling; (4) compare against a no-interoceptive-effect baseline. Predictions such as reduced exteroceptive confidence, altered affective bias, or phase-dependent decision shifts must be tied to explicit equations and parameter values before execution.

**Required before running:** equations for generative and observation models, priors, likelihoods, precision updates, parameter bounds, initial conditions, number of simulated trials, random seeds, baseline, sensitivity analysis, and quantitative effect metrics. Physiological plausibility must be justified from primary studies; “zero precision” is a computational ablation, not a literal biological lesion.

**Failure criteria:** no reproducible phase-dependent modulation; no predicted change after the specified ablation; or effects appear only under implausible parameter values. A positive result would show that the model's assumptions can generate the target pattern, not that interoception completely explains affect, consciousness, or spiritual experience.

### 3. IELF-class embodied-learning model: audit

The proposed state variables—exteroceptive observations (s_t), interoceptive observations (i_t), latent bodily state (x_t), and action (a_t)—are a useful starting vocabulary, not yet a complete executable model.

1. Define each state, unit, dimension, observation likelihood, transition likelihood, and initial distribution.

3. Replace the scalar sigmoid in the supplied equation with a normalized multi-channel arbitration function, or explicitly define independent gates rather than calling the output a probability vector.

### 6. Soul and spirit: compare claims without category errors
