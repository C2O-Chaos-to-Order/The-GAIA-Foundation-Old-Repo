## 1. Definition and scope

This report preserves two connected propositions as **framework metaphors**: **life as the soul of the Earth** and **Earth's core as its heart**. Each has a scientific referent and an interpretive layer. The scientific referents are the biosphere and Earth's deep interior; the words *soul* and *heart* express philosophical, ethical, or spiritual meaning unless separate, testable claims are explicitly defined.

The **biosphere** comprises Earth's living organisms and the regions and processes in which life exists. An **ecosystem** comprises living communities interacting with nonliving environmental conditions. The **Earth system** is broader still: atmosphere, oceans, land, ice, life, and deep geophysical processes interact across different spatial and temporal scales. The biosphere is not the entire Earth system, and neither is interchangeable with the scientific or philosophical concept of Gaia. [S01–S04]

For this framework, “life as Earth's soul” expresses the view that life gives the planet its living diversity and much of its experienced significance and ethical importance to humans. “The core as Earth's heart” expresses centrality, hidden dynamics, and influence on planetary processes. Neither metaphor is presented as a literal biological classification or evidence that Earth has a unified mind, intention, or subjective experience.

## 2. Evidence: life and the Earth system

### 2.1 Well-supported interactions

Life and planetary environments influence one another. Organisms alter and depend on chemical cycles, soils, atmospheric composition, ocean processes, and ecological relationships; environmental conditions constrain where and how life persists. Earth-system science studies these coupled processes and their evolution. NASA's Earth-science programs examine the interactions among atmosphere, land, oceans, ice, and life, while Gaia-related scholarship traces how organisms' collective effects can influence aspects of planetary habitability. [S01–S04]

Three different claims must remain distinct:

1. **Biotic–abiotic interaction:** life both depends on and modifies planetary environments. This is well established.
2. **Specified feedback or regulation:** a defined process can stabilize, amplify, or otherwise alter a named variable over a stated range of conditions and timescale. This must be evaluated mechanism by mechanism.
3. **Planetary agency or intention:** Earth acts as one intentional organism seeking an optimal state. This does not follow from the first two claims and is not established by the sources reviewed here.

A feedback is not automatically stabilizing, beneficial to every organism, or permanent. Stabilization of one variable may coexist with rapid change or harm elsewhere. “Regulation” should name the variables, mechanisms, boundaries, and timescales involved rather than imply a planet-wide purpose.

### 2.2 2026 clarification: Gaia and Earth-system science

Timothy M. Lenton and Sébastien Dutreuil's “Disentangling Gaia and the Earth System,” published in *Tellus* in July 2026, argues that Gaia and the Earth system are distinct concepts that have generated different scientific questions and different relationships between science and politics. The article traces Gaia from the foundational Lovelock–Margulis account and considers how Gaia's legacy influenced Earth-system science without making the terms identical. [S05]

This is a conceptual and historical analysis, not evidence that the planet is literally conscious. It supports a methodological distinction important to this report: **Earth-system science describes coupled Earth processes; Gaia theory is a related but distinct scientific and conceptual tradition; “Earth's soul” is this framework's interpretive metaphor.** Those three layers can inform one another without being treated as synonyms.

A separate 2026 article, “Systemic synthetic modelling: on a Gaian reformulation,” proposes a Gaian reformulation of synthetic modelling around distributed regulation and viability in coupled systems. It is relevant as a conceptual and methodological proposal, not as proof of a single Earth-wide organism or mind. [S06] The article appeared in *Cognitive Systems Research*, volume 98, September 2026; its publication details should be used rather than the earlier draft's broad “May 2026” label.

### 2.3 Ethical meaning and evidence boundaries

Calling life Earth's soul may serve as an ethical orientation: living systems deserve careful attention because human life depends on them and because living beings and ecosystems have moral, cultural, ecological, and intrinsic value under several ethical approaches. This is a philosophical and normative position supported by ethical argument, not a measurement of a literal planetary essence.

The metaphor should not imply that every organism or ecosystem exists to serve a single planetary goal, that nature is always harmonious, or that harm to particular beings is justified by an assumed “greater planetary good.” Ecological interdependence includes competition, disturbance, extinction, and uneven distributions of risk and benefit. Scientific evidence informs environmental ethics but does not alone determine every moral conclusion.

## 3. Evidence: the core and Earth's geodynamics

### 3.1 Structure and the geodynamo

Earth's deep interior is commonly described in terms of crust, mantle, liquid outer core, and solid inner core. The core is not one uniform object. Its physical behavior is linked to heat transfer, composition, fluid motion, rotation, electrical conductivity, magnetic-field generation, and coupling with surrounding layers. NASA's geodynamo research describes Earth's intrinsic magnetic field as originating primarily in electrically conducting fluid motion in the outer core, with convection driven in part by secular cooling and differentiation. [S07, S08]

The **geodynamo** is the physical process by which electrically conducting fluid motion in the outer core generates and maintains Earth's intrinsic magnetic field. It is not accurately described as the solid inner core simply pumping the field like a biological heart. The core's influence is real and physically specific; the “heart” label remains metaphorical.

### 3.2 2026 geodynamo simulation

Julien Aubert's “Geodynamo simulations spanning millennia in the physical conditions of Earth's core” was submitted on 23 March 2026, accepted on 4 May, and published in the *Journal of Studies of Earth's Deep Interior* in May 2026 (DOI: 10.46298/jsedi.17790). It reports a numerical simulation that matches key physical conditions including core density, rotation rate, convective power, and electrical conductivity, while keeping viscous losses minor. The model was integrated over nearly 1,700 years of physical time and compared with geomagnetic and geodetic observations. [S09]

The paper reports a quasi-geostrophic magneto-Archimedes–Coriolis force balance and argues that the model reproduces important observed characteristics without relying on the extrapolation used in earlier simulations. This is a substantive advance in geodynamo modelling and provides a stronger empirical anchor than a general overview alone. It remains a **numerical model**, however: its force balance, assumptions, parameter choices, resolution, and comparison metrics require specialist scrutiny, and a successful simulation is not the same as direct experimental access to the core. The paper does not establish a biological or experiential meaning for the core.

### 3.3 2026 inner-core torque and length-of-day variation

Huifeng Zhang and Mathieu Dumberry's “Gravitational torque drives multidecadal variations in length of day” appeared in *Nature* on 23 September 2026 (DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-10999-2). The authors analyze multidecadal variations in Earth's length of day and argue that gravitational torque associated with the reconstructed inner-core dynamics is the primary driver in their model, resisted by electromagnetic and topographic torques at the core–mantle boundary. Their interpretation also implies particular properties for the lowermost mantle and inner-core deformation. [S10]

The finding strengthens the case that deep-interior processes and coupling among Earth's layers can influence planetary rotation on decadal timescales. The conclusion is nevertheless conditional on the accuracy of reconstructed inner-core rotation and core-flow models; the authors themselves identify these dependencies. It is evidence about geophysical coupling, not evidence that the core is a heart in a biological sense or that it has feelings or intentions.

### 3.4 Inner-core differential rotation remains disputed

Xin Zhang and Lianxing Wen's “Episodic Change, Quiescence, and Recovery of Earth's Inner Core Surface,” published in *Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth* in September 2026 (DOI: 10.1029/2026JB034595), argues that observed seismic changes can be explained by episodic changes in the inner-core surface without requiring differential rotation. The authors map surface changes using repeating-earthquake observations from 2003–2024 and present candidate physical mechanisms, while identifying limitations and unresolved issues. [S11]

This is a research contribution and interpretation, not a final resolution of every question about the inner core. It directly cautions against treating differential rotation as settled or as the only explanation for temporal seismic changes. Zhang and Dumberry's torque analysis and Zhang and Wen's surface-change interpretation should be represented as distinct models and evaluated against their data, assumptions, and predictions rather than compressed into a single uncontested account.

**Synthesis:** the strongest grounded claim is that the core and surrounding deep Earth participate in complex, coupled physical dynamics, including magnetic-field generation and exchanges of angular momentum. The precise contributions of competing mechanisms remain an active research area. None of these studies tests the “heart” metaphor as if it were an anatomical hypothesis.

## 4. Perspectives: what the metaphors contribute—and where they stop

### 4.1 “Life as the soul of the Earth”

The metaphor can express life's diversity, ecological embeddedness, and the ethical significance of protecting the conditions that sustain living systems. Its interpretive force may be spiritual or philosophical. It should not be used as a substitute for biosphere measurements, ecology, biogeochemistry, or Earth-system models.

The reviewed literature does not establish a single planet-wide subject, a detachable planetary essence, or an Earth that makes conscious decisions. Whether “Earth's soul” is meaningful within a religious or philosophical tradition is a separate question from whether a literal planetary mind has been empirically demonstrated.

### 4.2 “The core as the heart of the Earth”

The metaphor can convey centrality, hidden influence, and dynamic coupling. Its limit is that the core is not a cardiac organ: it has no known biological circulation or established feelings. Earth's habitability and dynamics cannot be reduced to one privileged component. Surface climate, the biosphere, oceans, atmosphere, mantle, core, solar input, and many other processes operate at different scales.

A disciplined formulation is: **the core is a central geophysical subsystem whose fluid motion helps generate Earth's magnetic field and whose coupling with other deep layers can affect rotation; “heart” is the framework's metaphor for this significance.**

## 5. Relationships across the foundational framework

- **Body and life:** Human bodies are living systems embedded in ecosystems; physiological processes depend on environmental conditions and interact with them.
- **Mind and consciousness:** Human minds and experiences occur in embodied organisms. This does not imply that Earth itself has a unified mind.
- **Soul and spirit:** These terms can express identity, meaning, belonging, sacred relation, or transcendence in particular philosophical and religious traditions. Their meanings vary and should not be collapsed into a single universal definition.
- **Earth system and biosphere:** Living processes both depend on and modify environmental conditions. This is a scientific relationship that can be studied without assuming planetary intention.
- **Core and Earth dynamics:** Outer-core fluid motion generates the intrinsic geomagnetic field; coupling among the core, mantle, and other components contributes to changes in Earth's rotation and deep-interior behavior.
- **Transformative Intelligence:** The project's cycle—Divergence, Insurgence, Allegiance, Convergence, Ascendence—can be applied as a normative framework for expanding perspectives, challenging assumptions, committing to justified principles, integrating knowledge, and learning. It is not a geophysical mechanism or a scientifically established sequence in Earth's behavior.

The report's proposed conceptual chain is: **deep-interior dynamics influence selected planetary processes; Earth-system conditions interact with living systems; life depends on and modifies parts of those systems; humans interpret their place in this living world.** This chain does not entail “core = heart = soul = consciousness.”

## 6. Uncertainty and limits

Several uncertainties must remain explicit:

1. **Feedback is process-specific.** A stabilizing feedback for one variable or timescale can coexist with instability or harm elsewhere.
2. **Earth-system and Gaia frameworks differ.** The 2026 historical analysis argues for distinguishing them; neither should be equated with the framework's spiritual metaphor.
3. **Numerical geodynamo results depend on models.** Their evidential strength depends on parameter provenance, equations, resolution, numerical convergence, and comparison against observations.
4. **Core–mantle coupling is an active research topic.** The 2026 torque paper depends on reconstructed rotation and flow; alternative accounts of seismic observations challenge the necessity of inner-core differential rotation.
5. **Metaphor does not establish ontology.** Neither the ecological evidence nor the geophysical sources reviewed here establish a literal planetary soul, a conscious core, or a single Earth-wide experiencer.
6. **Ethical conclusions require explicit values.** Earth-system science can inform choices but does not, by itself, settle how to balance biodiversity, human well-being, justice, and competing interests.

This report neither proves nor disproves every form of planetary spirituality. It states the narrower evidence boundary: the reviewed ecological and geophysical evidence supports an interconnected living Earth and dynamic deep interior, but does not establish planetary consciousness or a literal heart-like core.

## 9. Conclusions

The scientific foundation is an interconnected Earth system containing a living biosphere and a dynamic deep interior. The 2026 geodynamo simulation strengthens numerical investigation of outer-core fluid dynamics; the 2026 length-of-day study proposes a specific role for gravitational torque in core–mantle coupling; and the 2026 inner-core surface study challenges the necessity of differential rotation to explain certain seismic observations. These findings strengthen the account of Earth's physical dynamics while leaving meaningful model-dependent uncertainties.

The philosophical foundation is the framework's interpretation of life as Earth's soul and the core as its heart. These metaphors can be preserved without presenting them as established scientific entities. The most rigorous next step is a geophysics-and-ecology review that evaluates specific mechanisms, datasets, and competing models while maintaining a clear boundary between empirical description, philosophical meaning, and spiritual interpretation. ## Research Pass 1 — Earth-System and Geophysical Update (2026)

### Geodynamo simulation: strong mechanism, scoped validation

Aubert's 2026 geodynamo simulation was integrated for nearly 1,700 years of physical time while matching core density, rotation rate, convective power, and electrical conductivity to Earth's conditions and maintaining small viscous losses in the force and power budgets. The paper reports successful comparison of many model outputs with geomagnetic and geodetic observations. This is substantial quantitative support for the convective geodynamo paradigm under the model's conditions—not validation of the “core as heart” metaphor and not proof that every parameter or submechanism is uniquely determined. The manuscript reports constraints including convective power and lower-mantle conductance; exact values should be quoted with the paper's assumptions and uncertainty rather than treated as universal constants. [S45]

### Core–mantle angular-momentum exchange remains model-dependent

Zhang and Dumberry's September 2026 *Nature* article argues that gravitational torque associated with the inner core is the primary driver of multidecadal length-of-day variations in their reconstruction, resisted by electromagnetic and topographic torques. These are inference results from physical modeling and reconstructed core dynamics; they do not constitute a direct measurement of every torque term independently. [S46]

Zhang and Wen's September 2026 *Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth* paper argues that localized, episodic inner-core surface changes can explain a set of seismic observations without differential rotation. This is a published competing interpretation and keeps the inner-core differential-rotation question open. Report the tension directly: the geodynamo mechanism is strongly supported, while specific torque partitions and inner-core kinematic interpretations remain contested. [S53]

### Gaia and Earth-system science

Lenton and Dutreuil's 2026 *Tellus* analysis argues that Gaia and Earth-system science are distinct frameworks with different questions and practices. This supports the report's distinction between measured Earth-system processes and the attributed interpretation “life as Earth's soul.” The metaphor may organize ethical or spiritual meaning; it must not be used as evidence for a planetary mind, intention, or literal biological heart. [S47]

### Simulation 1 — Geodynamo/length-of-day toy model (design only)

The proposed angular-momentum balance is a useful starting point, but it is not yet numerically executable as specified. Define the torque laws and their dimensions, the state variables and inertia terms, the relationship between angular-momentum change and ΔLOD, initial/boundary conditions, forcing, numerical solver, and data used for calibration. Distinguish fitted parameters from independently constrained ones. The proposed 10–30-year spectral peak and several-millisecond amplitude are **hypotheses**, not simulated findings. A toy model can test internal consequences of its assumptions; it cannot independently validate the geodynamo or decide inner-core rotation without appropriate calibration and comparison to observations.

### 1. Planetary active inference: a proof of concept, not planetary consciousness

Rubin, Heins, Mitsui, Da Costa, and Friston (2026) present a simple biosphere–climate model in which internal biosphere states and boundary variables behave as if they infer changes in incoming solar radiation, while greenhouse forcing and albedo are adjusted to maintain climate trajectories within modeled geophysiological bounds. The paper frames this as a proof of concept for a minimal form of planetary agency. (S64)

The claim should remain tightly scoped: **a particular biosphere–climate model can be described using active-inference mathematics and can show regulation-like behavior under specified conditions.** This does not establish that the actual Earth is a unified agent, has a subjective point of view, intends to regulate climate, or possesses a metaphysical soul. “As-if agency” is a dynamical interpretation of the model, not a direct observation of planetary experience.

### 2. Faithful reproduction before extension

The supplied variational-free-energy equation and gradient updates are schematic. Before execution, recover the source paper's exact generative model, state definitions, parameter values, constraints, and update equations. Specify how the likelihood depends on greenhouse forcing and albedo; define the variational family and parameterization; state the gradient method; enforce physical bounds and units; and distinguish the source paper's modeled solar-evolution range from a generic sweep of 0.9–1.1 times current solar input.

The proposed coupling range 0–1 and 1–10 internal degrees of freedom are exploratory design choices unless mapped to the source paper's dimensionless parameters. First reproduce the published model and figures; only then test extensions. The expected signatures in the supplied specification are hypotheses to test, not results already obtained here.

### 3. Whole-cell modeling is a distinct biological constraint

Thornburg et al. (2026) report a 4D spatial–kinetic model for the full cell cycle of JCVI-syn3A, integrating genetic information processes, metabolism, growth, and division, and comparing outputs with measurements including origin-to-terminus ratio, doubling time, mRNA half-lives, protein distributions, and ribosome counts. (S63)

This is an example of data-constrained multi-process modeling, not a model of Earth, a human body, a mind, or consciousness. The proposed lattice spacing, timestep, and parameter sweeps in the supplied protocol must not be attributed to the published paper unless checked against its methods, supplementary material, and code. A simplified model must be labelled an adaptation, not a reproduction of the published model. The published correction should be tracked.

### 4. Interpretation

Earth-system science can investigate feedback, habitability, biosphere–climate coupling, and planetary regulation. “Life as Earth's soul” remains a philosophical/spiritual interpretation. It can be related to active-inference dynamics by explicit analogy, but the model cannot supply evidence for a planetary soul or phenomenal consciousness.

## Version 0.6.0 — Geodynamo Validation, CMG Mode, and Climate-System Boundaries
**Revision date:** 2026-10-23. **Status:** research addendum; no new simulation executed.

### 1. Millennium-scale geodynamo
Aubert's 2026 simulation spans approximately 1,700 years of physical time while matching major core conditions and reports agreement between modeled velocity, convective density anomaly, and magnetic-field behavior and geomagnetic/geodetic observations over past centuries. It supports the convective geodynamo paradigm under the model's assumptions, including a QG-MAC force balance, near-3-TW convective power/Ohmic dissipation, and lower-mantle conductance on the order of 10^9 S to explain core-originated interannual-to-decadal length-of-day variation through electromagnetic coupling. The paper and its replication dataset should be used as the basis for a reproduction attempt. (S79–S80)

### 2. Core–mantle gravitational mode
Dumberry's 2026 paper proposes a core–mantle gravitational (CMG) oscillation involving angular-momentum exchange among the mantle, fluid core, and solid inner core, with a predicted 40–100-year period and potentially low quality factor under short inner-core viscous-relaxation times. This is a theoretical mode prediction, not an observed spectral peak already established as CMG. A numerical model must conserve total angular momentum across torque terms and define sign conventions consistently. The supplied schematic equations should be derived from the paper's equations before implementation. (S81)

The proposed parameter ranges for viscosity and torques are exploratory until source-checked and dimensionally justified. A 40–100-year band overlaps other multidecadal processes; spectral coincidence alone cannot identify a mechanism. Compare electromagnetic-only, gravitational-only, and coupled models, and use sensitivity analysis plus held-out observations.

### 3. Mantle heterogeneity
Biggin et al. (2026) analyze how mantle heterogeneity influenced Earth's ancient magnetic field, adding a spatially heterogeneous core–mantle heat-flux boundary condition to the long-term geodynamo picture. This concerns ancient field behavior and does not by itself prove the proposed modern CMG mode. (S82)

### 4. Planetary active inference and Markov blankets
Rubin et al. (2026) present a simple biosphere–climate proof of concept for regulation described using active inference; a related 2020 *Journal of the Royal Society Interface* paper formalizes a Gaia/climate-system proposal using Markov blankets and variational free energy. The blanket is a statistical organization of internal, external, sensory, and active states—not a subject or metaphysical boundary. (S83–S84)

## Research improvements

Aubert's 2026 geodynamo simulation runs for nearly 1,700 years of physical model time under core-like conditions. It reports QG-MAC force balance, agreement with geomagnetic/geodetic observations, magneto-Coriolis contributions near 60-year periods, convective power/Ohmic dissipation near 3 TW, and a lower-mantle conductance requirement around 10^9 S for modeled electromagnetic coupling. This strengthens a physical geodynamo account, not a biological-heart analogy. Dumberry's proposed 40–100-year core-mantle gravitational mode remains a theoretical mode requiring damping and detectability analysis, not an established observed periodicity. Simulation equations must conserve angular momentum, have consistent units and boundary conditions, and compare against electromagnetic-only baselines while accounting for spectral uncertainty. Rubin et al.'s planetary active-inference model supports an as-if agency interpretation under its assumptions, not phenomenal agency or a planetary soul. Markov blankets express conditional-dependence structure; they do not prove a subject or unique natural boundary. “Soul of Earth” and “core as heart” remain interpretive metaphors, distinguished from geophysical claims.
