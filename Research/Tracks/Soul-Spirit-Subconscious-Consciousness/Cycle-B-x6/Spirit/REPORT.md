# Spirit — Track Cycle B (x6)

Track: Soul, Spirit, Subconscious and Consciousness (`../../README.md`). Scope and boundary: `../../FRAMEWORK.md` § Spirit. Labels: **E** Established · **P** Plausible · **PS** Philosophical/Spiritual · **H** Hypothetical. GAIA is the motherthread (PS framing) and is kept separate from the science.

## Reading of this cycle
Spirituality is robustly associated with less harmful drug use (K19), but an E-value of 1.56 keeps this short of causal evidence. Psychedelic reductions in death anxiety are moderate-to-large in a small, heterogeneous literature (K20), and its mystical-experience moderator is fragile with 8 studies (TB-SPIRIT-12). A Spirit x Justice bridge comes from community accounts of radical healing (K21, qualitative).

Changes against Cycle A: Cycle A claims are carried with IDs TB-* and a change note (`change_vs_prev_track_cycle`). TB-SUBC-04 is revised and the boundary claims are extended.

## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **TB-SPIRIT-01** [E] In English validation data (280 measurements, 145 healthy participants, four placebo-controlled psilocybin studies), the four-factor Mystical Experience Questionnaire (MEQ30) and the six-factor MEQ40 showed acceptable to good confirmatory fit, and six of the eight Psychedelic Experience Scale subscales showed high internal consistency. *Sources:* K04. *Limits:* Drug-context sample; psychometric fit says the questionnaire measures reports consistently, not what the experiences are of. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-06;R4:C0v2-R4-11.
- **TB-SPIRIT-02** [E] Graziosi et al. (2026) did not reproduce reported effects of experimentally induced awe on agency detection, prosocial behaviour or well-being across online, laboratory and virtual-reality inductions, including three online direct replications with 2.5 times the original sample sizes. *Sources:* K05. *Limits:* Mostly online (MTurk) samples; induction strength can differ from originals. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-08.
- **TB-SPIRIT-03** [E] In SIM-SPIRIT-A (illustrative originals of 50 per group, published only if p < .05), published effects averaged d = 0.48 when the true effect was zero; a replication at 2.5x n has power .35 for a true d = 0.2, so three failed replications still have probability .27 (pre-registered H1 of < .10: not supported). A run of failures like K05's argues against medium effects (d = 0.3: .04) more than against small ones. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-A/iter-1#H2. *Limits:* Illustrative n0; equal-variance normal model. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-08.
- **TB-SPIRIT-05** [E] In SIM-SPIRIT-A, a binary 'all four subscales above a cut' classification was unstable even with subscale reliability .90: only 72% of people classified at test were classified again at retest (kappa .67; pre-registered H1 of at least 80%: not supported). Binary 'complete experience' counts carry more measurement noise than the subscale reliabilities suggest. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-A/iter-2#H1. *Limits:* Illustrative cut (60th percentile on each subscale), not the published criterion, which was not read. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-11.
- **TB-SPIRIT-06** [E] In SIM-SPIRIT-A, with no true effect, testing four outcome families without correction yields at least one significant result in 18% (uncorrelated) to 14% (r = .6) of simulated two-group studies. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-A/iter-3#H1. *Limits:* Generic; not an audit of any original study. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-08.
- **TB-SPIRIT-09** [E] A meta-analysis of 55 longitudinal studies found a protective association of spirituality with harmful or hazardous alcohol and other drug use (RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.84-0.91; 13% risk reduction), reaching 18% for people attending religious services more than weekly. *Sources:* K19. *Limits:* Observational. The authors call for trials and longitudinal designs to explore causality. Mostly US samples. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4;R6.
- **TB-SPIRIT-10** [E] The E-value for K19's RR of 0.87 is 1.56, and 1.43 for the confidence limit. An unmeasured confounder associated with both spirituality and drug use by risk ratios of about 1.6 could fully explain the association. K19's result is a robust association, not causal evidence. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-1#V2;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-1#R1. *Limits:* The E-value is a sensitivity summary and does not show that such a confounder exists. *Deepens:* TB-SPIRIT-09.
- **TB-SPIRIT-11** [E] A meta-analysis of 8 studies found reductions in death anxiety after psychedelic administration (d = 0.70, 95% CI 0.42-0.97), stronger in clinical settings. A meta-regression associated mystical experiences with larger reductions (B = 4.90, p = 0.050). The authors list few studies, potential publication bias and high heterogeneity as limitations. *Sources:* K20. *Limits:* Includes uncontrolled designs. Expectancy and unblinding are not addressed here. The moderator result is borderline. *Deepens:* R4;TB-SPIRIT-01.
- **TB-SPIRIT-12** [E] With 8 studies, DerSimonian-Laird 95% CIs covered the truth in 90.7% of cases at tau = 0.4 (pre-registered H1 of below 90%: not supported; Hartung-Knapp about 95%). For a null moderator, a fixed-effect z-test gave false positives in 14% of simulations at tau = 0.2 and 35% at tau = 0.4, while a heterogeneity-aware t-test stayed near 5%. A p = .050 moderator result from 8 heterogeneous studies is fragile unless a heterogeneity-aware test was used. K20's estimator was not checked. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-2#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-5#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-5#R1. *Limits:* Illustrative study sizes. Normal approximations. *Deepens:* TB-SPIRIT-11.
- **TB-SPIRIT-13** [E] Sensitivity checks on Cycle A. With a stricter cut (80th percentile on all four subscales) only 69% of those classified as having a 'complete' experience were classified again at retest (omega .9, kappa .65; H1 of at least 80%: not supported). A Holm correction across four outcome families held family-wise error at 5% but cut power for one real effect from .78 to .59 (H1 of at most 10 points lost: not supported). Binary mystical-experience labels stay noisy, and multiplicity control is costly at typical sample sizes. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-3#R1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-4#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-4#R1. *Limits:* Illustrative cut and sample sizes. *Deepens:* TB-SPIRIT-05;TB-SPIRIT-06.
- **TB-SPIRIT-15** [E] With six focus groups, a theme present in 10% of groups goes entirely unobserved with probability .53, and one present in 30% with probability .12 (H1 supported). Absence of a theme in a six-group study is weak evidence that it is absent from the communities. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-6#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-6#R1. *Limits:* Independent-groups idealisation. *Deepens:* TB-SPIRIT-14.
- **TB-SPIRIT-04** [P] Keltner & Monroy (2026) review evidence that awe is a distinct emotion expressed in mouth and gaze activity, vocal bursts and upward head orientation with 'some degree of universality' (in one paradigm, five cultures overlapped in 63% of awe-expression elements) and elaborated culturally in choral music, dance and art of the sublime. *Sources:* K06. *Limits:* Narrative review by proponents of the basic-emotion approach. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-06.
- **TB-SPIRIT-14** [P] In six focus groups across three marginalised South African communities, participants described radical healing through three interwoven themes: faith and spirituality (churches, religious gatherings), collective and action-oriented healing (activism), and micro, everyday acts. The authors frame healing as an embodied, collective, action-oriented praxis that resists structures of oppression, linking spirituality with justice in community practice. *Sources:* K21. *Limits:* Qualitative and decolonial-participatory, so not designed for generalisation. Describes participants' accounts. *Deepens:* R4;R6.
- **TB-SPIRIT-07** [PS] Transcendence (descriptive): traditions describe mystical experience as contact with or realisation of a reality beyond ordinary experience (S15, which excludes paranormal experiences). Questionnaires such as the MEQ measure how people report such experiences; whether the experienced reality exists is a philosophical and spiritual question the measurements do not settle. *Sources:* S15;K04;G03. *Limits:* Interpretive. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-06;R4:C0v2-R4-15.
- **TB-SPIRIT-16** [PS] Spirit and justice: community accounts describe spirituality as a collective, embodied practice of resisting oppression (K21). The track records this as a description of how participants understand spirit. That spirituality produces justice is not a finding. GAIA's motherthread framing (PS) may hold the two together without claiming causation. *Sources:* K21;G03. *Limits:* Interpretive. *Deepens:* R4;R6.
- **TB-SPIRIT-08** [H] Hypothesis: self-transcendent emotions such as awe are one pathway linking spirituality to well-being (Report 4). After K05's failed replications, this remains a hypothesis without robust experimental support; it would be tested by preregistered, adequately powered induction studies with manipulation checks. *Sources:* K05;K06;S11. *Limits:* Not tested here. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-08;R4:C0v2-R4-14.
