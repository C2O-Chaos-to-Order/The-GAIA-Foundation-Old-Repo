# Subconscious — Track Cycle B (x6)

Track: Soul, Spirit, Subconscious and Consciousness (`../../README.md`). Scope and boundary: `../../FRAMEWORK.md` § Subconscious. Labels: **E** Established · **P** Plausible · **PS** Philosophical/Spiritual · **H** Hypothetical. GAIA is the motherthread (PS framing) and is kept separate from the science.

## Reading of this cycle
The Cycle A flaw is fixed. With non-negative knowledge, 'below-chance' participants still know about half as much as average, yet the resulting priming is usually undetectable (TB-SUBC-04). Subjective 'unseen' reports with above-chance accuracy arise in a model with no unconscious route (TB-SUBC-19). Subliminal affective priming is small and setting-dependent (K23). Unconscious-thought evidence is mixed in the way a small effect predicts (K22, TB-SUBC-14). The psychodynamic repressed unconscious remains contested (K24).

Changes against Cycle A: Cycle A claims are carried with IDs TB-* and a change note (`change_vs_prev_track_cycle`). TB-SUBC-04 is revised and the boundary claims are extended.

## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **TB-SUBC-01** [E] A systematic review of 73 contextual-cuing studies (181 awareness tests) found a meta-analytic awareness effect of dz = 0.31, so learning in these 'implicit' tasks was conscious; with a median N of 16, awareness tests had power of about .21, matching the 21.5% of significant awareness results. *Sources:* K09. *Limits:* One paradigm (contextual cuing); generalisation to other implicit-learning paradigms is argued, not shown. *Deepens:* R2;R5:C0v2-R5-03.
- **TB-SUBC-02** [E] SIM-SUBC-A reproduced K09's power argument: analytic power .213 and simulated .211 at n = 16 and dz = .31, 84 participants for 80% power. 79% of such awareness tests come out non-significant although awareness is real by construction, while pooling 181 tests detected awareness in every simulated literature. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-1#V2;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-1#V3;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-1#H2. *Limits:* Normal difference scores. *Deepens:* R2.
- **TB-SUBC-03** [E] Doyen et al. (2012) did not replicate the 'elderly prime slows walking' effect with automated timing and 120 participants; a walking-speed effect appeared only when experimenters believed participants would walk slower, and debriefing suggested awareness of the primes. *Sources:* K08. *Limits:* Two experiments; one classic paradigm. *Deepens:* R2.
- **TB-SUBC-04** [E] Redo with the Cycle A design flaw fixed (SIM-SUBC-B iteration 1, non-negative latent knowledge): participants selected post hoc as 'unaware' because their awareness score fell below chance still had a mean true knowledge of 0.24, about half the population mean of 0.5. So below-chance awareness scores do not show absence of knowledge. However, priming carried entirely by that knowledge reached significance in only 9.9% of simulated studies of N = 100 (pre-registered H1 of at least 50%: not supported). In this model the misclassification is real but usually too small to show up as 'unconscious priming'. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-1#V2;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-1#R1;K25. *Limits:* Toy model: one awareness measure with reliability .5, gamma-distributed knowledge and linear priming. The rate depends on N, reliability and b. K25's reliability-corrected regression methods are cited for context only and were not re-run. *Deepens:* R2.
- **TB-SUBC-06** [E] In SIM-SUBC-A's toy of K10's proposal (dreams sample 3 of 30 reactivated memories; dreaming itself does nothing), dreamed memories were recalled 0.36 SD better than all other memories, significant in 75% of simulated studies (pre-registered H1 of at least 80%: not supported, though the association is large), and no better than reactivated-but-not-dreamed memories (4.9%). Dream-memory associations need the reactivated-not-dreamed comparison before dreams are credited. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-3#H1;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-3#H2. *Limits:* Toy; uniform dream sampling assumed. *Deepens:* R2.
- **TB-SUBC-07** [E] Among 178 UK mental-health professionals, most indicated some belief in unconscious repression (over 78%) and dissociative amnesia (over 84%), with higher agreement among EMDR and other trauma-focused practitioners; the authors link such beliefs to the risk of false or non-experienced memories in therapy. *Sources:* K11. *Limits:* Describes beliefs, which are not evidence that repression occurs; convenience sample. *Deepens:* R2.
- **TB-SUBC-09** [E] Yogacara Buddhism (descriptive): Vasubandhu and his followers posit a receptacle or repository consciousness (alaya-vijnana), 'a subliminal form of cognitive awareness' underlying the propensities, habits and tendencies that direct action. *Sources:* S32. *Limits:* Describes a doctrine; not an empirical claim about the brain. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-09;R5.
- **TB-SUBC-10** [E] Advaita Vedanta (descriptive): waking (jagrat), dreaming (svapna) and deep sleep (susupti) point to a fourth state, turiya (pure consciousness), to be realised as the true self. Deep sleep is described as partial experience of its bliss. *Sources:* S31. *Limits:* Doctrine described, not tested. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-08;R5.
- **TB-SUBC-11** [E] History of the idea (descriptive): Leibniz allowed infinitely many degrees of consciousness and unconscious 'petites perceptions', distinguishing perception from apperception, an early philosophical form of an unconscious mind long before Freud. *Sources:* S16. *Limits:* Historical description. *Deepens:* R5:C0v2-R5-01.
- **TB-SUBC-13** [E] Across three experiments with 822 participants, Steindorf, Rummel and Boywitt (2021) found an unconscious thought effect only in the first two experiments. Less deliberation went with better decisions, but the benefit disappeared when thoughts were probed during the interval. The authors note that samples should have been larger to detect small effects. *Sources:* K22. *Limits:* One task (apartment evaluation). Thought probes may change the process they measure. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-01;R2.
- **TB-SUBC-14** [E] With an illustrative 90 per condition, a true d = 0.2 gives power .27 per experiment. Two or more of three experiments come out significant with probability .17 (pre-registered H1 of at least .5: not supported), and a mixed pattern with probability .59. Under d = 0 the corresponding figures are .007 and .14. K22's two-of-three pattern is far more likely under a small real effect than under none, though it cannot pin down the size. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-4#H1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-4#R1. *Limits:* Illustrative n; independent experiments. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-13.
- **TB-SUBC-15** [E] A three-level meta-analysis (62 studies, 182 effect sizes, 4,772 participants) of affective priming by briefly presented (150 ms or less) facial expressions found g = 0.32 (95% PI -0.23 to 0.88). Supraliminal primes produced larger effects than subliminal ones, but subliminal presentations still yielded reliable priming. *Sources:* K23. *Limits:* Includes the authors' own bias assessment (not re-run here). The prediction interval spans zero. Awareness checks vary across studies (see TB-SUBC-01). *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-03;R2.
- **TB-SUBC-16** [E] K23's interval implies a between-setting SD of about 0.28 and about 12.5% of settings with a true affective-priming effect of zero or below (H1 supported). In a selection toy, a true mean of 0.10 gave a naive pooled estimate of 0.26 (pre-registered H1 of at least 0.30: not supported). Selection can inflate such estimates substantially but did not fully reproduce 0.32 here. Subliminal affective priming is best described as small and setting-dependent. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-2#H1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-2#R1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-6#H1. *Limits:* Normal approximation to a three-level model; illustrative selection rule. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-15.
- **TB-SUBC-17** [E] Otgaar and colleagues (2019) report that belief in repressed memories remains common: 58% in one cited survey of clinicians, with signs of an increase since the 1990s. They argue that 'dissociative amnesia' functions as a substitute term for repression and that some therapeutic techniques risk producing false memories. The psychodynamic concept of a repressed unconscious remains scientifically contested. *Sources:* K24. *Limits:* The authors are one side of the 'memory wars'. Survey figures are cited from other studies. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-07;R2.
- **TB-SUBC-18** [E] Jimenez and colleagues (2025) review how objective and subjective awareness measures yield different thresholds, which leads to two different ways of demonstrating unconscious perception. They describe newer methods (regression-based Bayesian modelling, sensitivity-versus-awareness curves, liminal primes, two-interval forced choice) meant to address them. *Sources:* K25. *Limits:* Review; methods not tested here. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-01;R5.
- **TB-SUBC-19** [E] In a single-process signal-detection model with no unconscious route, discrimination accuracy on trials reported as 'unseen' was 61% (d' = 1, criterion 1; H1 supported), and 62.5% of trials were reported unseen. Above-chance performance under a subjective 'no awareness' report is therefore what a conservative criterion predicts. It does not by itself show a separate unconscious process. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-5#V2;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-5#H1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-5#R1. *Limits:* Equal-variance Gaussian toy. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-18;R5.
- **TB-SUBC-20** [E] In SIM-SUBC-B, dreaming had no causal role, but dreams preferentially sampled strongly encoded reactivated memories. Under that rule, dreamed memories beat reactivated-but-not-dreamed memories in 97% (beta 0.5) and 100% (beta 1) of simulated studies (H1 supported). Under uniform selection the rate was 5%. Testing K10's proposal requires controlling for encoding strength. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-3#R1. *Limits:* Toy; K10 specifies no selection rule. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-05;TB-SUBC-06.
- **TB-SUBC-08** [P] Tsikandilakis et al. (2026) distinguish 'subliminality' (responses to imperceptible elicitors) from an 'unconscious mind' of automatic processes that precede and later interact with awareness, and argue that a purely subliminal module could not support skill acquisition or adaptation. They note the lack of an experimental paradigm to test this. *Sources:* K07. *Limits:* Evolutionary argument, not an experiment. *Deepens:* R2;R5.
- **TB-SUBC-12** [PS] Scope: 'the subconscious' is not one thing. The track keeps apart the psychodynamic repressed unconscious (contested; K11 documents practitioner beliefs, not evidence), cognitive and implicit processing (evidence depends on awareness measurement, K09), sleep-based implicit memory processing (K10, a proposal) and contemplative doctrines (S32, S31, described). Claims about one may not be carried to another. *Sources:* G03;K07;K09;K10;K11;S32. *Limits:* Framework statement. *Deepens:* R2;R5.
- **TB-SUBC-21** [PS] Two senses of 'subconscious' are kept apart in this track. The psychodynamic repressed unconscious is described as a clinical and theoretical tradition whose central memory claim is contested (K24). The cognitive unconscious is supported for perception and automatic processing, but its size depends on how awareness is measured (K25). Contemplative store-consciousness views (Cycle A) are recorded as descriptions. *Sources:* K24;K25;G03. *Limits:* Interpretive organisation. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-12.
- **TB-SUBC-05** [H] Budson & Paller's memory theory of consciousness proposes that the explicit-memory system produces all conscious experience, that reactivation of stored memories during sleep for consolidation stays implicit (below experience), and that fragments of a subset of reactivated memories are strung into the dream storyline. This is a proposal, not an established account. *Sources:* K10. *Limits:* Theory paper; rival theories of dreaming and consciousness exist. *Deepens:* R2;R5:C0v2-R5-08.
