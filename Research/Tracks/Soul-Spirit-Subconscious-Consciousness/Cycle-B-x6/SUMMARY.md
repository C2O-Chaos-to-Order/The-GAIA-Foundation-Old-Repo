## Readings
- **Soul.** Cycle B added implicit and end-of-life evidence and tested death-anxiety accounts. Dualism persists implicitly in people who reject it explicitly (K15). A one-belief model cannot produce that, so the implicit effect is something other than hidden belief (TB-SOUL-12). End-of-life experiences are common and often comforting (K16). Terror Management's core effects failed replication (K17) and shrink under bias correction (K18), so the 'afterlife beliefs defend against death anxiety' account is now a hypothesis in this track. Nothing here bears on whether souls exist.
- **Spirit.** Spirituality is robustly associated with less harmful drug use (K19), but an E-value of 1.56 keeps this short of causal evidence. Psychedelic reductions in death anxiety are moderate-to-large in a small, heterogeneous literature (K20), and its mystical-experience moderator is fragile with 8 studies (TB-SPIRIT-12). A Spirit x Justice bridge comes from community accounts of radical healing (K21, qualitative).
- **Subconscious.** The Cycle A flaw is fixed. With non-negative knowledge, 'below-chance' participants still know about half as much as average, yet the resulting priming is usually undetectable (TB-SUBC-04). Subjective 'unseen' reports with above-chance accuracy arise in a model with no unconscious route (TB-SUBC-19). Subliminal affective priming is small and setting-dependent (K23). Unconscious-thought evidence is mixed in the way a small effect predicts (K22, TB-SUBC-14). The psychodynamic repressed unconscious remains contested (K24).
- **Consciousness.** Two matrix gaps were addressed. For Consciousness x Justice, covert consciousness in unresponsive patients is common enough that a negative test leaves a substantial chance of consciousness (K26, K30, TB-CONS-11), and K30's presumption of consciousness is recorded as an ethical position. For Consciousness x Love, brief meditation showed no total effect on compassion, the pattern expected for a small mediated effect (K28; flagged run). The adversarial-collaboration dispute gained a methodological point: decodability is not consciousness (TB-CONS-13).
