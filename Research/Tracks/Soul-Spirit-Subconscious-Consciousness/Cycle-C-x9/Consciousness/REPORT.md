## Reading of this cycle
Cycle C read the primary IIT-GNWT adversarial collaboration (K42), which challenged key tenets of both theories. Scoring shows one failed prediction is weak evidence against a theory, but two of three central failures are substantial (TC-CONS-19), consistent with K41's caution. Dreaming under anaesthesia is common, mostly pleasant, and a clear case of disconnected consciousness (K32); its reported independence from awareness rests on low-powered studies, and interview timing explains under 10% of the clinical-experimental recall gap (TC-CONS-21). The Consciousness x Justice cell gained a survey of neurologists' diagnosis and treatment attitudes (K47), a reporting inconsistency the track could not resolve (TC-CONS-23), and an ethical-position statement (TC-CONS-24).

Changes against Cycle B: Cycle B claims are carried with IDs TC-* and a change note (`change_vs_prev_track_cycle`). The boundary statements TC-SOUL-10 and TC-CONS-07 are extended, and TC-CONS-07 is revised to include K42.

## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **TC-CONS-01** [E] In Whyte, Hohwy & Smith's active-inference model of masking, late P3b-like potentials and increased PFC activity were produced by the working-memory demands of self-report and vanished without report, yet simulated PFC activity on visible trials still crossed the reportability threshold. On this model, no-report results do not necessarily contradict cognitive (global-workspace or higher-order) theories. *Sources:* K13. *Limits:* One computational model; shows compatibility, not that the theories are right. *Deepens:* R5:C1-R5-02;R5:C2-R5-03.
- **TC-CONS-02** [E] In SIM-CONS-A's minimal threshold toy (gating + report demand), removing report cut the visible-minus-invisible PFC contrast by 60% while 84% of visible trials still exceeded the reportability threshold. K13's qualitative pattern follows from these two assumptions, so the toy shows the logic is coherent but adds no evidence about brains. *Sources:* SIM-CONS-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-CONS-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-CONS-A/iter-1#H2. *Limits:* Assumption-driven by construction. *Deepens:* R5:C1-R5-02.
- **TC-CONS-04** [E] The Minimal Phenomenal Experience questionnaire (92 items; 1,403 usable of 3,627 responses from meditators, mostly Vipassana 43.9% and Zen 34.9%) yielded 12 factors explaining 44% of variance, including 'Pure Awareness in Dream and Sleep', 'Luminosity', 'Wakeful Presence' and 'Witness Consciousness'. *Sources:* K14. *Limits:* Self-selected meditators; exploratory factor names are interpretive. *Deepens:* R5:C3-R5-07;R5:C0v2-R5-04.
- **TC-CONS-05** [E] In SIM-CONS-A, 92 structureless items (n = 1,403) gave about 43 'factors' by the eigenvalue-above-1 rule but about 0 by parallel analysis; with 12 true factors parallel analysis recovered exactly 12 at loadings of .4 to .75, and '12 components explain 44%' corresponded to loadings near .6. K14's dimensionality is statistically plausible for data of its size; what the factors are experiences of is a separate question. *Sources:* SIM-CONS-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-CONS-A/iter-2#H1;SIM-CONS-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-CONS-A/iter-3#H1;SIM-CONS-A/iter-3#H2. *Limits:* Principal components on simulated normal items; K14 used factor analysis on real data (not re-run). *Deepens:* R5:C3-R5-07.
- **TC-CONS-08** [E] An individual-participant-data meta-analysis (56 studies, 1,248 patients with disorders of consciousness) found cognitive motor dissociation (volitional brain responses without behavioural response) less often after anoxic (OR 0.35) and cerebrovascular (OR 0.51) injury than after traumatic brain injury, and more often in MCS-minus than in UWS (OR 1.50). Time since injury and test modality were not independently associated. *Sources:* K26. *Limits:* Heterogeneous paradigms. QUADAS-2 shows some concerns for index tests and flow and timing. *Deepens:* R5;TB-CONS-01.
- **TC-CONS-11** [E] Assume prevalence .25, specificity .95 and sensitivity .5 (illustrative). A patient with a NEGATIVE covert-consciousness test is then still conscious with probability .15 (H1 supported). A negative test does not rule consciousness out, which is the probabilistic core of K30's presumption argument. Separately, K26's OR of 0.35 at a baseline detection rate of .25 corresponds to a rate of .10 and a risk ratio of .42, not .35 (H1 supported). *Sources:* SIM-CONS-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-1#R1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-2#H1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-2#R1. *Limits:* Assumed test characteristics; CMD is not identical to consciousness. *Deepens:* TB-CONS-08;TB-CONS-09;TB-CONS-10.
- **TC-CONS-13** [E] In a model where the stimulus category is coded on every trial and awareness is independent of that code, a decoder trained on seen trials classified unseen trials at 72% (signal 0.1) and 90% (signal 0.2; H1 supported). Decodability alone is therefore not a marker of conscious content, which supports the logical point in K27 without deciding the empirical dispute. *Sources:* SIM-CONS-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-3#R1. *Limits:* Toy decoder; independence of awareness assumed. *Deepens:* TB-CONS-12.
- **TC-CONS-15** [E] Sensitivity checks on Cycle A. With weak report demand and a higher reportability threshold, removing report reduced the PFC contrast by only 33% (H1: not supported), and only 65% of visible trials exceeded threshold (H2: not supported). The Cycle A no-report pattern depends on parameters. With 5-point skewed ordinal items, the Kaiser rule still retained about 44 spurious factors while parallel analysis retained none (supported). *Sources:* SIM-CONS-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-5#H1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-5#H2;SIM-CONS-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-6#H1. *Limits:* Toy models. *Deepens:* TB-CONS-02;TB-CONS-05.
- **TC-CONS-17** [E] In the preregistered adversarial collaboration testing IIT and GNWT (Cogitate Consortium 2025, Nature; n = 256; fMRI, MEG and intracranial EEG), information about conscious content was found in visual, ventrotemporal and inferior frontal cortex. The results fit some predictions of both theories while substantially challenging key tenets of each. For IIT, sustained synchronisation within posterior cortex was missing. For GNWT, there was a general lack of ignition at stimulus offset and limited prefrontal representation of some conscious dimensions. *Sources:* K42. *Limits:* One paradigm (suprathreshold visual stimuli, mostly task-irrelevant). Proponents dispute interpretations (K27 for GNWT). It tests theories' predictions, not consciousness itself. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-12;TC-CONS-07.
- **TC-CONS-19** [E] In SIM-CONS-C, a true theory tested on 3 critical predictions at power .8 fails at least one 49% of the time, and on 5 at power .9, 41% of the time (H1 supported both times). With a prior of .5 and false theories passing 30% of tests, one failure leaves the posterior at .67 (3 tests) or .92 (5 tests) (H2 supported). Two failures out of three drop it to .18 (pre-registered H2 of at least .5: not supported). This supports K41's point that one failure is weak evidence against a theory. It also shows that several central failures, as K42 reports for both theories, are substantial evidence against them under these assumptions. *Sources:* SIM-CONS-C/iter-1#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-1#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-1#H2;SIM-CONS-C/iter-1#R1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-5#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-5#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-5#H2;SIM-CONS-C/iter-5#R1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-8#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-8#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-8#H2;SIM-CONS-C/iter-8#R1. *Limits:* Cartoon: independent, equally weighted binary tests. K42 weighted predictions and they are not independent. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-17;TC-CONS-18.
- **TC-CONS-20** [E] A scoping review of 157 studies (1921-2024; 87,866 participants) found dream recall after anaesthesia typically 3%-8% in large clinical trials and often 40%-80% in experimental settings. Ketamine and propofol were most associated with recall, and dreams were mostly pleasant with everyday autobiographical content. Immediate interviews gave higher recall than delayed ones. Dream recall was not associated with intraoperative awareness. Anaesthesia dreaming is treated as disconnected consciousness, experience without environmental awareness, distinct from both unconsciousness and connected consciousness. *Sources:* K32. *Limits:* Scoping review with heterogeneous definitions and assessment methods. Neural evidence is limited. *Deepens:* TC-SUBC-20;TC-CONS-11.
- **TC-CONS-21** [E] Two checks on K32. (1) With awareness at 0.5% and recall at 6%, a study of 2,000 patients detects even a tripled awareness risk among dream recallers with power 0.24 (H1 supported). A pooled 10,000 patients gives power 0.74 (pre-registered H1 of below 0.5: not supported). 'Not associated' therefore rests on low-powered single studies unless data are pooled. (2) Taking K32's immediate (about 10%) and delayed (5.9%) recall at face value, interview timing explains only 7% of the clinical-experimental gap at the midpoints and 6% at the range ends (H1 supported both times). Other factors (drugs, suggestion, populations, methods) must carry most of it. *Sources:* SIM-CONS-C/iter-2#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-2#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-2#R1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-6#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-6#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-6#R1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-3#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-3#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-3#R1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-9#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-9#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-9#R1. *Limits:* Illustrative awareness rate and RR. The timing arithmetic combines figures from different studies and assumes one uniform effect. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-20.
- **TC-CONS-22** [E] Consciousness x Justice. In a vignette survey of 360 Chinese neurologists (78% response), 63% chose the correct diagnostic category for patients with disorders of consciousness or locked-in syndrome. 73% believed patients in unresponsive wakefulness could feel pain. Most preferred never to limit life-sustaining treatment (46.7%, 62.8% and 66.7% for UWS, MCS and LIS vignettes; chi-square 5.93, p = .052). Deciding without a surrogate was rated extremely challenging. The authors call for training and more long-term-care resources, so who is judged conscious bears directly on treatment ethics and resource allocation. *Sources:* K47. *Limits:* Vignettes rather than real patients, one country, and self-selected respondents. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-11;TC-CONS-16.
- **TC-CONS-03** [P] Integrated information theory predicts that silencing all active neurons in the main complex leaves consciousness present (silent-brain prediction) and that disabling silent neurons can change experience (disabled-neuron prediction). Against the objection that these are untestable, Ponce de Leon & Yoshimi (2026) argue both can be tested, while noting why such theoretical disputes are hard to resolve (Lakatos). *Sources:* K12. *Limits:* Philosophical analysis; no experiment run. *Deepens:* R5:C0v2-R5-08;R5:C2-R5-03.
- **TC-CONS-09** [P] A neuropalliative review reports evidence that up to one in four patients diagnosed with unresponsive wakefulness syndrome may have covert consciousness, and that cognitive motor dissociation is associated with better functional outcomes. *Sources:* K30. *Limits:* 'Up to' figure reported secondarily. Prevalence depends on the tests used (TB-CONS-08). *Deepens:* TB-CONS-08;R5.
- **TC-CONS-12** [P] Proponents of global neuronal workspace theory argue that several analyses in the IIT-GNWT adversarial collaboration support GNWT predictions, such as transient ignition. They also argue that posterior decoding, presented as evidence for IIT, could have arisen from purely non-conscious processes. *Sources:* K27. *Limits:* Partisan commentary. The original Nature article and the IIT replies were not read in this cycle. *Deepens:* TB-CONS-03;R5.
- **TC-CONS-14** [P] Consciousness and love: in a preregistered trial (n = 80), one brief mindfulness meditation did not change compassionate responses overall. However, a serial path ran through mindfulness state and decentration (K28). In SIM-CONS-B a small, fully mediated effect gave total-effect power of only .13 at n = 80, while the indirect path was jointly significant in 92% of studies. The 'no total effect, significant indirect path' pattern is what a small mediated effect predicts. Disclosure: this run's validation check V1 failed narrowly (null indirect rate .055 against a pre-registered limit of .05, within Monte Carlo error of .05), so the claim is labelled Plausible. *Sources:* K28;SIM-CONS-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-4#H1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-4#H2. *Limits:* Illustrative path sizes. Joint-significance test rather than bootstrap. Validation V1 Fail recorded as is (see VERIFICATION). *Deepens:* R5;R6;MR.
- **TC-CONS-18** [P] He (2025) argues that a theory must be testable, generating falsifiable hypotheses, but that treating single contrary results as falsification would be a mistake. She notes that Hebbian plasticity remains a cornerstone theory despite important exceptions. She also notes that a few dominant consciousness theories have become entrenched, with young researchers aligned to camps, and that adversarial collaborations were funded to pit them against each other. *Sources:* K41. *Limits:* Opinion piece. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-17.
- **TC-CONS-23** [P] Consciousness x Justice consistency check on K47. A chi-square test of 'never limit' against other answers, with about 120 neurologists per vignette and the reported 46.7%, 62.8% and 66.7%, has power 0.86 (pre-registered H1 of below 0.8: not supported), and 0.98 with 200 per group. Proportions that differ this much with these group sizes would usually give p well below .052. The reported chi-square of 5.93 therefore probably comes from a different table, such as all three answer options or fewer item responses. The track could not resolve this from the text read, so the p = .052 'no difference' should not be read as evidence that diagnosis does not affect treatment preferences. *Sources:* SIM-CONS-C/iter-4#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-4#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-4#R1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-7#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-7#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-7#R1. *Limits:* Equal groups of about 120 assumed. K47's exact test and table are not reproduced. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-22.
- **TC-CONS-06** [PS] 'Pure awareness' (interpretive): contemplative traditions describe consciousness without ordinary content (for example turiya in Advaita, S31), and meditators' reports of such states have a measurable structure (K14). Whether awareness without content exists as more than a reported state is a philosophical question the track does not settle. *Sources:* S31;K14;S32;G03. *Limits:* Interpretive. *Deepens:* R5:C0v2-R5-12;R5:C3-R5-07.
- **TC-CONS-07** [PS] Boundary: no theory has solved consciousness. The preregistered adversarial collaboration between IIT and GNWT (K42) found results that fit some predictions of each theory and challenged key tenets of both. Track Cycles A-C sharpened methodological questions (report confounds, testability, decoding without awareness, how to score failed predictions) but decided none of the theories. *Sources:* S16;S19;G03;K42. *Limits:* Framework statement. *Deepens:* R5:C0v2-R5-12.
- **TC-CONS-10** [PS] Consciousness and justice: Hawkins (2026) argues that suffering in disorders of consciousness includes existential dimensions beyond pain, and that a presumption of consciousness should guide clinical care regardless of imaging findings. The track records this as an ethical position about the dignity and moral standing of possibly conscious patients, not as an empirical result. *Sources:* K30;G03. *Limits:* Normative argument; one author. *Deepens:* R5;R6;MR.
- **TC-CONS-16** [PS] Josipovic and Miskovic (2020) propose that consciousness-as-such is a non-conceptual, nondual awareness of a unique kind, not captured by the two-dimensional model of arousal plus phenomenal content. This contrasts with the minimal-phenomenal-experience framing of K14. The track records both as positions and links them to contemplative accounts (Report 3). *Sources:* K29;K14. *Limits:* Theoretical proposal. *Deepens:* TB-CONS-06;R3;R5.
- **TC-CONS-24** [PS] Consciousness x Justice (ethical position, not a finding). Some diagnosed unresponsive patients are covertly conscious (K26, K30), and many clinicians misclassify disorders of consciousness on vignettes (K47). Fairness to such patients therefore depends on careful assessment, care that does not assume their absence, and resources for long-term care. This extends the Master Report's care question. *Sources:* K47;K30;K26. *Limits:* Normative statement. The empirical inputs carry their own uncertainty. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-15;TC-CONS-22.
