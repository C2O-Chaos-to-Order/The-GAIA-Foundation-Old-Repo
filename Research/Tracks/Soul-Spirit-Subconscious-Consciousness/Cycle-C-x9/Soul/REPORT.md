# Soul — Track Cycle C (x9)

Track: Soul, Spirit, Subconscious and Consciousness (`../../README.md`). Scope and boundary: `../../FRAMEWORK.md` § Soul. Labels: **E** Established · **P** Plausible · **PS** Philosophical/Spiritual · **H** Hypothetical. GAIA is the motherthread (PS framing) and is kept separate from the science.

## Reading of this cycle
Cycle C turned to near-death experiences. A clinical summary reports them in 15-17% of critically ill patients and urges clinicians not to pathologise them (K37); its claim that biomedical models cannot account for them is recorded as Hypothetical. Reliving NDE memories engaged autobiographical-memory networks in 15 people (K38), but at n = 15 and p < .001 uncorrected both the activations and the null regions are weak evidence (TC-SOUL-23). Chinese NDE narratives share common features and occasionally carry local motifs (K46), in a sample too small to separate real from chance contrasts (TC-SOUL-25). Five of nine passes added no new source, one fetched source was a duplicate (K44 = K01), and K01's primary paper is still missing.

Changes against Cycle B: Cycle B claims are carried with IDs TC-* and a change note (`change_vs_prev_track_cycle`). The boundary statements TC-SOUL-10 and TC-CONS-07 are extended, and TC-CONS-07 is revised to include K42.

## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **TC-SOUL-02** [E] Replication record in the cognitive science of supernatural belief (as reported by van Elk 2022): the finding that priming analytic thinking reduces supernatural belief could not be replicated in a high-powered replication, and a cross-cultural study found the religiosity-intuitive-thinking relation in only three of 13 countries. *Sources:* K01. *Limits:* Reported via a perspective article, not re-analysed here. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-13.
- **TC-SOUL-04** [E] In a survey of 2,657 neuroscientists, 63.62% agreed with reductive physicalism, 36.68% with dualism, 42.94% with nonreductive physicalism, 40.94% with dual-aspect monism and 34.69% with functionalism (positions rated separately), and only 17.52% agreed that free will does not exist. *Sources:* K03. *Limits:* Self-selected respondents; agreement items, not forced choice; describes beliefs, not truth of the positions. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-16;R5:C0v2-R5-12.
- **TC-SOUL-05** [E] In SIM-SOUL-A, K03's published agreement shares alone force only 0.3% of respondents to agree with both reductive physicalism and dualism (pre-registered H1 of at least 10%: not supported; independence would give 23.3%), although they do force at least 29.7% to agree with two or more of the five positions. How many neuroscientists hold both views cannot be read from the marginals. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-2#H1;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-2#H2. *Limits:* Uses only marginals; K03's cross-tabulations (SI) were not read. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-16.
- **TC-SOUL-06** [E] In open-ended answers from 387 science-oriented Finns, 76% mentioned some kind of annihilation after death and 35% expressed some kind of continuity, mostly nonreligious (social bonds, nature); secular continuity was mentioned more by unbelievers, while theists relied mainly on afterlife beliefs. *Sources:* K02. *Limits:* Online sample recruited through science-promoting organisations; single-country; categories overlap. *Deepens:* R3:C1-R3-02;R3:C0v2-R3-15.
- **TC-SOUL-07** [E] In SIM-SOUL-A, K02's coding reliability (96% agreement, kappa .79) implies chance agreement of .81, i.e. a skewed category (prevalence about .11 in a binary symmetric-error model, coder error about 2.4%); the same coders would reach kappa .91 at prevalence .5 and .65 at prevalence .05. Kappa values in soul-conception coding (Report 3) therefore need the category prevalence reported beside them. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-3#V2;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-3#H1;K02;W07. *Limits:* Binary simplification of a multi-category scheme. *Deepens:* R3:C2-R3-04;R3:C3-R3-02.
- **TC-SOUL-08** [E] In SIM-SOUL-A's illustrative continuity-judgment model (marginals 8% physical, 18% mental, not K01's numbers), a mental-over-physical continuity difference was overwhelming (t = 43 at N = 10,000; power .998 at n = 100) while 67% of simulated respondents said every state ceases and only 17% showed a 'dualist' pattern: 'people favour mental continuity' and 'most people expect total cessation' are both true of the same data. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-1#H2;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-1#H3. *Limits:* Illustrative parameters; demonstrates a reading problem, not a fact about any population. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-16.
- **TC-SOUL-12** [E] In SIM-SOUL-B, suppose implicit and explicit answers reflect one latent dualism belief. Then explicit rejecters show a NEGATIVE mean implicit score (-0.32), and a significant positive score in 0% of studies (pre-registered H1 of at least 50%: not supported). A finding like K15's therefore needs an implicit component that is independent of the explicit belief, or a measurement artefact. It cannot be a hidden copy of the explicit belief. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-3#R1. *Limits:* Single-factor linear model with illustrative parameters. Shows what a result implies; does not test K15's data. *Deepens:* TB-SOUL-11.
- **TC-SOUL-13** [E] In a longitudinal study of 85 terminally ill patients in three Brazilian palliative care units, 34% reported end-of-life experiences (ELEs). The median time from first ELE to death was 29.5 days. Nearly half (49%) of the ELEs occurred during sleep, 80% were perceived as real and 52% involved deceased loved ones. 36% were described as comforting and 27% as distressing, and 78% were reported by non-delirious patients. *Sources:* K16. *Limits:* Small single-country sample. 65% believed in an afterlife. Only 41% were followed to death. The experiences are described; nothing about their source or about survival of death follows. *Deepens:* R3:C1-R3-02;R6.
- **TC-SOUL-14** [E] The 95% Wilson interval for K16's 29 of 85 is 24.9% to 44.7%. In SIM-SOUL-B's illustrative follow-up model, following only 41% of patients to death lowered observed prevalence to 0.86 of the true value (pre-registered H1 of below 0.8: not supported). Incomplete follow-up biases ELE prevalence downward, but only modestly under these assumptions. The sampling interval is the larger source of uncertainty. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-4#H1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-4#R1;K16. *Limits:* True prevalence, survival and observation fractions are illustrative. *Deepens:* TB-SOUL-13.
- **TC-SOUL-15** [E] Treger, Benau and Timko (2023) ran five studies with standard mortality-salience procedures. They could not replicate the basic worldview-defence pattern of Terror Management Theory, and pooled effect sizes did not differ significantly from zero. *Sources:* K17. *Limits:* Five samples from one team. The authors discuss possible methodological reasons. Not a test of every TMT prediction. *Deepens:* R3;R6.
- **TC-SOUL-16** [E] A meta-analysis of 64 effect sizes for the mortality salience x norm salience interaction found g = 0.34 (95% CI 0.26-0.41). Bias-adjustment methods indicated publication bias and/or researcher degrees of freedom and gave smaller estimates, in several cases nonsignificant (corrected g from -0.36 to 0.15). *Sources:* K18. *Limits:* Concerns one TMT moderator hypothesis. Bias-correction methods disagree with each other. *Deepens:* R3;R6.
- **TC-SOUL-17** [E] In SIM-SOUL-B, a pooled null from five studies of 50 per group has power .97 against K18's uncorrected g = 0.34 but only .39 against g = 0.15. A K17-style null is therefore informative against the uncorrected effect, not against the bias-corrected range. Separately, publishing non-significant results with probability .15 turned a true mean of 0 into a naive pooled estimate of only 0.10 (pre-registered H1 of at least 0.30: not supported). In this toy, selective publication alone did not reproduce an estimate as large as 0.34. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-5#H1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-5#H2;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-6#H1. *Limits:* Illustrative n. Main-effect rather than interaction studies. Real literatures also involve analytic flexibility, which is not modelled. *Deepens:* TB-SOUL-15;TB-SOUL-16.
- **TC-SOUL-18** [E] K03's five marginals force no pair of mind-brain positions to be co-endorsed by 10% or more. The largest forced overlap is 6.6% (reductive plus nonreductive physicalism; H1 not supported). K02's marginals (76% annihilation, 35% continuity) force at least 11% of respondents to have mentioned both, which is supported. Overlap between afterlife conceptions is therefore built into K02's data but cannot be read from K03's. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-1#R1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-2#H1. *Limits:* Uses marginals only. *Deepens:* TB-SOUL-04;TB-SOUL-06.
- **TC-SOUL-23** [E] In SIM-SOUL-C, K38's design is weak in both directions. At n = 15 and p < .001, a large true effect (d = 0.8) is detected at a voxel with power 0.31 (H1 supported), so 'no activation in limbic areas' is weak evidence of absence. Doubling to n = 30 raises power to 0.82 (pre-registered H1 of below 0.5: not supported). In pure-noise data with the same p < .001 and 10-voxel rule, 92.5% of simulated datasets showed at least one false 'activation' cluster, with 2.0 to 2.5 per dataset on average depending on smoothness (H1 supported). K38's positive and negative findings both need replication with corrected thresholds. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-C/iter-1#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-1#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-1#R1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-2#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-2#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-2#R1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-6#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-6#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-6#R1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-7#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-7#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-7#R1. *Limits:* Small synthetic grid (fewer voxels than a brain, which would add false clusters) and Gaussian smoothness. Real data and K38's small-volume corrections differ. *Deepens:* TC-SOUL-22.
- **TC-SOUL-25** [E] In SIM-SOUL-C, K46's 4 of 23 versus 1 of 25 gives Fisher p = .18. Even if those proportions were true, a design of that size detects the difference with power below .5 (H1 supported); about 100 per group would be needed for .85. With no true difference in any of six features, at least one feature appeared 15 or more points 'descriptively higher' in 54% of simulated studies (H1 supported). With one real 20-point difference, some feature looked 15 or more points higher in 80% of studies, but the real one did so in only 63% (H1 supported). Because a 'higher' feature appears in more than half of studies with no difference at all, K46's descriptive contrasts cannot separate real from chance differences. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-C/iter-3#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-3#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-3#R1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-4#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-4#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-4#R1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-9#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-9#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-9#R1. *Limits:* Treats observed proportions as true, features as independent and base rates as illustrative. *Deepens:* TC-SOUL-24.
- **TC-SOUL-26** [E] In SIM-SOUL-C, classifying 37% of respondents as NDE cases by a cut-off on a scale with reliability .8 made 18.8% of classified cases true non-cases (pre-registered H1 of at least 20%: not supported). With reliability .9 the share was 13.1% (H1 not supported). Cut-off classification adds modest but non-trivial misclassification, smaller than the threshold set in advance. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-C/iter-5#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-5#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-5#R1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-8#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-8#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-8#R1. *Limits:* K46's scale reliability was not available in what was read; .8 and .9 are assumptions. *Deepens:* TC-SOUL-24.
- **TC-SOUL-01** [P] van Elk (2022) describes a 24-country vignette study (more than 10,000 participants): mental states were judged more likely than physical states to continue after a person's death, but the modal response was that both mental and physical states cease, which questions the universality of mind-body dualism and fits an 'intuitive materialism' account. *Sources:* K01. *Limits:* Secondary description of a study that was 'submitted' when K01 was written; K01 gives no numbers. Vignette judgments are not a measure of explicit soul belief. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-13;R3:C0v2-R3-16.
- **TC-SOUL-03** [P] Among proposed proximate mechanisms of belief in souls, afterlife and spirits (mentalizing, dualistic reasoning, intuitive thinking), the link between mentalizing ability and supernatural belief 'appears robust' but with small effect sizes, and people with mentalizing difficulties can still hold such beliefs. *Sources:* K01. *Limits:* Small effects; correlational; reported via review. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-13;R2.
- **TC-SOUL-11** [P] In five experiments using a brief implicit association task, Berent and Sansiveri (2025) found that people associated the mind with ethereal properties ('airy') and the body with physical ones. This held even in participants who explicitly rejected dualism. The authors call dualism an implicit bias that persists despite explicit attitudes. *Sources:* K15. *Limits:* Implicit association measures have contested validity. The word-property design may tap semantic associations rather than belief. Western educated samples; one research group. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-16;TB-SOUL-01.
- **TC-SOUL-20** [P] A brief clinical summary in CMAJ (Delgado-Ron 2026) states that near-death experiences occur in 15% to 17% of critically ill hospitalised patients. It says they often reduce fear of death and increase prosocial behaviour, and that clinicians should not pathologise them. Some patients report dismissive responses from health care providers. *Sources:* K37. *Limits:* Secondary summary of a few studies, partly by researchers from one research tradition. The prevalence figure was not checked against its primary source. *Deepens:* TC-SOUL-13;R3.
- **TC-SOUL-22** [P] In a preliminary fMRI study, 15 people with documented life-threatening events and loss of consciousness relived their memories of 'recalled experiences of death'. Doing so activated autobiographical-memory and default-mode regions (precuneus, posterior cingulate, medial prefrontal cortex). No activation was found in primary sensorimotor or limbic areas. The authors read the pattern as vivid episodic memory, not like primary sensory hallucination. *Sources:* K38. *Limits:* 15 participants, no control memory condition reported in the abstract, whole-brain p < .001 uncorrected. The study concerns memories of the experience, not the experience itself. *Deepens:* TC-SOUL-13;R3;R5.
- **TC-SOUL-24** [P] In a Chinese online sample (63 participants: 23 self-reported NDEs, 25 NDE-like experiences, 15 below threshold), narratives shared common NDE features: out-of-body experience, bright light, emotional change. Culturally specific motifs (Guimen Pass, Black and White Impermanence, karmic judgement, reincarnation) were infrequent, appearing in 4 of 23 NDE and 1 of 25 NDE-like narratives. The authors call the findings preliminary and hypothesis-generating. *Sources:* K46. *Limits:* Retrospective self-report, unverified medical contexts and a convenience sample. Classification rests on a self-report scale cut-off. *Deepens:* TC-SOUL-13;TC-SOUL-03.
- **TC-SOUL-09** [PS] Persistence of the person: psychological-continuity views distinguish psychological connectedness from continuity and face the fission problem; soul, organism and continuity answers remain competing philosophical positions, and the track records them without choosing one. *Sources:* U13;G03. *Limits:* Philosophical; not decidable by the data reviewed. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-13.
- **TC-SOUL-10** [PS] Boundary: nothing in Track Cycles A-C establishes the existence, nonexistence or nature of a soul. The empirical results concern beliefs about souls and how such beliefs are measured. GAIA as motherthread organises the topic as a Philosophical/Spiritual principle and is not a finding. *Sources:* G03. *Limits:* Framework statement. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-18.
- **TC-SOUL-19** [H] The track treats as a hypothesis, not a finding, the Terror Management claim that beliefs in souls and afterlife defend against death anxiety. Core mortality-salience effects failed replication (K17) and pooled estimates shrink under bias correction (K18). *Sources:* K17;K18. *Limits:* TMT includes correlational and clinical work not reviewed here. *Deepens:* R3;R6.
- **TC-SOUL-21** [H] K37 also asserts that NDEs can occur when cortical activity should be minimal and that current biomedical models cannot fully account for all observed phenomena. The track records this as a contested hypothesis, not a finding. The track read no primary evidence on timing or veridical perception this cycle. *Sources:* K37. *Limits:* Assertion in a short summary. Skeptical neuroscientific accounts were not reviewed in this cycle. *Deepens:* TC-SOUL-10;R3;R5.
