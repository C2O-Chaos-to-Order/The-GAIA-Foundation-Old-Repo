# Soul — Track Cycle D (x12)

Track: Soul, Spirit, Subconscious and Consciousness (`../../README.md`). Scope and boundary: `../../FRAMEWORK.md` § Soul. Labels: **E** Established · **P** Plausible · **PS** Philosophical/Spiritual · **H** Hypothetical. GAIA is the motherthread (PS framing) and is kept separate from the science.

## Reading of this cycle
Cycle D read the primary 24-country continuity study (K61), which upgrades TD-SOUL-01 to Established: mental continuity is judged more likely than bodily continuity, religiosity and religious framing raise continuity judgments, yet cessation is the modal answer in 19 of 24 countries. Judgments cluster within persons, as broad stances (TD-SOUL-28), and a single exception country is expected from noise (TD-SOUL-29). Soul and afterlife beliefs predict lower approval of mind upload (K55), though K55's 'full mediation' follows from collinearity (TD-SOUL-31). A new veridical-NDE scale (K53) has only moderate, imprecisely estimated agreement (TD-SOUL-33), and hidden-target designs need thousands of arrests under central assumptions (TD-SOUL-35).

Changes against Cycle C: Cycle C claims are carried with IDs TD-* and a change note (`change_vs_prev_track_cycle`). TD-SOUL-01 is revised (Plausible -> Established, primary study K61 read); the boundary statements TD-SOUL-10 and TD-CONS-07 are extended to Cycle D.

## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **TD-SOUL-01** [E] A preregistered 24-country vignette study (N = 10,195; primary report K61, described secondhand in K01) found that mental states were judged more likely than bodily states to continue after death, and that individual religiosity and a religious framing increased continuity judgments. Yet the modal response in most countries, and in the pooled sample, was cessation of all states, which questions the universality of mind-body dualism. *Sources:* K01;K61. *Limits:* K61 was read as a PsyArXiv preprint (version file AfterlifeBeliefsReligionV2); the published version was not read. Vignette judgments are not explicit soul belief. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-13;R3:C0v2-R3-16.
- **TD-SOUL-02** [E] Replication record in the cognitive science of supernatural belief (as reported by van Elk 2022): the finding that priming analytic thinking reduces supernatural belief could not be replicated in a high-powered replication, and a cross-cultural study found the religiosity-intuitive-thinking relation in only three of 13 countries. *Sources:* K01. *Limits:* Reported via a perspective article, not re-analysed here. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-13.
- **TD-SOUL-04** [E] In a survey of 2,657 neuroscientists, 63.62% agreed with reductive physicalism, 36.68% with dualism, 42.94% with nonreductive physicalism, 40.94% with dual-aspect monism and 34.69% with functionalism (positions rated separately), and only 17.52% agreed that free will does not exist. *Sources:* K03. *Limits:* Self-selected respondents; agreement items, not forced choice; describes beliefs, not truth of the positions. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-16;R5:C0v2-R5-12.
- **TD-SOUL-05** [E] In SIM-SOUL-A, K03's published agreement shares alone force only 0.3% of respondents to agree with both reductive physicalism and dualism (pre-registered H1 of at least 10%: not supported; independence would give 23.3%), although they do force at least 29.7% to agree with two or more of the five positions. How many neuroscientists hold both views cannot be read from the marginals. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-2#H1;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-2#H2. *Limits:* Uses only marginals; K03's cross-tabulations (SI) were not read. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-16.
- **TD-SOUL-06** [E] In open-ended answers from 387 science-oriented Finns, 76% mentioned some kind of annihilation after death and 35% expressed some kind of continuity, mostly nonreligious (social bonds, nature); secular continuity was mentioned more by unbelievers, while theists relied mainly on afterlife beliefs. *Sources:* K02. *Limits:* Online sample recruited through science-promoting organisations; single-country; categories overlap. *Deepens:* R3:C1-R3-02;R3:C0v2-R3-15.

- **TD-SOUL-12** [E] In SIM-SOUL-B, suppose implicit and explicit answers reflect one latent dualism belief. Then explicit rejecters show a NEGATIVE mean implicit score (-0.32), and a significant positive score in 0% of studies (pre-registered H1 of at least 50%: not supported). It cannot be a hidden copy of the explicit belief. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-3#R1. *Limits:* Single-factor linear model with illustrative parameters. - **TD-SOUL-13** [E] In a longitudinal study of 85 terminally ill patients in three Brazilian palliative care units, 34% reported end-of-life experiences (ELEs). The median time from first ELE to death was 29.5 days. Nearly half (49%) of the ELEs occurred during sleep, 80% were perceived as real and 52% involved deceased loved ones. 36% were described as comforting and 27% as distressing, and 78% were reported by non-delirious patients. *Sources:* K16. *Limits:* Small single-country sample. 65% believed in an afterlife. Only 41% were followed to death. The experiences are described; nothing about their source or about survival of death follows. *Deepens:* R3:C1-R3-02;R6.
- **TD-SOUL-14** [E] The 95% Wilson interval for K16's 29 of 85 is 24.9% to 44.7%. In SIM-SOUL-B's illustrative follow-up model, following only 41% of patients to death lowered observed prevalence to 0.86 of the true value (pre-registered H1 of below 0.8: not supported). Incomplete follow-up biases ELE prevalence downward, but only modestly under these assumptions. The sampling interval is the larger source of uncertainty. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-4#H1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-4#R1;K16. *Limits:* True prevalence, survival and observation fractions are illustrative. *Deepens:* TB-SOUL-13.
- **TD-SOUL-15** [E] Treger, Benau and Timko (2023) ran five studies with standard mortality-salience procedures. They could not replicate the basic worldview-defence pattern of Terror Management Theory, and pooled effect sizes did not differ significantly from zero. *Sources:* K17. *Limits:* Five samples from one team. The authors discuss possible methodological reasons. Not a test of every TMT prediction. *Deepens:* R3;R6.
- **TD-SOUL-16** [E] A meta-analysis of 64 effect sizes for the mortality salience x norm salience interaction found g = 0.34 (95% CI 0.26-0.41). Bias-adjustment methods indicated publication bias and/or researcher degrees of freedom and gave smaller estimates, in several cases nonsignificant (corrected g from -0.36 to 0.15). *Sources:* K18. *Limits:* Concerns one TMT moderator hypothesis. Bias-correction methods disagree with each other. *Deepens:* R3;R6.
- **TD-SOUL-17** [E] In SIM-SOUL-B, a pooled null from five studies of 50 per group has power .97 against K18's uncorrected g = 0.34 but only .39 against g = 0.15. A K17-style null is therefore informative against the uncorrected effect, not against the bias-corrected range. Separately, publishing non-significant results with probability .15 turned a true mean of 0 into a naive pooled estimate of only 0.10 (pre-registered H1 of at least 0.30: not supported). In this toy, selective publication alone did not reproduce an estimate as large as 0.34. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-5#H1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-5#H2;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-SOUL-B/iter-6#H1. *Limits:* Illustrative n. - **TD-SOUL-23** [E] In SIM-SOUL-C, K38's design is weak in both directions. At n = 15 and p < .001, a large true effect (d = 0.8) is detected at a voxel with power 0.31 (H1 supported), so 'no activation in limbic areas' is weak evidence of absence. Doubling to n = 30 raises power to 0.82 (pre-registered H1 of below 0.5: not supported). In pure-noise data with the same p < .001 and 10-voxel rule, 92.5% of simulated datasets showed at least one false 'activation' cluster, with 2.0 to 2.5 per dataset on average depending on smoothness (H1 supported). K38's positive and negative findings both need replication with corrected thresholds. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-C/iter-1#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-1#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-1#R1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-2#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-2#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-2#R1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-6#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-6#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-6#R1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-7#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-7#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-7#R1. *Limits:* Small synthetic grid (fewer voxels than a brain, which would add false clusters) and Gaussian smoothness. - **TD-SOUL-25** [E] In SIM-SOUL-C, K46's 4 of 23 versus 1 of 25 gives Fisher p = .18. Even if those proportions were true, a design of that size detects the difference with power below .5 (H1 supported); about 100 per group would be needed for .85. With no true difference in any of six features, at least one feature appeared 15 or more points 'descriptively higher' in 54% of simulated studies (H1 supported). With one real 20-point difference, some feature looked 15 or more points higher in 80% of studies, but the real one did so in only 63% (H1 supported). Because a 'higher' feature appears in more than half of studies with no difference at all, K46's descriptive contrasts cannot separate real from chance differences. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-C/iter-3#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-3#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-3#R1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-4#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-4#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-4#R1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-9#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-9#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-9#R1. *Limits:* Treats observed proportions as true, features as independent and base rates as illustrative. *Deepens:* TC-SOUL-24.
- **TD-SOUL-26** [E] In SIM-SOUL-C, classifying 37% of respondents as NDE cases by a cut-off on a scale with reliability .8 made 18.8% of classified cases true non-cases (pre-registered H1 of at least 20%: not supported). With reliability .9 the share was 13.1% (H1 not supported). Cut-off classification adds modest but non-trivial misclassification, smaller than the threshold set in advance. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-C/iter-5#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-5#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-5#R1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-8#V1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-8#H1;SIM-SOUL-C/iter-8#R1. *Limits:* K46's scale reliability was not available in what was read; .8 and .9 are assumptions. *Deepens:* TC-SOUL-24.
- **TD-SOUL-27** [E] In K61's 24-country sample, people judged 31.8% of states to continue after death (16.1% of bodily and 47.4% of mental states). 61.0% judged at least one state to continue and 2.0% judged all six to continue. Only in China, India, Japan, Romania and Singapore was continuity of some states more likely than complete cessation. The most religious participants were 46.5% more likely to make continuity judgments than the least religious, and a religious framing raised continuity judgments by 9.8% relative to a medical one, with the direction holding in all countries but Morocco. *Sources:* K61. *Limits:* Preprint version. One vignette about a grandmother; online and student samples vary by country. *Deepens:* TD-SOUL-01;R3.
- **TD-SOUL-28** [E] In SIM-SOUL-D, if K61's six continuity judgments were independent, 91.4% of people would judge at least one state to continue, against the reported 61.0% (H1 supported); with item rates 20% lower it would still be 84.3%. A two-class model (a pure 'cessation' group plus a 'continuity' group answering items independently) predicts only 0.5% judging all six, against the reported 2.0% (H2 supported). Continuity judgments therefore cluster within persons: people tend to be broadly materialist or broadly continuist, not to answer item by item. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-D/iter-1#V1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-1#H1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-1#H2;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-1#R1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-7#V1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-7#H1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-7#H2;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-7#R1. *Limits:* Uses pooled sample rates and equal item rates within bodily and mental sets. *Deepens:* TD-SOUL-27.
- **TD-SOUL-29** [E] In SIM-SOUL-D, with K61's framing effect (logit mean 0.52, between-country SD 0.22), each country has under a 1% chance of a truly negative effect. Once illustrative country-level estimation error (SE 0.15) is added, at least one of 24 countries shows a negative estimate in 46% of samples (H1 supported), and 87% with SE 0.30. A single exception such as Morocco is expected from noise and heterogeneity and does not by itself mark a culturally distinct pattern. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-D/iter-2#V1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-2#H1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-2#R1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-8#V1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-8#H1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-8#R1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-12#V1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-12#H1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-12#R1. *Limits:* Estimation SEs are illustrative; K61's country estimates are partially pooled. *Deepens:* TD-SOUL-27.
- **TD-SOUL-30** [E] In a cross-sectional study of 1,007 people, those certain of some afterlife approved least of hypothetical mind-upload technology (mean 3.91), the uncertain were in between (4.21) and non-believers approved most (4.58). Existential 'mattering' correlated -.19 with approval; comprehension (-.09) and purpose (-.08) correlated less. The authors read this as people who believe in a soul or afterlife and feel their life matters being least likely to approve of 'digital immortality'. *Sources:* K55. *Limits:* Cross-sectional; one hypothetical technology; Finnish-led online sample. The text reports 'B mattering = 0.20' alongside t = -5.62, an apparent sign typo. *Deepens:* TD-SOUL-01;R3.
- **TD-SOUL-31** [E] Two checks on K55. (1) From the published correlations alone, a simultaneous regression gives standardised betas of 0.02 for comprehension and 0.02 for purpose, and -0.21 for mattering (H1 supported). K55's 'full mediation' is therefore what collinearity among the meaning subscales produces, not evidence of a mediating process. (2) The mattering slopes for the uncertain (-0.28) and the non-believers (-0.18) do not differ significantly (z = -0.85, p = .39; H1 supported), nor do uncertain and believers (z = -0.69, p = .49). The 'steepest slope for the uncertain' is not a demonstrated difference. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-D/iter-3#V1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-3#H1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-3#R1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-4#V1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-4#H1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-4#R1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-9#V1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-9#H1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-9#R1. *Limits:* Treats slopes as independent estimates; the paper's interaction test was not reproduced. *Deepens:* TD-SOUL-30.
- **TD-SOUL-33** [E] In SIM-SOUL-D, with 17 cases, 14 raters and a true reliability near K53's, the central 95% of alpha estimates spanned 0.23 to 0.59, a range of 0.36 (H1 supported). With 34 cases it was still 0.26 wide. K53's interval of 0.40-0.58 is narrower than case-level resampling implies, so the scale's reliability is less certain than reported. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-D/iter-5#V1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-5#H1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-5#R1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-10#V1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-10#H1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-10#R1. *Limits:* Latent model and cut points illustrative; K53's CI method not reproduced. *Deepens:* TD-SOUL-32.
- **TD-SOUL-35** [E] In SIM-SOUL-D, with illustrative stage probabilities (survival to interview, interviewability, OBE recall, a target in the room and in the reported view), an 80% chance of even one testable hidden-target case needs about 8,000 cardiac arrests (H1 supported). Under very optimistic stages it needs about 184 (pre-registered H1 of at least 1,000: not supported). Whether hidden-target designs are feasible depends almost entirely on these unknown rates. A null from a few hundred arrests is uninformative under central assumptions. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-D/iter-6#V1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-6#H1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-6#R1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-11#V1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-11#H1;SIM-SOUL-D/iter-11#R1. *Limits:* All stage values are illustrative, not from sources. *Deepens:* TD-SOUL-34.
- **TD-SOUL-03** [P] Among proposed proximate mechanisms of belief in souls, afterlife and spirits (mentalizing, dualistic reasoning, intuitive thinking), the link between mentalizing ability and supernatural belief 'appears robust' but with small effect sizes, and people with mentalizing difficulties can still hold such beliefs. *Sources:* K01. *Limits:* Small effects; correlational; reported via review. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-13;R2.
- **TD-SOUL-11** [P] In five experiments using a brief implicit association task, Berent and Sansiveri (2025) found that people associated the mind with ethereal properties ('airy') and the body with physical ones. This held even in participants who explicitly rejected dualism. The authors call dualism an implicit bias that persists despite explicit attitudes. *Sources:* K15. *Limits:* Implicit association measures have contested validity. The word-property design may tap semantic associations rather than belief. Western educated samples; one research group. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-16;TB-SOUL-01.
- **TD-SOUL-20** [P] A brief clinical summary in CMAJ (Delgado-Ron 2026) states that near-death experiences occur in 15% to 17% of critically ill hospitalised patients. It says they often reduce fear of death and increase prosocial behaviour, and that clinicians should not pathologise them. Some patients report dismissive responses from health care providers. *Sources:* K37. *Limits:* Secondary summary of a few studies, partly by researchers from one research tradition. The prevalence figure was not checked against its primary source. *Deepens:* TC-SOUL-13;R3.
- **TD-SOUL-22** [P] In a preliminary fMRI study, 15 people with documented life-threatening events and loss of consciousness relived their memories of 'recalled experiences of death'. Doing so activated autobiographical-memory and default-mode regions (precuneus, posterior cingulate, medial prefrontal cortex). No activation was found in primary sensorimotor or limbic areas. The authors read the pattern as vivid episodic memory, not like primary sensory hallucination. *Sources:* K38. *Limits:* 15 participants, no control memory condition reported in the abstract, whole-brain p < .001 uncorrected. The study concerns memories of the experience, not the experience itself. *Deepens:* TC-SOUL-13;R3;R5.
- **TD-SOUL-24** [P] In a Chinese online sample (63 participants: 23 self-reported NDEs, 25 NDE-like experiences, 15 below threshold), narratives shared common NDE features: out-of-body experience, bright light, emotional change. Culturally specific motifs (Guimen Pass, Black and White Impermanence, karmic judgement, reincarnation) were infrequent, appearing in 4 of 23 NDE and 1 of 25 NDE-like narratives. The authors call the findings preliminary and hypothesis-generating. *Sources:* K46. *Limits:* Retrospective self-report, unverified medical contexts and a convenience sample. Classification rests on a self-report scale cut-off. *Deepens:* TC-SOUL-13;TC-SOUL-03.
- **TD-SOUL-32** [P] Greyson and colleagues (2025) built a veridical Near-Death Experience Scale by Delphi consensus of 13 experts: eight criteria covering investigation timing, medical condition, third-person verification, and the number, type and quality of reported perceptions. Applied to 17 cases by 11 human and 3 LLM raters, agreement was moderate (Krippendorff's alpha 0.49, 0.40-0.58). Human and AI judges agreed in over 75% of cases only after adjacent evidence levels were merged (14 of 17). The scale grades the evidential strength of claimed veridical perceptions. It does not test whether any perception was veridical. *Sources:* K53. *Limits:* Developers' own validation; case selection not described as random; merged levels inflate agreement. *Deepens:* TC-SOUL-21;R3.
- **TD-SOUL-34** [P] Weiler, Pehlivanova and Cozzolino (2026) argue that laboratory hidden-target tests of veridical perception in out-of-body experiences have struggled to replicate case reports because they misalign with the OBE state. They name five constraints: the participant's state, the participant-researcher relationship, researcher intention, participant skill and the experience's intrinsic purpose. They propose adding rigorously vetted spontaneous cases while preserving falsifiability. *Sources:* K54. *Limits:* Perspective article; 'researcher intention' as a constraint is itself an untested and contested claim. *Deepens:* TC-SOUL-21;TD-SOUL-32.
- **TD-SOUL-09** [PS] Persistence of the person: psychological-continuity views distinguish psychological connectedness from continuity and face the fission problem; soul, organism and continuity answers remain competing philosophical positions, and the track records them without choosing one. *Sources:* U13;G03. *Limits:* Philosophical; not decidable by the data reviewed. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-13.
