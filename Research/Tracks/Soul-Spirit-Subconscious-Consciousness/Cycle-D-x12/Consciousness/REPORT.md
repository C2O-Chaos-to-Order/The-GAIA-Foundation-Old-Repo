## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **TD-CONS-01** [E] In Whyte, Hohwy & Smith's active-inference model of masking, late P3b-like potentials and increased PFC activity were produced by the working-memory demands of self-report and vanished without report, yet simulated PFC activity on visible trials still crossed the reportability threshold. On this model, no-report results do not necessarily contradict cognitive (global-workspace or higher-order) theories. *Sources:* K13. *Limits:* One computational model; shows compatibility, not that the theories are right. *Deepens:* R5:C1-R5-02;R5:C2-R5-03.
- **TD-CONS-02** [E] In SIM-CONS-A's minimal threshold toy (gating + report demand), removing report cut the visible-minus-invisible PFC contrast by 60% while 84% of visible trials still exceeded the reportability threshold. K13's qualitative pattern follows from these two assumptions, so the toy shows the logic is coherent but adds no evidence about brains. *Sources:* SIM-CONS-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-CONS-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-CONS-A/iter-1#H2. *Limits:* Assumption-driven by construction. *Deepens:* R5:C1-R5-02.
- **TD-CONS-04** [E] The Minimal Phenomenal Experience questionnaire (92 items; 1,403 usable of 3,627 responses from meditators, mostly Vipassana 43.9% and Zen 34.9%) yielded 12 factors explaining 44% of variance, including 'Pure Awareness in Dream and Sleep', 'Luminosity', 'Wakeful Presence' and 'Witness Consciousness'. *Sources:* K14. *Limits:* Self-selected meditators; exploratory factor names are interpretive. *Deepens:* R5:C3-R5-07;R5:C0v2-R5-04.
- **TD-CONS-05** [E] In SIM-CONS-A, 92 structureless items (n = 1,403) gave about 43 'factors' by the eigenvalue-above-1 rule but about 0 by parallel analysis; with 12 true factors parallel analysis recovered exactly 12 at loadings of .4 to .75, and '12 components explain 44%' corresponded to loadings near .6. K14's dimensionality is statistically plausible for data of its size; what the factors are experiences of is a separate question. *Sources:* SIM-CONS-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-CONS-A/iter-2#H1;SIM-CONS-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-CONS-A/iter-3#H1;SIM-CONS-A/iter-3#H2. *Limits:* Principal components on simulated normal items; K14 used factor analysis on real data (not re-run). *Deepens:* R5:C3-R5-07.
- **TD-CONS-08** [E] An individual-participant-data meta-analysis (56 studies, 1,248 patients with disorders of consciousness) found cognitive motor dissociation (volitional brain responses without behavioural response) less often after anoxic (OR 0.35) and cerebrovascular (OR 0.51) injury than after traumatic brain injury, and more often in MCS-minus than in UWS (OR 1.50). Time since injury and test modality were not independently associated. *Sources:* K26. *Limits:* Heterogeneous paradigms. QUADAS-2 shows some concerns for index tests and flow and timing. *Deepens:* R5;TB-CONS-01.
- **TD-CONS-11** [E] Assume prevalence .25, specificity .95 and sensitivity .5 (illustrative). A patient with a NEGATIVE covert-consciousness test is then still conscious with probability .15 (H1 supported). A negative test does not rule consciousness out, which is the probabilistic core of K30's presumption argument. Separately, K26's OR of 0.35 at a baseline detection rate of .25 corresponds to a rate of .10 and a risk ratio of .42, not .35 (H1 supported). *Sources:* SIM-CONS-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-1#R1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-2#H1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-2#R1. *Limits:* Assumed test characteristics; CMD is not identical to consciousness. *Deepens:* TB-CONS-08;TB-CONS-09;TB-CONS-10.
- **TD-CONS-13** [E] In a model where the stimulus category is coded on every trial and awareness is independent of that code, a decoder trained on seen trials classified unseen trials at 72% (signal 0.1) and 90% (signal 0.2; H1 supported). Decodability alone is therefore not a marker of conscious content, which supports the logical point in K27 without deciding the empirical dispute. *Sources:* SIM-CONS-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-3#R1. *Limits:* Toy decoder; independence of awareness assumed. *Deepens:* TB-CONS-12.
- **TD-CONS-15** [E] Sensitivity checks on Cycle A. With weak report demand and a higher reportability threshold, removing report reduced the PFC contrast by only 33% (H1: not supported), and only 65% of visible trials exceeded threshold (H2: not supported). The Cycle A no-report pattern depends on parameters. With 5-point skewed ordinal items, the Kaiser rule still retained about 44 spurious factors while parallel analysis retained none (supported). *Sources:* SIM-CONS-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-5#H1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-5#H2;SIM-CONS-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-6#H1. *Limits:* Toy models. *Deepens:* TB-CONS-02;TB-CONS-05.
- **TD-CONS-17** [E] In the preregistered adversarial collaboration testing IIT and GNWT (Cogitate Consortium 2025, Nature; n = 256; fMRI, MEG and intracranial EEG), information about conscious content was found in visual, ventrotemporal and inferior frontal cortex. The results fit some predictions of both theories while substantially challenging key tenets of each. For IIT, sustained synchronisation within posterior cortex was missing. For GNWT, there was a general lack of ignition at stimulus offset and limited prefrontal representation of some conscious dimensions. *Sources:* K42. *Limits:* One paradigm (suprathreshold visual stimuli, mostly task-irrelevant). Proponents dispute interpretations (K27 for GNWT). It tests theories' predictions, not consciousness itself. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-12;TC-CONS-07.
- **TD-CONS-19** [E] In SIM-CONS-C, a true theory tested on 3 critical predictions at power .8 fails at least one 49% of the time, and on 5 at power .9, 41% of the time (H1 supported both times). With a prior of .5 and false theories passing 30% of tests, one failure leaves the posterior at .67 (3 tests) or .92 (5 tests) (H2 supported). Two failures out of three drop it to .18 (pre-registered H2 of at least .5: not supported). This supports K41's point that one failure is weak evidence against a theory. It also shows that several central failures, as K42 reports for both theories, are substantial evidence against them under these assumptions. *Sources:* SIM-CONS-C/iter-1#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-1#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-1#H2;SIM-CONS-C/iter-1#R1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-5#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-5#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-5#H2;SIM-CONS-C/iter-5#R1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-8#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-8#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-8#H2;SIM-CONS-C/iter-8#R1. *Limits:* Cartoon: independent, equally weighted binary tests. K42 weighted predictions and they are not independent. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-17;TC-CONS-18.
- **TD-CONS-20** [E] A scoping review of 157 studies (1921-2024; 87,866 participants) found dream recall after anaesthesia typically 3%-8% in large clinical trials and often 40%-80% in experimental settings. Ketamine and propofol were most associated with recall, and dreams were mostly pleasant with everyday autobiographical content. Immediate interviews gave higher recall than delayed ones. Dream recall was not associated with intraoperative awareness. Anaesthesia dreaming is treated as disconnected consciousness, experience without environmental awareness, distinct from both unconsciousness and connected consciousness. *Sources:* K32. *Limits:* Scoping review with heterogeneous definitions and assessment methods. Neural evidence is limited. *Deepens:* TC-SUBC-20;TC-CONS-11.
- **TD-CONS-21** [E] Two checks on K32. (1) With awareness at 0.5% and recall at 6%, a study of 2,000 patients detects even a tripled awareness risk among dream recallers with power 0.24 (H1 supported). A pooled 10,000 patients gives power 0.74 (pre-registered H1 of below 0.5: not supported). 'Not associated' therefore rests on low-powered single studies unless data are pooled. (2) Taking K32's immediate (about 10%) and delayed (5.9%) recall at face value, interview timing explains only 7% of the clinical-experimental gap at the midpoints and 6% at the range ends (H1 supported both times). Other factors (drugs, suggestion, populations, methods) must carry most of it. *Sources:* SIM-CONS-C/iter-2#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-2#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-2#R1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-6#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-6#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-6#R1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-3#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-3#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-3#R1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-9#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-9#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-9#R1. *Limits:* Illustrative awareness rate and RR. The timing arithmetic combines figures from different studies and assumes one uniform effect. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-20.
- **TD-CONS-22** [E] Consciousness x Justice. In a vignette survey of 360 Chinese neurologists (78% response), 63% chose the correct diagnostic category for patients with disorders of consciousness or locked-in syndrome. 73% believed patients in unresponsive wakefulness could feel pain. Most preferred never to limit life-sustaining treatment (46.7%, 62.8% and 66.7% for UWS, MCS and LIS vignettes; chi-square 5.93, p = .052). Deciding without a surrogate was rated extremely challenging. The authors call for training and more long-term-care resources, so who is judged conscious bears directly on treatment ethics and resource allocation. *Sources:* K47. *Limits:* Vignettes rather than real patients, one country, and self-selected respondents. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-11;TC-CONS-16.
- **TD-CONS-25** [E] IIT's two 'silent neuron' predictions are that silencing all active neurons in the main complex does not abolish consciousness, and that disabling silent neurons can change experience. Bartlett (2022) had argued these are untestable, because evidence for either would imply the test conditions were not met. Ponce de Leon and Yoshimi (2026) argue that both can be tested and use Lakatos to explain why disputes between theories of consciousness are hard to resolve. This partly addresses the Cycle C gap on replies to K42, but it is a philosophical analysis, not an IIT proponents' reply to the adversarial collaboration. *Sources:* K58. *Limits:* Philosophy-of-science paper; no data. *Deepens:* TD-CONS-07;TC-CONS-19.
- **TD-CONS-26** [E] In SIM-CONS-D's cartoon of auxiliary assumptions, even if the condition needed for a fair test (for example, neurons silent yet able to fire) is verified only half the time, one failed prediction still lowers a theory's probability from 0.5 to 0.28 (pre-registered H1 that it stays at or above 0.4: not supported). It falls to 0.06 if the condition is verified 90% of the time, 0.17 if failures are rarer when the condition fails, and 0.14 from a prior of 0.3. Uncertainty about auxiliary conditions softens the force of a failure but does not neutralise it, against the idea that such predictions are immune to evidence. *Sources:* SIM-CONS-D/iter-2#V1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-2#H1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-2#R1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-7#V1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-7#H1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-7#R1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-8#V1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-8#H1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-8#R1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-12#V1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-12#H1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-12#R1. *Limits:* Two-state cartoon; illustrative probabilities. *Deepens:* TD-CONS-25.
- **TD-CONS-27** [E] A second adversarial-collaboration protocol (Gibbons et al. 2026) will test GNWT and IIT predictions on the location, timing and functional connectivity of neural correlates of consciousness in macaques and mice. It uses a go/no-go task that controls for report confounds, Neuropixels recordings, and causal manipulation of prefrontal cortex. Data collection continues until a Bayes factor of at least 10 favours either hypothesis, with at least 9 and at most 15 sessions per macaque. *Sources:* K59. *Limits:* Protocol only: predictions, not results. *Deepens:* TD-CONS-07;TC-CONS-19.
- **TD-CONS-30** [E] Two checks on K60. (1) Three correct guesses out of 36 are significant at .05 only if the chance of guessing all three fruits is below 2.3% (H1 supported). That holds for a pool of 8 or more equally likely fruits but not for 5 (p = .71). Whether 3/36 indicates implicit learning depends on an unreported chance rate. (2) The Wilson 95% interval for delta activity during arrest (3 of 9) is 12%-65%, 0.53 wide (H1 supported). For usable EEG (9 of 36) it is 14%-41%, and for other memories (3 of 35) 3%-22%, both narrower than 0.5 (not supported). The study shows feasibility, not prevalence. *Sources:* SIM-CONS-D/iter-3#V1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-3#H1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-3#R1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-4#V1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-4#H1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-4#R1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-9#V1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-9#H1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-9#R1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-10#V1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-10#H1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-10#R1. *Limits:* Pool sizes illustrative; real guesses are not uniformly likely. *Deepens:* TD-CONS-29.
- **TD-CONS-32** [E] An individual-participant-data meta-analysis of 56 studies (1,248 patients with disorders of consciousness, 44.5% in unresponsive wakefulness) found that cognitive motor dissociation, meaning volitional brain responses in patients who appear unresponsive, is common but varies with cause and clinical state. Detection was lower after anoxic (OR 0.35) and cerebrovascular (OR 0.51) injury than after traumatic injury, and higher in MCS- than in UWS (OR 1.50). It was not independently associated with time since injury, diagnostic modality or task paradigm. This partly addresses the Cycle C call for pooled data on covert consciousness. *Sources:* K64. *Limits:* Pooled from heterogeneous published studies; detection depends on test sensitivity; overall prevalence not extracted here. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-11;TC-CONS-24.
- **TD-CONS-03** [P] Integrated information theory predicts that silencing all active neurons in the main complex leaves consciousness present (silent-brain prediction) and that disabling silent neurons can change experience (disabled-neuron prediction). Against the objection that these are untestable, Ponce de Leon & Yoshimi (2026) argue both can be tested, while noting why such theoretical disputes are hard to resolve (Lakatos). *Sources:* K12. *Limits:* Philosophical analysis; no experiment run. *Deepens:* R5:C0v2-R5-08;R5:C2-R5-03.
- **TD-CONS-09** [P] A neuropalliative review reports evidence that up to one in four patients diagnosed with unresponsive wakefulness syndrome may have covert consciousness, and that cognitive motor dissociation is associated with better functional outcomes. *Sources:* K30. *Limits:* 'Up to' figure reported secondarily. Prevalence depends on the tests used (TB-CONS-08). *Deepens:* TB-CONS-08;R5.
- **TD-CONS-12** [P] Proponents of global neuronal workspace theory argue that several analyses in the IIT-GNWT adversarial collaboration support GNWT predictions, such as transient ignition. They also argue that posterior decoding, presented as evidence for IIT, could have arisen from purely non-conscious processes. *Sources:* K27. *Limits:* Partisan commentary. The original Nature article and the IIT replies were not read in this cycle. *Deepens:* TB-CONS-03;R5.
- **TD-CONS-14** [P] Consciousness and love: in a preregistered trial (n = 80), one brief mindfulness meditation did not change compassionate responses overall. However, a serial path ran through mindfulness state and decentration (K28). In SIM-CONS-B a small, fully mediated effect gave total-effect power of only .13 at n = 80, while the indirect path was jointly significant in 92% of studies. The 'no total effect, significant indirect path' pattern is what a small mediated effect predicts. Disclosure: this run's validation check V1 failed narrowly (null indirect rate .055 against a pre-registered limit of .05, within Monte Carlo error of .05), so the claim is labelled Plausible. *Sources:* K28;SIM-CONS-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-4#H1;SIM-CONS-B/iter-4#H2. *Limits:* Illustrative path sizes. Joint-significance test rather than bootstrap. Validation V1 Fail recorded as is (see VERIFICATION). *Deepens:* R5;R6;MR.
- **TD-CONS-18** [P] He (2025) argues that a theory must be testable, generating falsifiable hypotheses, but that treating single contrary results as falsification would be a mistake. She notes that Hebbian plasticity remains a cornerstone theory despite important exceptions. She also notes that a few dominant consciousness theories have become entrenched, with young researchers aligned to camps, and that adversarial collaborations were funded to pit them against each other. *Sources:* K41. *Limits:* Opinion piece. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-17.
- **TD-CONS-23** [P] Consciousness x Justice consistency check on K47. A chi-square test of 'never limit' against other answers, with about 120 neurologists per vignette and the reported 46.7%, 62.8% and 66.7%, has power 0.86 (pre-registered H1 of below 0.8: not supported), and 0.98 with 200 per group. Proportions that differ this much with these group sizes would usually give p well below .052. The reported chi-square of 5.93 therefore probably comes from a different table, such as all three answer options or fewer item responses. The track could not resolve this from the text read, so the p = .052 'no difference' should not be read as evidence that diagnosis does not affect treatment preferences. *Sources:* SIM-CONS-C/iter-4#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-4#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-4#R1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-7#V1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-7#H1;SIM-CONS-C/iter-7#R1. *Limits:* Equal groups of about 120 assumed. K47's exact test and table are not reproduced. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-22.
- **TD-CONS-28** [P] In SIM-CONS-D, under K59's sampling plan (BF >= 10, 9-15 sessions), a true null almost never reached BF >= 10 for the null: 99% of runs ended inconclusive (H1 supported), and so did 81% at d = 0.5. With up to 30 sessions, 98% of null runs were still inconclusive. With a weaker bound of BF >= 3, 66% of null runs ended favouring the null, but 5.4% ended with misleading evidence for an effect (validation V1 of at most 5%: Fail, recorded and not re-run; H1 not supported). With three critical tests at power 0.8, a true theory fails at least one 49% of the time, and one failure leaves its posterior at 0.67 (both supported). The macaque arm can confirm large predicted effects but is unlikely to deliver strong evidence for absent ones. *Sources:* SIM-CONS-D/iter-1#V1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-1#H1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-1#R1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-5#V1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-5#H1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-5#R1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-6#V1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-6#H1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-6#R1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-11#V1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-11#H1;SIM-CONS-D/iter-11#H2;SIM-CONS-D/iter-11#R1. *Limits:* One-sample test across independent sessions; illustrative d. The iter-5 V1 was a design-property check mislabelled as validation; it is reported as a Fail. *Deepens:* TD-CONS-27.
- **TD-CONS-29** [P] In a 10-hospital feasibility study of 36 patients undergoing deep hypothermic circulatory arrest, EEG was usable in only 9. Three of those showed delta waves during arrest, and the rest showed isoelectric patterns. None explicitly recalled three fruit names played during the procedure, but 3 of 36 'correctly guessed' them, which the authors read as possible implicit learning. 3 recalled other memories, including themes consistent with a recalled experience of death. The authors conclude that a spectrum of consciousness 'may be present' despite no visible signs. *Sources:* K60. *Limits:* Feasibility design; most EEG unusable; guessing chance not reported in the parts read. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-20;TC-SOUL-21.
- **TD-CONS-06** [PS] 'Pure awareness' (interpretive): contemplative traditions describe consciousness without ordinary content (for example turiya in Advaita, S31), and meditators' reports of such states have a measurable structure (K14). Whether awareness without content exists as more than a reported state is a philosophical question the track does not settle. *Sources:* S31;K14;S32;G03. *Limits:* Interpretive. *Deepens:* R5:C0v2-R5-12;R5:C3-R5-07.
- **TD-CONS-07** [PS] Boundary: no theory has solved consciousness. The preregistered adversarial collaboration between IIT and GNWT (K42) found results that fit some predictions of each theory and challenged key tenets of both. Track Cycles A-D sharpened methodological questions (report confounds, testability, decoding without awareness, how to score failed predictions) but decided none of the theories. *Sources:* S16;S19;G03;K42. *Limits:* Framework statement. *Deepens:* R5:C0v2-R5-12.
- **TD-CONS-10** [PS] Consciousness and justice: Hawkins (2026) argues that suffering in disorders of consciousness includes existential dimensions beyond pain, and that a presumption of consciousness should guide clinical care regardless of imaging findings. The track records this as an ethical position about the dignity and moral standing of possibly conscious patients, not as an empirical result. *Sources:* K30;G03. *Limits:* Normative argument; one author. *Deepens:* R5;R6;MR.
- **TD-CONS-16** [PS] Josipovic and Miskovic (2020) propose that consciousness-as-such is a non-conceptual, nondual awareness of a unique kind, not captured by the two-dimensional model of arousal plus phenomenal content. This contrasts with the minimal-phenomenal-experience framing of K14. The track records both as positions and links them to contemplative accounts (Report 3). *Sources:* K29;K14. *Limits:* Theoretical proposal. *Deepens:* TB-CONS-06;R3;R5.
- **TD-CONS-24** [PS] Consciousness x Justice (ethical position, not a finding). Some diagnosed unresponsive patients are covertly conscious (K26, K30), and many clinicians misclassify disorders of consciousness on vignettes (K47). Fairness to such patients therefore depends on careful assessment, care that does not assume their absence, and resources for long-term care. This extends the Master Report's care question. *Sources:* K47;K30;K26. *Limits:* Normative statement. The empirical inputs carry their own uncertainty. *Deepens:* TC-CONS-15;TC-CONS-22.
- **TD-CONS-31** [PS] Interpretation (not a finding): after four track cycles, consciousness science is converging on method (adversarial protocols, explicit testability analyses, report-free paradigms) faster than on any theory. In GAIA's framing this is the motherthread's honest edge: the track can say how a claim about consciousness would be tested or rejected, not what consciousness ultimately is. *Sources:* K58;K59;K42. *Limits:* Interpretive. *Deepens:* TD-CONS-07.
