## Reading of this cycle
Cycle D finally read the 'complete mystical experience' criterion (K49): at least 60% on all four MEQ30 subscales, with good agreement against the MEQ43 (kappa 0.80) but a reported d' that cannot be reproduced (TD-SPIRIT-26) and a noisy all-or-none boundary (TD-SPIRIT-27). Psychedelic trials are functionally unblinded (K50; K51 blinding index 0.88-1.00), and expectancy alone could produce effects of K51's size (TD-SPIRIT-30). Death anxiety falls after psychedelics in uncontrolled pre-post studies (K52), but the prediction interval spans zero and regression to the mean can mimic part of the effect (TD-SPIRIT-32). Transformative spiritual experiences predict later volunteering and some vulnerability markers (K62), with an E-value of 1.71 (TD-SPIRIT-34).

Changes against Cycle C: Cycle C claims are carried with IDs TD-* and a change note (`change_vs_prev_track_cycle`). TD-SOUL-01 is revised (Plausible -> Established, primary study K61 read); the boundary statements TD-SOUL-10 and TD-CONS-07 are extended to Cycle D.

- **TD-SPIRIT-01** [E] In English validation data (280 measurements, 145 healthy participants, four placebo-controlled psilocybin studies), the four-factor Mystical Experience Questionnaire (MEQ30) and the six-factor MEQ40 showed acceptable to good confirmatory fit, and six of the eight Psychedelic Experience Scale subscales showed high internal consistency. *Sources:* K04. *Limits:* Drug-context sample; psychometric fit says the questionnaire measures reports consistently, not what the experiences are of. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-06;R4:C0v2-R4-11.
- **TD-SPIRIT-02** [E] Graziosi et al. (2026) did not reproduce reported effects of experimentally induced awe on agency detection, prosocial behaviour or well-being across online, laboratory and virtual-reality inductions, including three online direct replications with 2.5 times the original sample sizes. *Sources:* K05. *Limits:* Mostly online (MTurk) samples; induction strength can differ from originals. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-08.
- **TD-SPIRIT-03** [E] In SIM-SPIRIT-A (illustrative originals of 50 per group, published only if p < .05), published effects averaged d = 0.48 when the true effect was zero; a replication at 2.5x n has power .35 for a true d = 0.2, so three failed replications still have probability .27 (pre-registered H1 of < .10: not supported). A run of failures like K05's argues against medium effects (d = 0.3: .04) more than against small ones. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-A/iter-1#H2. *Limits:* Illustrative n0; equal-variance normal model. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-08.
- **TD-SPIRIT-05** [E] In SIM-SPIRIT-A, a binary 'all four subscales above a cut' classification was unstable even with subscale reliability .90: only 72% of people classified at test were classified again at retest (kappa .67; pre-registered H1 of at least 80%: not supported). Binary 'complete experience' counts carry more measurement noise than the subscale reliabilities suggest. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-A/iter-2#H1. *Limits:* Illustrative cut (60th percentile on each subscale), not the published criterion, which was not read. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-11.
- **TD-SPIRIT-06** [E] In SIM-SPIRIT-A, with no true effect, testing four outcome families without correction yields at least one significant result in 18% (uncorrelated) to 14% (r = .6) of simulated two-group studies. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-A/iter-3#H1. *Limits:* Generic; not an audit of any original study. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-08.
- **TD-SPIRIT-09** [E] A meta-analysis of 55 longitudinal studies found a protective association of spirituality with harmful or hazardous alcohol and other drug use (RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.84-0.91; 13% risk reduction), reaching 18% for people attending religious services more than weekly. *Sources:* K19. *Limits:* Observational. The authors call for trials and longitudinal designs to explore causality. Mostly US samples. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4;R6.
- **TD-SPIRIT-10** [E] The E-value for K19's RR of 0.87 is 1.56, and 1.43 for the confidence limit. An unmeasured confounder associated with both spirituality and drug use by risk ratios of about 1.6 could fully explain the association. K19's result is a robust association, not causal evidence. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-1#V2;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-1#R1. *Limits:* The E-value is a sensitivity summary and does not show that such a confounder exists. *Deepens:* TB-SPIRIT-09.
- **TD-SPIRIT-11** [E] A meta-analysis of 8 studies found reductions in death anxiety after psychedelic administration (d = 0.70, 95% CI 0.42-0.97), stronger in clinical settings. A meta-regression associated mystical experiences with larger reductions (B = 4.90, p = 0.050). The authors list few studies, potential publication bias and high heterogeneity as limitations. *Sources:* K20. *Limits:* Includes uncontrolled designs. Expectancy and unblinding are not addressed here. The moderator result is borderline. *Deepens:* R4;TB-SPIRIT-01.
- **TD-SPIRIT-12** [E] With 8 studies, DerSimonian-Laird 95% CIs covered the truth in 90.7% of cases at tau = 0.4 (pre-registered H1 of below 90%: not supported; Hartung-Knapp about 95%). For a null moderator, a fixed-effect z-test gave false positives in 14% of simulations at tau = 0.2 and 35% at tau = 0.4, while a heterogeneity-aware t-test stayed near 5%. A p = .050 moderator result from 8 heterogeneous studies is fragile unless a heterogeneity-aware test was used. K20's estimator was not checked. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-2#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-5#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-5#R1. *Limits:* Illustrative study sizes. Normal approximations. *Deepens:* TB-SPIRIT-11.
- **TD-SPIRIT-13** [E] Sensitivity checks on Cycle A. With a stricter cut (80th percentile on all four subscales) only 69% of those classified as having a 'complete' experience were classified again at retest (omega .9, kappa .65; H1 of at least 80%: not supported). A Holm correction across four outcome families held family-wise error at 5% but cut power for one real effect from .78 to .59 (H1 of at most 10 points lost: not supported). Binary mystical-experience labels stay noisy, and multiplicity control is costly at typical sample sizes. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-3#R1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-4#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-4#R1. *Limits:* Illustrative cut and sample sizes. *Deepens:* TB-SPIRIT-05;TB-SPIRIT-06.
- **TD-SPIRIT-15** [E] With six focus groups, a theme present in 10% of groups goes entirely unobserved with probability .53, and one present in 30% with probability .12 (H1 supported). Absence of a theme in a six-group study is weak evidence that it is absent from the communities. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-6#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-B/iter-6#R1. *Limits:* Independent-groups idealisation. *Deepens:* TB-SPIRIT-14.
- **TD-SPIRIT-17** [E] A review by Atiq et al. (2025) notes that clinical trials of classic psychedelics find strong correlations between acute subjective effects, sometimes mystical, and lasting therapeutic outcomes. It also notes that pharmacology and medicinal-chemistry studies have cast doubt on whether those effects are necessary, since non-hallucinogenic compounds show therapeutic-like effects in preclinical models. Whether the experience can be separated from the benefit is open. *Sources:* K39. *Limits:* Narrative review. The preclinical evidence comes from animal models. No new data. *Deepens:* TC-SPIRIT-11;TC-SPIRIT-12.
- **TD-SPIRIT-18** [E] In SIM-SPIRIT-C, a common cause of acute experience and outcome (for example drug response or expectancy), with no causal effect of the experience itself, produced an experience-outcome correlation of 0.39 (H1 supported). That was significant in 99% of trials with n = 100, and in 100% with n = 300. Adding a real causal effect of 0.2 raised it to 0.54. Larger trials do not remove the confound. Correlations like those K39 describes cannot show that the mystical experience causes the benefit; designs that manipulate the experience are needed. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-1#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-1#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-1#R1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-5#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-5#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-5#R1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-9#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-9#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-9#R1. *Limits:* Linear-normal model with illustrative path values. *Deepens:* TC-SPIRIT-17.
- **TD-SPIRIT-19** [E] A dose-response meta-analysis of LSD's subjective effects (5D-ASC, 11-ASC and MEQ30), covering doses up to 200 ug, found that most scales rise in a sigmoid-like way and plateau at around 100 ug. Effects were strongest for perception changes and positively experienced ego dissolution. Considerable variability across studies points to non-pharmacological factors (set and setting). *Sources:* K40. *Limits:* Few studies at each dose. Doses above 200 ug were not covered. Study-level data only. *Deepens:* TC-SPIRIT-02;TC-SPIRIT-11.
- **TD-SPIRIT-20** [E] In SIM-SPIRIT-C's illustrative Emax curve, effects still rise 17.6% from 100 to 200 ug. A comparison of 6 study means at 100 ug with 3 at 200 ug (between-study SD 15) failed to detect the rise in 81.6% of simulated literatures (H1 supported), and still in 59.9% with 12 and 6 studies (H1 supported). A 'plateau' in a sparse, variable literature may partly reflect limited precision. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-4#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-4#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-4#R1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-8#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-8#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-8#R1. *Limits:* Illustrative curve, noise and study counts. K40's spline meta-regression was not re-run. *Deepens:* TC-SPIRIT-19.
- **TD-SPIRIT-21** [E] In the English validation of the Psychedelic Experience Scale (Stocker et al. 2026, with Barrett and Richards), five of six MEQ40 subscales had high internal consistency (alpha and omega above .90) and the sixth was good. The non-mystical visual-experience subscale was only acceptable (.70). The MEQ30 and MEQ40 models fit acceptably to well, and English scale means were higher than German ones. *Sources:* K43. *Limits:* One validation sample of controlled psilocybin and LSD sessions. Internal consistency does not establish validity. The 'complete mystical experience' criterion paper itself was still not read. *Deepens:* TC-SPIRIT-02;TC-SPIRIT-13.
- **TD-SPIRIT-22** [E] Under classical test theory, reliabilities of .70 and .90 lower a true correlation of .50 to about .40 (H1 supported), and reliabilities of .6 and .7 lower .30 to .19 (H1 supported). Correlations involving K43's visual subscale are therefore understated relative to the mystical subscales. If the measures in K45 had reliability .8, its spirituality-mindfulness r of .198 would correspond to about .25 (an assumption, reported only). *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-3#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-3#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-3#R1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-7#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-7#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-7#R1. *Limits:* Treats alpha as reliability. The reliabilities for K45's measures are assumed. *Deepens:* TC-SPIRIT-21;TC-SPIRIT-23.
- **TD-SPIRIT-23** [E] In a longitudinal study of people planning to take a psychedelic (654 at baseline, 379 after the experience), baseline wellbeing predicted more mystical and fewer challenging experiences. Baseline depression and anxiety predicted more challenging but not more mystical experiences. Mindfulness and spirituality were associated with mystical experiences, and mystical and challenging experiences were weakly but positively correlated. The authors name attrition bias as a limitation. *Sources:* K45. *Limits:* Self-selected naturalistic users, self-report and attrition from 654 to 379. The associations are observational. *Deepens:* TC-SPIRIT-11;TC-SPIRIT-04.
- **TD-SPIRIT-24** [E] In SIM-SPIRIT-C, with K45's retention (58%) and a true baseline-outcome correlation of .20, dropout that depended on the outcome shifted the completer correlation by only -0.017 when moderate (m = 1) and by -0.036 when strong (m = 2). Neither reached the pre-registered 0.05 (H1 not supported in both runs). Under this selection model, K45's attrition attenuates correlations slightly but does not plausibly create or erase them. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-2#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-2#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-2#R1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-6#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-6#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-C/iter-6#R1. *Limits:* Selection only on the outcome, with one logistic form. Selection on other variables or on interactions could matter more. *Deepens:* TC-SPIRIT-23.
- **TD-SPIRIT-25** [E] The 'complete mystical experience' criterion (read this cycle, closing an open item since Cycle B): Barrett, Johnson and Griffiths (2015) pooled five psilocybin experiments (n = 184). An experience counts as 'complete' if it scores at least 60% of the maximum on each of the four MEQ30 subscales, adapted from the MEQ43 six-scale rule. Against the MEQ43 classification (Table 2: 80, 14, 4, 86), the MEQ30 rule had hit rate 0.95, false-alarm rate 0.14 and proportion correct 90.2%. The subscales were highly reliable (alpha 0.97, 0.92, 0.86, 0.90), and MEQ30 scores predicted persisting effects controlling for drug intensity. *Sources:* K49. *Limits:* Validation against an older version of the same instrument by its developers; healthy-volunteer laboratory samples. *Deepens:* TC-SPIRIT-19;TC-SPIRIT-22.
- **TD-SPIRIT-26** [E] Recomputing from K49's Table 2 reproduces the hit rate, false-alarm rate and proportion correct exactly, and gives Cohen's kappa 0.80. But d' is 2.75 by the standard formula (2.68 with a log-linear correction), not the reported 3.464 (H1 supported: no standard convention comes within 0.1). Agreement is good, but the reported sensitivity appears overstated or computed by an unstated method. This is recorded as an unresolved reporting question. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-1#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-1#V2;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-1#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-1#R1. *Limits:* K49 does not state its d' formula. *Deepens:* TD-SPIRIT-25.
- **TD-SPIRIT-28** [E] In a randomised trial of a single psilocybin dose versus niacin in 35 adults with recurrent major depression, the MADRS change on day 8 favoured psilocybin by -7.27 points (95% CI -12.89 to -1.65). The difference persisted to day 42 but not to day 365 (-3.68, p = .20). Blinding failed. On day 365, 16 of 17 psilocybin and 16 of 16 niacin participants guessed their allocation correctly, and the blinded rater on day 8 guessed 12 of 17 and 15 of 17. *Sources:* K51. *Limits:* Small single-site trial; guesses collected at day 365; two participants had persistent severe anxiety. *Deepens:* TC-SPIRIT-18.
- **TD-SPIRIT-29** [E] Krsak and colleagues (2026) argue that the main challenge of psychedelic trials is that assignment cannot be concealed. All approaches tried so far suffered complete or significant functional unblinding, which mixes the drug effect with positive expectancy in the active arm and negative expectancy in the placebo arm. This is especially problematic if unblinding is part of the mechanism itself. They cite a ketamine trial under surgical anaesthesia that could not be distinguished from placebo. *Sources:* K50. *Limits:* Narrative review; the anaesthesia-masked ketamine trial is reported secondhand. *Deepens:* TC-SPIRIT-18;TD-SPIRIT-28.
- **TD-SPIRIT-30** [E] Two checks on K51 and K50. (1) Bang's blinding index for participants' day-365 guesses was 0.88 (psilocybin) and 1.00 (niacin), essentially complete unblinding (H1 supported). For the blinded rater on day 8 it was 0.41 and 0.76 (pre-registered H1 of at least 0.8 in every arm: not supported), so partial unblinding reached the outcome assessor. (2) With K51's sample size and outcome SD, an expectancy shift of +/-3.5 MADRS points and no drug effect gives p < .05 in 66% of trials (H1 supported), and +/-2 points in 27% (not supported). An effect of K51's size could arise largely from expectancy unless expectancy is measured and modelled. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-4#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-4#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-4#R1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-8#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-8#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-8#R1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-5#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-5#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-5#R1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-9#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-9#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-9#R1. *Limits:* Expectancy sizes are illustrative; K51 measured expectancy before dosing but this was not re-analysed. *Deepens:* TD-SPIRIT-28;TD-SPIRIT-29.
- **TD-SPIRIT-31** [E] A meta-analysis of 8 studies (16 effect sizes, 3,050 participants) found that death anxiety fell after psychedelic experiences, d = 0.70 (95% CI 0.42-0.97), with heterogeneity I^2 = 96.3%. Effects were larger in clinical settings, and mystical experience was associated with larger reductions (B = 4.90, p = .050). All studies were within-subject pre-post designs. Egger's test did not indicate publication bias, though the funnel plot showed mild asymmetry. *Sources:* K52. *Limits:* No controlled designs; four survey studies supply most participants; 16 effects from 8 studies are not independent. *Deepens:* TC-SOUL-01;TC-SPIRIT-18.
- **TD-SPIRIT-32** [E] Two checks on K52. (1) Its pooled d, CI and I^2 imply a between-study SD of about 0.55 and a 95% prediction interval of -0.52 to 1.92 (H1 supported). A new setting could plausibly show no reduction. (2) Selecting the top 30% on baseline death anxiety with test-retest r = 0.7 produces an apparent pre-post reduction of 0.35 SD with no treatment at all (H1 supported). Under milder selection (top 50%, r = 0.85) it is 0.12 SD (not supported). Uncontrolled pre-post designs can manufacture part of a d of 0.70. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-6#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-6#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-6#R1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-7#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-7#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-7#R1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-11#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-11#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-11#R1. *Limits:* Equal within-study variances; selection and reliability illustrative. *Deepens:* TD-SPIRIT-31.
- **TD-SPIRIT-33** [E] In the US Add Health cohort (n = 10,529), reporting a transformative religious or spiritual experience in late adolescence was followed in early adulthood by more volunteering (RR 1.21, 1.12-1.30; E-value 1.71; the most robust outcome) and, less robustly, by more voting, loneliness (beta 0.13) and PTSD diagnosis (OR 1.54). Antecedents included adverse childhood environments and higher religious involvement. The authors describe a 'double-edged' pattern of vulnerability and connection. *Sources:* K62. *Limits:* Observational; single-item exposure; several weaker outcomes did not survive conservative adjustment. *Deepens:* TC-SPIRIT-14;R4.
- **TD-SPIRIT-34** [E] K62's E-values recompute correctly: 1.71 for RR 1.21 and 1.49 for its lower limit (V3 Pass). Because the E-value is below 2 (H1 supported), an unmeasured confounder associated with both the experience and volunteering by a risk ratio of about 1.7 could explain the association away. That is modest for social behaviour, so the spiritual-experience-to-prosociality link is suggestive, not causal. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-3#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-3#V2;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-3#V3;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-3#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-3#R1. *Limits:* E-value assumes a single confounder and the risk-ratio scale. *Deepens:* TD-SPIRIT-33.
- **TD-SPIRIT-04** [P] Keltner & Monroy (2026) review evidence that awe is a distinct emotion expressed in mouth and gaze activity, vocal bursts and upward head orientation with 'some degree of universality' (in one paradigm, five cultures overlapped in 63% of awe-expression elements) and elaborated culturally in choral music, dance and art of the sublime. *Sources:* K06. *Limits:* Narrative review by proponents of the basic-emotion approach. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-06.
- **TD-SPIRIT-14** [P] In six focus groups across three marginalised South African communities, participants described radical healing through three interwoven themes: faith and spirituality (churches, religious gatherings), collective and action-oriented healing (activism), and micro, everyday acts. The authors frame healing as an embodied, collective, action-oriented praxis that resists structures of oppression, linking spirituality with justice in community practice. *Sources:* K21. *Limits:* Qualitative and decolonial-participatory, so not designed for generalisation. Describes participants' accounts. *Deepens:* R4;R6.
- **TD-SPIRIT-27** [P] In SIM-SPIRIT-D, with K49's subscale reliabilities and an illustrative score distribution, two parallel measurements of the same experience disagreed on 'complete' for 9.6% of experiences (pre-registered H1 of at least 10%: not supported), and about 21% of experiences classed 'complete' once would not be classed so again. With a weaker general factor it was 5.6% (not supported), and with reliabilities of 0.80 it was 13% (supported). An all-subscales cut-off amplifies measurement error near the threshold, so a 'complete' label is a noisy dichotomy of a continuous score. *Sources:* SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-2#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-2#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-2#R1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-10#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-10#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-10#R1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-12#V1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-12#H1;SIM-SPIRIT-D/iter-12#R1. *Limits:* Score distribution, loading and normality are illustrative. *Deepens:* TD-SPIRIT-25.
- **TD-SPIRIT-07** [PS] Transcendence (descriptive): traditions describe mystical experience as contact with or realisation of a reality beyond ordinary experience (S15, which excludes paranormal experiences). Questionnaires such as the MEQ measure how people report such experiences; whether the experienced reality exists is a philosophical and spiritual question the measurements do not settle. *Sources:* S15;K04;G03. *Limits:* Interpretive. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-06;R4:C0v2-R4-15.
- **TD-SPIRIT-16** [PS] Spirit and justice: community accounts describe spirituality as a collective, embodied practice of resisting oppression (K21). The track records this as a description of how participants understand spirit. That spirituality produces justice is not a finding. GAIA's motherthread framing (PS) may hold the two together without claiming causation. *Sources:* K21;G03. *Limits:* Interpretive. *Deepens:* R4;R6.
- **TD-SPIRIT-08** [H] Hypothesis: self-transcendent emotions such as awe are one pathway linking spirituality to well-being (Report 4). After K05's failed replications, this remains a hypothesis without robust experimental support; it would be tested by preregistered, adequately powered induction studies with manipulation checks. *Sources:* K05;K06;S11. *Limits:* Not tested here. *Deepens:* R4:C0v2-R4-08;R4:C0v2-R4-14.
