Labels: **E** Established · **P** Plausible · **PS** Philosophical/Spiritual · **H** Hypothetical. GAIA is the motherthread (PS framing) and is kept separate from the science.

## Reading of this cycle
Cycle D deepened Mind x Justice. Implicit race bias can operate without awareness; its proponents argue that controllability implies responsibility (K56). Small individual-level biases can yield 1.2-1.6-fold disparities in selective decisions (TD-SUBC-37), and K56's state-level r = 0.64 is compatible with an individual r near 0.05 (TD-SUBC-38). A registered report found that lowering free-will belief changed neither control nor automaticity (K63), though at its size small effects remain possible (TD-SUBC-35). With K35 and K57, beliefs about agency appear to shape punishment attitudes more than behaviour (TD-SUBC-39, Plausible).

Changes against Cycle C: Cycle C claims are carried with IDs TD-* and a change note (`change_vs_prev_track_cycle`). TD-SOUL-01 is revised (Plausible -> Established, primary study K61 read); the boundary statements TD-SOUL-10 and TD-CONS-07 are extended to Cycle D.

## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **TD-SUBC-01** [E] A systematic review of 73 contextual-cuing studies (181 awareness tests) found a meta-analytic awareness effect of dz = 0.31, so learning in these 'implicit' tasks was conscious; with a median N of 16, awareness tests had power of about .21, matching the 21.5% of significant awareness results. *Sources:* K09. *Limits:* One paradigm (contextual cuing); generalisation to other implicit-learning paradigms is argued, not shown. *Deepens:* R2;R5:C0v2-R5-03.
- **TD-SUBC-02** [E] SIM-SUBC-A reproduced K09's power argument: analytic power .213 and simulated .211 at n = 16 and dz = .31, 84 participants for 80% power. 79% of such awareness tests come out non-significant although awareness is real by construction, while pooling 181 tests detected awareness in every simulated literature. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-1#V2;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-1#V3;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-1#H2. *Limits:* Normal difference scores. *Deepens:* R2.
- **TD-SUBC-03** [E] Doyen et al. (2012) did not replicate the 'elderly prime slows walking' effect with automated timing and 120 participants; a walking-speed effect appeared only when experimenters believed participants would walk slower, and debriefing suggested awareness of the primes. *Sources:* K08. *Limits:* Two experiments; one classic paradigm. *Deepens:* R2.
- **TD-SUBC-04** [E] Redo with the Cycle A design flaw fixed (SIM-SUBC-B iteration 1, non-negative latent knowledge): participants selected post hoc as 'unaware' because their awareness score fell below chance still had a mean true knowledge of 0.24, about half the population mean of 0.5. So below-chance awareness scores do not show absence of knowledge. However, priming carried entirely by that knowledge reached significance in only 9.9% of simulated studies of N = 100 (pre-registered H1 of at least 50%: not supported). In this model the misclassification is real but usually too small to show up as 'unconscious priming'. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-1#V2;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-1#R1;K25. *Limits:* Toy model: one awareness measure with reliability .5, gamma-distributed knowledge and linear priming. The rate depends on N, reliability and b. K25's reliability-corrected regression methods are cited for context only and were not re-run. *Deepens:* R2.
- **TD-SUBC-06** [E] In SIM-SUBC-A's toy of K10's proposal (dreams sample 3 of 30 reactivated memories; dreaming itself does nothing), dreamed memories were recalled 0.36 SD better than all other memories, significant in 75% of simulated studies (pre-registered H1 of at least 80%: not supported, though the association is large), and no better than reactivated-but-not-dreamed memories (4.9%). Dream-memory associations need the reactivated-not-dreamed comparison before dreams are credited. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-3#H1;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-3#H2. *Limits:* Toy; uniform dream sampling assumed. *Deepens:* R2.
- **TD-SUBC-07** [E] Among 178 UK mental-health professionals, most indicated some belief in unconscious repression (over 78%) and dissociative amnesia (over 84%), with higher agreement among EMDR and other trauma-focused practitioners; the authors link such beliefs to the risk of false or non-experienced memories in therapy. *Sources:* K11. *Limits:* Describes beliefs, which are not evidence that repression occurs; convenience sample. *Deepens:* R2.
- **TD-SUBC-09** [E] Yogacara Buddhism (descriptive): Vasubandhu and his followers posit a receptacle or repository consciousness (alaya-vijnana), 'a subliminal form of cognitive awareness' underlying the propensities, habits and tendencies that direct action. *Sources:* S32. *Limits:* Describes a doctrine; not an empirical claim about the brain. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-09;R5.
- **TD-SUBC-10** [E] Advaita Vedanta (descriptive): waking (jagrat), dreaming (svapna) and deep sleep (susupti) point to a fourth state, turiya (pure consciousness), to be realised as the true self. Deep sleep is described as partial experience of its bliss. *Sources:* S31. *Limits:* Doctrine described, not tested. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-08;R5.
- **TD-SUBC-11** [E] History of the idea (descriptive): Leibniz allowed infinitely many degrees of consciousness and unconscious 'petites perceptions', distinguishing perception from apperception, an early philosophical form of an unconscious mind long before Freud. *Sources:* S16. *Limits:* Historical description. *Deepens:* R5:C0v2-R5-01.
- **TD-SUBC-13** [E] Across three experiments with 822 participants, Steindorf, Rummel and Boywitt (2021) found an unconscious thought effect only in the first two experiments. Less deliberation went with better decisions, but the benefit disappeared when thoughts were probed during the interval. The authors note that samples should have been larger to detect small effects. *Sources:* K22. *Limits:* One task (apartment evaluation). Thought probes may change the process they measure. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-01;R2.
- **TD-SUBC-14** [E] With an illustrative 90 per condition, a true d = 0.2 gives power .27 per experiment. Two or more of three experiments come out significant with probability .17 (pre-registered H1 of at least .5: not supported), and a mixed pattern with probability .59. Under d = 0 the corresponding figures are .007 and .14. K22's two-of-three pattern is far more likely under a small real effect than under none, though it cannot pin down the size. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-4#H1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-4#R1. *Limits:* Illustrative n; independent experiments. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-13.
- **TD-SUBC-15** [E] A three-level meta-analysis (62 studies, 182 effect sizes, 4,772 participants) of affective priming by briefly presented (150 ms or less) facial expressions found g = 0.32 (95% PI -0.23 to 0.88). Supraliminal primes produced larger effects than subliminal ones, but subliminal presentations still yielded reliable priming. *Sources:* K23. *Limits:* Includes the authors' own bias assessment (not re-run here). The prediction interval spans zero. Awareness checks vary across studies (see TB-SUBC-01). *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-03;R2.
- **TD-SUBC-16** [E] K23's interval implies a between-setting SD of about 0.28 and about 12.5% of settings with a true affective-priming effect of zero or below (H1 supported). In a selection toy, a true mean of 0.10 gave a naive pooled estimate of 0.26 (pre-registered H1 of at least 0.30: not supported). Selection can inflate such estimates substantially but did not fully reproduce 0.32 here. Subliminal affective priming is best described as small and setting-dependent. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-2#H1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-2#R1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-6#H1. *Limits:* Normal approximation to a three-level model; illustrative selection rule. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-15.
- **TD-SUBC-17** [E] Otgaar and colleagues (2019) report that belief in repressed memories remains common: 58% in one cited survey of clinicians, with signs of an increase since the 1990s. They argue that 'dissociative amnesia' functions as a substitute term for repression and that some therapeutic techniques risk producing false memories. The psychodynamic concept of a repressed unconscious remains scientifically contested. *Sources:* K24. *Limits:* The authors are one side of the 'memory wars'. Survey figures are cited from other studies. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-07;R2.
- **TD-SUBC-18** [E] Jimenez and colleagues (2025) review how objective and subjective awareness measures yield different thresholds, which leads to two different ways of demonstrating unconscious perception. They describe newer methods (regression-based Bayesian modelling, sensitivity-versus-awareness curves, liminal primes, two-interval forced choice) meant to address them. *Sources:* K25. *Limits:* Review; methods not tested here. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-01;R5.
- **TD-SUBC-19** [E] In a single-process signal-detection model with no unconscious route, discrimination accuracy on trials reported as 'unseen' was 61% (d' = 1, criterion 1; H1 supported), and 62.5% of trials were reported unseen. Above-chance performance under a subjective 'no awareness' report is therefore what a conservative criterion predicts. It does not by itself show a separate unconscious process. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-5#V2;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-5#H1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-5#R1. *Limits:* Equal-variance Gaussian toy. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-18;R5.
- **TD-SUBC-20** [E] In SIM-SUBC-B, dreaming had no causal role, but dreams preferentially sampled strongly encoded reactivated memories. Under that rule, dreamed memories beat reactivated-but-not-dreamed memories in 97% (beta 0.5) and 100% (beta 1) of simulated studies (H1 supported). Under uniform selection the rate was 5%. Testing K10's proposal requires controlling for encoding strength. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-SUBC-B/iter-3#R1. *Limits:* Toy; K10 specifies no selection rule. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-05;TB-SUBC-06.
- **TD-SUBC-24** [E] Jacoby's process dissociation procedure (PDP) separates conscious recollection (R) from automatic, unconscious influence (F) by contrasting an inclusion condition, where both help, with an exclusion condition, where they act in opposition (R = I - E). It needs strong assumptions, mainly that the two processes are independent and act the same way in both conditions. Balota (2026) notes these assumptions generated controversy, including reports of correlated R and F estimates. *Sources:* K33. *Limits:* Historical review by one author. It describes the debate and does not settle it. *Deepens:* TC-SUBC-01;R2.
- **TD-SUBC-25** [E] In SIM-SUBC-C, a correlation of .5 between recollection and automatic influence across items biased the PDP estimate of the automatic component by -0.06 (H1 supported), and a correlation of .3 by -0.035. If 10% of recollected items slipped through in exclusion, the bias was +0.027 (pre-registered H1 of at least 0.05: not supported); it reached 0.05 only at 20%. Violated PDP assumptions can shift estimates of unconscious influence by a few points, enough to matter for small effects but not to create or erase large ones. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-C/iter-1#V1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-1#H1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-1#R1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-7#V1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-7#H1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-7#R1. *Limits:* Probit item model with illustrative parameters. Real violations may be larger or of other forms. *Deepens:* TC-SUBC-24.
- **TD-SUBC-26** [E] Elbaz, Yaron and Mudrik (2025) argue that standard calibration methods are not designed to find the highest stimulus intensity that still stays unconscious (the upper subliminal threshold). In their simulations, existing methods estimated it with strikingly low accuracy, and their new procedure (STEP) did better. Overestimating the threshold risks presenting stimuli that are partly conscious, while underestimating it weakens unconscious effects and can make consciousness look more necessary than it is. *Sources:* K34. *Limits:* Developers' own evaluation; three validation experiments; the comparison depends on the simulation framework they chose. *Deepens:* TC-SUBC-19;TC-SUBC-07.
- **TD-SUBC-27** [E] In SIM-SUBC-C, a standard 1-up-2-down staircase put 87.6% of simulated observers at an intensity with at least 60% objective accuracy (H1 supported), a median 3.2 times the true upper subliminal threshold. With K34's design of 9 levels and 64 trials, the naive rule 'highest level not significantly above chance' overestimated the threshold for 92.4% of observers (H1 supported) and picked the right level for only 7.4%. With 128 trials per level it still overestimated it for 78.4% (H1 supported; exact level 19.9%). Treating 'not significantly above chance' as 'unconscious' systematically lets partly visible stimuli through. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-C/iter-2#V1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-2#H1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-2#R1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-3#V1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-3#H1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-3#R1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-9#V1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-9#H1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-9#R1. *Limits:* Weibull observers with illustrative thresholds. STEP itself was not re-implemented. *Deepens:* TC-SUBC-26.
- **TD-SUBC-28** [E] Mind x Justice. In a preregistered survey of 8,917 people in 44 countries, stronger belief in free will predicted harsher judgements of how much everyday moral wrongdoers deserve punishment. This held within countries (b = 0.11 per SD) and between countries (b = 0.24), controlling for age, gender, religiosity and political orientation. Country-mean free will belief did not predict national death-penalty endorsement (b = 0.65, SE = 0.74, p = .385; 38 countries), which the authors call a low-powered null. The authors link the effect to attributions of moral responsibility and retributive justice. *Sources:* K35. *Limits:* Correlational. Scalar measurement invariance was not supported, so country means may not be comparable. Online panel samples. *Deepens:* R2;R6.
- **TD-SUBC-29** [E] Mind x Justice, two readings of K35. (1) Even if the country-level slope were truly 0.65, a test with SE 0.74 has power 0.14 with 38 countries (H1 supported) and 0.16 with all 44. About 200 countries of similar precision would be needed for 50% power, and about 400 for 80%. K35's death-penalty null is therefore uninformative, as the authors say. (2) The within-country effect corresponds to r of about 0.10 (1% of variance). Of two random people, the one with stronger free will belief is the more punitive only 53% of the time (H1 supported). The free-will and punitiveness link is reliable but small for individuals. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-C/iter-4#V1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-4#H1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-4#R1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-5#V1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-5#H1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-5#R1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-8#V1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-8#H1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-8#R1. *Limits:* Treats reported coefficients as true values and approximates a covariate-adjusted slope by a correlation. *Deepens:* TC-SUBC-28.
- **TD-SUBC-31** [E] In SIM-SUBC-C's toy version of K36's hypothesis, an 'intention' signal that preceded action but had no causal role still came to predict the action on at least 80% of trials after 2,000 trials of associative learning (85.3%; H1 supported). With an uninformative signal, prediction stayed at chance (V1). The toy shows only that the proposed mechanism is coherent. It is not evidence that human free will is illusory. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-C/iter-6#V1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-6#H1;SIM-SUBC-C/iter-6#R1. *Limits:* One-cause delta-rule learner. Nothing in it is fitted to neural or behavioural data. *Deepens:* TC-SUBC-30.
- **TD-SUBC-32** [E] A Registered Report replicated the first 'unconscious working memory' study across 19 laboratories (N = 531, 720 trials each). Working-memory accuracy on cue-present trials rated unseen was above chance (.55). Performance correlated positively with cue-detection sensitivity (r = .228), contrary to the original preregistered expectation of no correlation. The regression of performance on sensitivity had an above-chance intercept (.521; the original study reported .60). The authors conclude that working memory can operate on unconscious representations but remains linked to perceptual sensitivity. *Sources:* K48. *Limits:* One paradigm (rotated Gabor, 2-s delay) and a subjective awareness criterion (PAS). The intercept method assumes a linear relation down to zero sensitivity. *Deepens:* TC-SUBC-19;TC-SUBC-07.
- **TD-SUBC-34** [E] In a registered report using Bayesian sequential analysis, an anti-free-will manipulation lowered free-will belief substantially (d = .75; n = 309 vs 272). Yet it failed to conceptually replicate the link between free-will belief and cognitive control: the evidence mostly supported the null, and diminished belief in free will changed neither control nor reliance on automatic impulses in a task-switching paradigm. *Sources:* K63. *Limits:* One task-switching paradigm; online sample. Earlier positive findings used other measures. *Deepens:* TC-SUBC-28;TC-SUBC-30.
- **TD-SUBC-35** [E] In SIM-SUBC-D, at K63's group sizes a default Bayes factor would favour the null by at least 3 in 24% of studies even if the true effect were d = 0.2 (pre-registered H1 of at most 5%: not supported). Against d = 0.3 it would do so in only 3% (H1 supported). With 100 per group, misleading null evidence at d = 0.2 rose to 44% (not supported). K63's null rules out moderate effects of free-will disbelief on control, but not small ones. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-D/iter-1#V1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-1#H1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-1#R1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-4#V1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-4#H1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-4#R1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-10#V1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-10#H1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-10#R1. *Limits:* Single index; K63's exact prior and sequential stopping were not modelled. *Deepens:* TD-SUBC-34.
- **TD-SUBC-36** [E] Mind x Justice. Banaji, Fiske and Massey (2021) describe systemic racism as operating 'with or without intention and with or without awareness'. Implicit race biases improve only slightly over time and predict racialized behaviour. They note that the attitude-behaviour link is marred by poor-quality studies and controversy. Because racialized social behaviour is controllable given incentive, they argue, and control implies responsibility in lay and legal minds, people bear responsibility for unexamined bias, an interpretation they expect to be contested. They distinguish 'systemic race bias' (implicit cognition) from racism. *Sources:* K56. *Limits:* Tutorial review by advocates of the implicit-bias research programme; it addresses implicit bias and responsibility conceptually, not in a direct study (the Cycle C gap remains only partly filled). *Deepens:* TC-SUBC-28;TC-SUBC-30.
- **TD-SUBC-37** [E] Mind x Justice, in SIM-SUBC-D. An individual-level association of r = 0.10 (1% of variance) between group membership and a decision score gives a 1.43-fold difference in favourable outcomes when the top 10% are selected (H1 supported), and 1.58-fold at the top 2% (supported). At a median split it is 1.18-fold (pre-registered H1 of at least 1.2: not supported). At r = 0.05 and the top 10% it is still 1.20-fold (supported). Small individual biases can produce material group disparities in selective decisions, which is why their small size does not settle their social importance. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-D/iter-2#V1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-2#H1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-2#R1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-5#V1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-5#H1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-5#R1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-7#V1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-7#H1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-7#R1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-11#V1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-11#H1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-11#R1. *Limits:* Illustrative effect sizes; one decision per person; no accumulation. *Deepens:* TD-SUBC-36.
- **TD-SUBC-38** [E] In SIM-SUBC-D, an individual-level correlation of 0.05 between a regional history variable and individual bias becomes a state-level correlation of 0.88 across 50 states with 2,000 respondents each (H1 supported), and 0.89 with 20 regions. It falls to 0.53 with an individual r of 0.02, 0.54 with 200 respondents per state, and 0.48 when unrelated state-level noise is added (pre-registered H1 of at least 0.6: not supported in those three). K56's state-level r = 0.64 is compatible with a very small individual-level association. Aggregate correlations must not be read as individual effect sizes. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-D/iter-3#V1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-3#H1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-3#R1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-6#V1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-6#H1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-6#R1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-8#V1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-8#H1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-8#R1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-9#V1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-9#H1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-9#R1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-12#V1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-12#H1;SIM-SUBC-D/iter-12#R1. *Limits:* Illustrative parameters; no confounding modelled except state noise. *Deepens:* TD-SUBC-36.
- **TD-SUBC-08** [P] Tsikandilakis et al. (2026) distinguish 'subliminality' (responses to imperceptible elicitors) from an 'unconscious mind' of automatic processes that precede and later interact with awareness, and argue that a purely subliminal module could not support skill acquisition or adaptation. They note the lack of an experimental paradigm to test this. *Sources:* K07. *Limits:* Evolutionary argument, not an experiment. *Deepens:* R2;R5.
- **TD-SUBC-33** [P] Read against Track B, K48's unseen-trial accuracy of .55 is the size that SIM-SUBC-B iteration 5 produced with no unconscious route at all, just from rating unseen trials on the same evidence. So that figure alone is not decisive. The stronger evidence is the above-chance intercept at zero sensitivity (.521), together with the reliability and validity checks K48 reports. The track rates unconscious working memory as Plausible, with a small effect closely tied to perceptual sensitivity. *Sources:* K48. *Limits:* The Track B model is a single-process signal-detection toy. K48's intercept was not re-analysed. *Deepens:* TC-SUBC-32;TC-SUBC-19.
- **TD-SUBC-39** [P] Mind x Justice. Anderson and Kiehl (2020) argue that neuroscience will not much change how legal responsibility is assigned. It can, however, help shed 'our harshest retributivist instincts' in favour of pragmatic strategies against mass incarceration and recidivism. Read with K35 (free-will belief predicts punitiveness) and K63 (lowering free-will belief did not change control), this suggests that beliefs about agency shape punishment attitudes more than they shape behaviour. *Sources:* K57. *Limits:* Perspective article; the synthesis with K35 and K63 is the track's own reading. *Deepens:* TC-SUBC-28;TD-SUBC-34.
- **TD-SUBC-12** [PS] Scope: 'the subconscious' is not one thing. The track keeps apart the psychodynamic repressed unconscious (contested; K11 documents practitioner beliefs, not evidence), cognitive and implicit processing (evidence depends on awareness measurement, K09), sleep-based implicit memory processing (K10, a proposal) and contemplative doctrines (S32, S31, described). Claims about one may not be carried to another. *Sources:* G03;K07;K09;K10;K11;S32. *Limits:* Framework statement. *Deepens:* R2;R5.
- **TD-SUBC-21** [PS] Two senses of 'subconscious' are kept apart in this track. The psychodynamic repressed unconscious is described as a clinical and theoretical tradition whose central memory claim is contested (K24). The cognitive unconscious is supported for perception and automatic processing, but its size depends on how awareness is measured (K25). Contemplative store-consciousness views (Cycle A) are recorded as descriptions. *Sources:* K24;K25;G03. *Limits:* Interpretive organisation. *Deepens:* TB-SUBC-12.
- **TD-SUBC-05** [H] Budson & Paller's memory theory of consciousness proposes that the explicit-memory system produces all conscious experience, that reactivation of stored memories during sleep for consolidation stays implicit (below experience), and that fragments of a subset of reactivated memories are strung into the dream storyline. This is a proposal, not an established account. *Sources:* K10. *Limits:* Theory paper; rival theories of dreaming and consciousness exist. *Deepens:* R2;R5:C0v2-R5-08.
- **TD-SUBC-30** [H] Kansala et al. (2026) propose, as a hypothesis, that the felt experience of free will is a conditioned illusion. On this view, unconscious premotor activity precedes and causes action, and the temporal contiguity of felt intention and action reinforces a misattribution of authorship. The authors postulate determinism rather than conclude it, and note that the underlying question may be untestable in principle. The track records this as a Hypothetical bridge from the subconscious to questions of moral and legal responsibility. *Sources:* K36. *Limits:* No new data. It depends on readiness-potential interpretations that are themselves disputed. The authors state the model is unnecessary if free will exists. *Deepens:* TC-SUBC-28;R2.
