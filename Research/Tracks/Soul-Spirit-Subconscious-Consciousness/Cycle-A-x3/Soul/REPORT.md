# Soul — Track Cycle A (x3)

Track: Soul, Spirit, Subconscious and Consciousness (`../../README.md`). Scope and boundary: `../../FRAMEWORK.md` § Soul. Labels: **E** Established · **P** Plausible · **PS** Philosophical/Spiritual · **H** Hypothetical. GAIA is the motherthread (PS framing) and is kept separate from the science.

## Reading of this cycle
Cycle A turned from what traditions say about the soul (Report 3) to what can be studied about **beliefs** in souls. The most useful finding is a reading problem. People judge mental states more likely than physical states to continue after death, yet most expect everything to cease (K01, described; SIM-SOUL-A iter-1 shows both summaries can hold for the same data). Survey marginals also cannot show how many scientists hold dualism and physicalism together (K03; SIM-SOUL-A iter-2, H1 not supported). Kappa needs prevalence beside it (SIM-SOUL-A iter-3), which feeds back into Report 3's coding work.



## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **TA-SOUL-02** [E] Replication record in the cognitive science of supernatural belief (as reported by van Elk 2022): the finding that priming analytic thinking reduces supernatural belief could not be replicated in a high-powered replication, and a cross-cultural study found the religiosity-intuitive-thinking relation in only three of 13 countries. *Sources:* K01. *Limits:* Reported via a perspective article, not re-analysed here. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-13.
- **TA-SOUL-04** [E] In a survey of 2,657 neuroscientists, 63.62% agreed with reductive physicalism, 36.68% with dualism, 42.94% with nonreductive physicalism, 40.94% with dual-aspect monism and 34.69% with functionalism (positions rated separately), and only 17.52% agreed that free will does not exist. *Sources:* K03. *Limits:* Self-selected respondents; agreement items, not forced choice; describes beliefs, not truth of the positions. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-16;R5:C0v2-R5-12.
- **TA-SOUL-05** [E] In SIM-SOUL-A, K03's published agreement shares alone force only 0.3% of respondents to agree with both reductive physicalism and dualism (pre-registered H1 of at least 10%: not supported; independence would give 23.3%), although they do force at least 29.7% to agree with two or more of the five positions. How many neuroscientists hold both views cannot be read from the marginals. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-2#H1;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-2#H2. *Limits:* Uses only marginals; K03's cross-tabulations (SI) were not read. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-16.
- **TA-SOUL-06** [E] In open-ended answers from 387 science-oriented Finns, 76% mentioned some kind of annihilation after death and 35% expressed some kind of continuity, mostly nonreligious (social bonds, nature); secular continuity was mentioned more by unbelievers, while theists relied mainly on afterlife beliefs. *Sources:* K02. *Limits:* Online sample recruited through science-promoting organisations; single-country; categories overlap. *Deepens:* R3:C1-R3-02;R3:C0v2-R3-15.
- **TA-SOUL-07** [E] In SIM-SOUL-A, K02's coding reliability (96% agreement, kappa .79) implies chance agreement of .81, i.e. a skewed category (prevalence about .11 in a binary symmetric-error model, coder error about 2.4%); the same coders would reach kappa .91 at prevalence .5 and .65 at prevalence .05. Kappa values in soul-conception coding (Report 3) therefore need the category prevalence reported beside them. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-3#V2;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-3#H1;K02;W07. *Limits:* Binary simplification of a multi-category scheme. *Deepens:* R3:C2-R3-04;R3:C3-R3-02.
- **TA-SOUL-08** [E] In SIM-SOUL-A's illustrative continuity-judgment model (marginals 8% physical, 18% mental, not K01's numbers), a mental-over-physical continuity difference was overwhelming (t = 43 at N = 10,000; power .998 at n = 100) while 67% of simulated respondents said every state ceases and only 17% showed a 'dualist' pattern: 'people favour mental continuity' and 'most people expect total cessation' are both true of the same data. *Sources:* SIM-SOUL-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-1#H2;SIM-SOUL-A/iter-1#H3. *Limits:* Illustrative parameters; demonstrates a reading problem, not a fact about any population. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-16.
- **TA-SOUL-01** [P] van Elk (2022) describes a 24-country vignette study (more than 10,000 participants): mental states were judged more likely than physical states to continue after a person's death, but the modal response was that both mental and physical states cease, which questions the universality of mind-body dualism and fits an 'intuitive materialism' account. *Sources:* K01. *Limits:* Secondary description of a study that was 'submitted' when K01 was written; K01 gives no numbers. Vignette judgments are not a measure of explicit soul belief. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-13;R3:C0v2-R3-16.
- **TA-SOUL-03** [P] Among proposed proximate mechanisms of belief in souls, afterlife and spirits (mentalizing, dualistic reasoning, intuitive thinking), the link between mentalizing ability and supernatural belief 'appears robust' but with small effect sizes, and people with mentalizing difficulties can still hold such beliefs. *Sources:* K01. *Limits:* Small effects; correlational; reported via review. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-13;R2.
- **TA-SOUL-09** [PS] Persistence of the person: psychological-continuity views distinguish psychological connectedness from continuity and face the fission problem; soul, organism and continuity answers remain competing philosophical positions, and the track records them without choosing one. *Sources:* U13;G03. *Limits:* Philosophical; not decidable by the data reviewed. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-13.
- **TA-SOUL-10** [PS] Boundary: nothing in Track Cycle A establishes the existence, nonexistence or nature of a soul. The empirical results concern beliefs about souls and how such beliefs are measured. GAIA as motherthread organises the topic as a Philosophical/Spiritual principle and is not a finding. *Sources:* G03. *Limits:* Framework statement. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-18.
