# Subconscious — Track Cycle A (x3)

Track: Soul, Spirit, Subconscious and Consciousness (`../../README.md`). Scope and boundary: `../../FRAMEWORK.md` § Subconscious. Labels: **E** Established · **P** Plausible · **PS** Philosophical/Spiritual · **H** Hypothetical. GAIA is the motherthread (PS framing) and is kept separate from the science.

## Reading of this cycle
The Subconscious is new. Cycle A set its scope (five senses of the term, kept apart) and found that the strongest current evidence is **methodological**. 'Unconscious' learning often rests on awareness tests too small to detect awareness (K09, reproduced in SIM-SUBC-A iter-1), and the classic behavioural-priming result failed replication, with signs of experimenter expectancy and awareness (K08). One simulation had a design flaw, disclosed and scheduled for Cycle B (iter-2). Traditional and historical views (Yogacara, Advaita, Leibniz) are described from sources already in the corpus.



## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **TA-SUBC-01** [E] A systematic review of 73 contextual-cuing studies (181 awareness tests) found a meta-analytic awareness effect of dz = 0.31, so learning in these 'implicit' tasks was conscious; with a median N of 16, awareness tests had power of about .21, matching the 21.5% of significant awareness results. *Sources:* K09. *Limits:* One paradigm (contextual cuing); generalisation to other implicit-learning paradigms is argued, not shown. *Deepens:* R2;R5:C0v2-R5-03.
- **TA-SUBC-02** [E] SIM-SUBC-A reproduced K09's power argument: analytic power .213 and simulated .211 at n = 16 and dz = .31, 84 participants for 80% power. 79% of such awareness tests come out non-significant although awareness is real by construction, while pooling 181 tests detected awareness in every simulated literature. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-1#V2;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-1#V3;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-1#H2. *Limits:* Normal difference scores. *Deepens:* R2.
- **TA-SUBC-03** [E] Doyen et al. (2012) did not replicate the 'elderly prime slows walking' effect with automated timing and 120 participants; a walking-speed effect appeared only when experimenters believed participants would walk slower, and debriefing suggested awareness of the primes. *Sources:* K08. *Limits:* Two experiments; one classic paradigm. *Deepens:* R2.
- **TA-SUBC-04** [E] In SIM-SUBC-A, the post-hoc 'unaware subgroup' model as specified did not produce spurious unconscious priming (significant in 1.3% of studies; pre-registered H1 of at least 50%: not supported). Disclosed design flaw: knowledge was unbounded below, so selecting below-chance awareness scores selected below-average knowledge (mean -0.31). The test is redone with non-negative knowledge in Track Cycle B. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-2#H1. *Limits:* Design flaw disclosed; this is not evidence for or against the selection artefact. *Deepens:* R2.
- **TA-SUBC-06** [E] In SIM-SUBC-A's toy of K10's proposal (dreams sample 3 of 30 reactivated memories; dreaming itself does nothing), dreamed memories were recalled 0.36 SD better than all other memories, significant in 75% of simulated studies (pre-registered H1 of at least 80%: not supported, though the association is large), and no better than reactivated-but-not-dreamed memories (4.9%). Dream-memory associations need the reactivated-not-dreamed comparison before dreams are credited. *Sources:* SIM-SUBC-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-3#H1;SIM-SUBC-A/iter-3#H2. *Limits:* Toy; uniform dream sampling assumed. *Deepens:* R2.
- **TA-SUBC-07** [E] Among 178 UK mental-health professionals, most indicated some belief in unconscious repression (over 78%) and dissociative amnesia (over 84%), with higher agreement among EMDR and other trauma-focused practitioners; the authors link such beliefs to the risk of false or non-experienced memories in therapy. *Sources:* K11. *Limits:* Describes beliefs, which are not evidence that repression occurs; convenience sample. *Deepens:* R2.
- **TA-SUBC-09** [E] Yogacara Buddhism (descriptive): Vasubandhu and his followers posit a receptacle or repository consciousness (alaya-vijnana), 'a subliminal form of cognitive awareness' underlying the propensities, habits and tendencies that direct action. *Sources:* S32. *Limits:* Describes a doctrine; not an empirical claim about the brain. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-09;R5.
- **TA-SUBC-10** [E] Advaita Vedanta (descriptive): waking (jagrat), dreaming (svapna) and deep sleep (susupti) point to a fourth state, turiya (pure consciousness), to be realised as the true self. Deep sleep is described as partial experience of its bliss. *Sources:* S31. *Limits:* Doctrine described, not tested. *Deepens:* R3:C0v2-R3-08;R5.
- **TA-SUBC-11** [E] History of the idea (descriptive): Leibniz allowed infinitely many degrees of consciousness and unconscious 'petites perceptions', distinguishing perception from apperception, an early philosophical form of an unconscious mind long before Freud. *Sources:* S16. *Limits:* Historical description. *Deepens:* R5:C0v2-R5-01.
- **TA-SUBC-08** [P] Tsikandilakis et al. (2026) distinguish 'subliminality' (responses to imperceptible elicitors) from an 'unconscious mind' of automatic processes that precede and later interact with awareness, and argue that a purely subliminal module could not support skill acquisition or adaptation. They note the lack of an experimental paradigm to test this. *Sources:* K07. *Limits:* Evolutionary argument, not an experiment. *Deepens:* R2;R5.
- **TA-SUBC-12** [PS] Scope: 'the subconscious' is not one thing. The track keeps apart the psychodynamic repressed unconscious (contested; K11 documents practitioner beliefs, not evidence), cognitive and implicit processing (evidence depends on awareness measurement, K09), sleep-based implicit memory processing (K10, a proposal) and contemplative doctrines (S32, S31, described). Claims about one may not be carried to another. *Sources:* G03;K07;K09;K10;K11;S32. *Limits:* Framework statement. *Deepens:* R2;R5.
- **TA-SUBC-05** [H] Budson & Paller's memory theory of consciousness proposes that the explicit-memory system produces all conscious experience, that reactivation of stored memories during sleep for consolidation stays implicit (below experience), and that fragments of a subset of reactivated memories are strung into the dream storyline. This is a proposal, not an established account. *Sources:* K10. *Limits:* Theory paper; rival theories of dreaming and consciousness exist. *Deepens:* R2;R5:C0v2-R5-08.
