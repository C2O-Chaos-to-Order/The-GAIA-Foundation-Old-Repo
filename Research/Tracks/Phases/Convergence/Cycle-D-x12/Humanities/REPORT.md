- **CVD-HUM-06** [P] P82 describes dialect levelling as the reduction of differences between dialects through contact. In Hinskens' Limburg study 14 of 21 dialect features lost distinctions, the wider a feature's distribution the less likely it was to level with the standard, and levelling did not necessarily converge toward the standard (sometimes diverged). New Zealand English formed mostly between 1840 and 1880 by levelling out demographic minority dialect forms (Australians were 7% of immigrants before 1881); Trudgill describes focusing until only one variant remains per variable, a process that 'may take an extended length of time'. Taiwan Mandarin tonal levelling took about 30 years, a generation earlier than Trudgill's general pattern. *Operationalization:* HUM Convergence = convergence of dialects by levelling. *Sources:* P82. *Limits:* Wikipedia (tertiary); the primary studies (Hinskens 1998, Trudgill 1986, Kerswill) were not read; the New Zealand dates and 7% are marked 'citation needed' in P82; P82 misspells Hinskens twice.
