- **CVC-HUM-01** [P] In shadowing and interactive tasks with German speakers, phonetic accommodation of -ig toward a model talker's variant occurred in 53.5% of shadowing trials (301/563) and 27.3% of interaction trials (157/575); a binomial mixed model selected by likelihood-ratio tests (final M4) shows accommodation growing with exposure order and a task x frequency x gender interaction, with low-frequency words accommodating more in shadowing. *Operationalization:* HUM Convergence = phonetic convergence (accommodation). *Sources:* P71. *Limits:* One variable, one dialect pair; M9 had lower AIC and BIC but was rejected for separation instability; P71 discloses ChatGPT use for language editing.
- **CVC-HUM-06** [P] P82 describes dialect levelling as the reduction of differences between dialects through contact. In Hinskens' Limburg study 14 of 21 dialect features lost distinctions, the wider a feature's distribution the less likely it was to level with the standard, and levelling did not necessarily converge toward the standard (sometimes diverged). New Zealand English formed mostly between 1840 and 1880 by levelling out demographic minority dialect forms (Australians were 7% of immigrants before 1881); Trudgill describes focusing until only one variant remains per variable, a process that 'may take an extended length of time'. Taiwan Mandarin tonal levelling took about 30 years, a generation earlier than Trudgill's general pattern. *Operationalization:* HUM Convergence = convergence of dialects by levelling. *Sources:* P82. *Limits:* Wikipedia (tertiary); the primary studies (Hinskens 1998, Trudgill 1986, Kerswill) were not read; the New Zealand dates and 7% are marked 'citation needed' in P82; P82 misspells Hinskens twice.
- **CVC-HUM-03** [H] A minimal exemplar model of P71's entrenchment account (100 vs 10 stored exemplars; attention 1.0 vs 0.3) gives more accommodation for low-frequency words (0.244 vs 0.034 in shadowing), more in shadowing, and growth over exposure, but its logit-scale task x frequency interaction is about 0 (-0.06; 0.06 with decay), not > 0.5: not supported, so P71's positive task x frequency coefficient (1.68) needs more than proportional exemplar updating. *Operationalization:* HUM Convergence = phonetic convergence via exemplars. *Sources:* SIM-CV-HUM-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-CV-HUM-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-CV-HUM-A/iter-1#H2;SIM-CV-HUM-A/iter-1#H3;SIM-CV-HUM-A/iter-1#H4;SIM-CV-HUM-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-CV-HUM-A/iter-2#H1;SIM-CV-HUM-A/iter-2#H2. *Limits:* Toy; parameters arbitrary; foreknown from arithmetic (disclosed).

- **CVC-HUM-07** [H] In the agent's copying toy, levelling toward the demographic majority (P82's New Zealand account) needs majority-amplifying copying: with neutral copying a variant fixes with probability equal to its share (0.068 from 7%, 0.705 from 70%), so a majority is far from certain; with conformist copying (c = 2) every majority (55-93%) fixed and every minority (7-45%) was lost, in 3.7 generations on average from 70% (N 1000; 4.0 for N 10000), so the pre-registered 'within 2 generations' (New Zealand's 1840-1880) was not supported; weak conformity (c = 1.2) still fixed a 55% majority in 97.7% of runs; a 54% majority in a community of 50 with c = 3 fixed in 95%.
