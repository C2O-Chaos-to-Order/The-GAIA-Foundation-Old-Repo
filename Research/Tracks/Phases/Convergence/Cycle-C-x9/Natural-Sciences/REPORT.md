## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **CVC-NAT-01** [E] P68 models collective synchrony with a Sakaguchi-Kuramoto model in which each individual's phase lag is its strategy; in a population of cooperators (lag 0) and cheaters (lag alpha) the two-cluster state with phase gap alpha is stable when the cheater fraction n_ch < 1/(1 - cos alpha), numerical critical fractions follow this curve, and under P68's payoffs persistent populations settle at 50-50 because the minority loses its advantage as it grows. *Operationalization:* NAT Convergence = synchronisation (oscillators converging on a common rhythm). *Sources:* P68. *Limits:* Identical natural frequencies; one coupling strength. Slips: Eq. 9 has a stray bracket and drops K from the second term; 'strays too far form'; the Fig. 2 caption says the system is incoherent 'when alpha_ch >= pi/2', which on the bound applies only above the critical fraction.

- **CVC-NAT-06** [E] P68's replicator dynamic ended at 0.500 cheaters for alpha pi/3, pi/2, 2pi/3 and 5pi/6 from both 0.2 and 0.8, consistent with P68's 'always 50-50' (payoffs equalise when the cheater share is one half). *Operationalization:* NAT Convergence = evolutionary convergence to a mixed population. *Sources:* SIM-CV-NAT-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-CV-NAT-A/iter-3#H1. *Limits:* Uses P68's payoff forms; does not test P68's mutation runs.
- **CVC-NAT-07** [E] P76 states the Kuramoto model of synchronisation: each oscillator has its own natural frequency and is coupled to all others through the sine of the phase difference; for a unimodal symmetric frequency density g there is a critical coupling K_c = 2/(pi g(0)) below which the long-run order parameter r is 0, with r ~ sqrt(16/(pi K_c^3)) sqrt(mu/(-g''(0))) just above it (K = K_c(1 + mu)); for N = 2 the pair locks exactly when K > K_c = 2|omega_1|, N = 3 cannot be chaotic, and chaos first occurs at N = 4. The model assumes weak coupling and identical or nearly identical oscillators. *Operationalization:* NAT Convergence = synchronisation of heterogeneous oscillators. *Sources:* P76. *Limits:* Wikipedia (tertiary; formulas rendered from markup); the primary sources (Kuramoto 1975, 1984; Strogatz 2000) were not read. The Lorentzian closed form r = sqrt(1 - K_c/K) is not stated in P76.
- **CVC-NAT-08** [E] P76's onset results reproduce. Unit Gaussian: K_c = 1.59577 and r was within 0.001 of the self-consistency solution at K = 1.65-2.5. P76's small-mu formula is good only at onset: 0.327 vs 0.316 at mu 0.034 and 0.551 vs 0.503 at mu 0.097, but 0.892 vs 0.715 at K = 2 and 1.33 (an impossible r > 1) at K = 2.5. *Operationalization:* NAT Convergence = synchronisation threshold (heterogeneous). *Sources:* SIM-CV-NAT-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-CV-NAT-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-CV-NAT-B/iter-1#H2;SIM-CV-NAT-B/iter-1#H3;SIM-CV-NAT-B/iter-1#H4;SIM-CV-NAT-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-CV-NAT-B/iter-2#H1;SIM-CV-NAT-B/iter-2#H2;SIM-CV-NAT-B/iter-2#H3;SIM-CV-NAT-B/iter-2#H4;SIM-CV-NAT-B/iter-2#H5. *Limits:* All-to-all coupling, RK4 dt 0.01, r averaged over t = 100-200; 1.3% of Lorentzian mass clipped. - **CVC-NAT-12** [P] Finite-size behaviour of P76's model with random Gaussian frequencies (K_c = 1.596): below K_c (K = 0.8) r fell from 0.129 (N = 100) to 0.018 (N = 6400) with r sqrt(N) between 1.29 and 1.43 (incoherent N^-1/2 scaling, drifting up by 11%); above K_c (K = 2.5) r at N = 6400 was 0.8686 against the self-consistency value 0.8697, and its spread across draws fell from 0.042 to 0.0014; at K_c, r fell only from 0.375 to 0.144 (about N^-0.23), more slowly than N^-1/2.
