## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **CVC-SOC-08** [E] P69's lower bound sup F(beta)(1 - F(n beta)^(n-1)) for log-normal estimates never exceeded the Monte Carlo Omega_n in 27 cases, but in P69's Fig. 2-type contexts (n 50, theta 2, omega 1/3) it is nearly trivial: 0.0003-0.11 against Omega_n 0.67-0.75. With sigma fixed the bound falls with group size for sigma 1 and 2 (sigma 2: 0.33 at n 10 to 0.004 at n 10^4) and peaks at n 1000 for sigma 3 (0.61, 0.64, 0.61); for sigma 4 and 6 it rises to 0.84 and 0.95 by n 10^4, and for sigma 0.25 it is 0. This matches P69's remark that tail decay must be limited for a non-zero limit and shows its phase-transition behaviour at finite n. *Operationalization:* SOC Convergence = collective estimation under centralization. *Sources:* SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-1#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-1#H1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-1#H2;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-2#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-2#H1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-2#H2;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-3#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-3#H1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-3#H2;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-4#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-4#H1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-4#H2. *Limits:* The agent's log-normal parametrisation; P69's SI (proofs, scaling of sigma with n) not read; sup on a finite beta grid (a lower estimate of the sup, which keeps the comparison conservative).
- **CVC-SOC-10** [E] P69's figure captions give two different counts for the same subset: the logistic analysis 'was conducted on 678 observations (groups with social influence)', while the linear analysis lists '687 observations; 582 groups with social influence (centralized) and 105 groups without social influence'. 582 + 105 = 687 matches the 687 collective estimations in P69's text, so 678 is most likely a transposition of 687 or a miscount of the 582 social-influence groups; the paper does not resolve it. *Operationalization:* SOC Convergence = collective estimation (reporting audit). *Sources:* P69. *Limits:* Caption-level audit only; P69's data and SI were not checked, so which count the logistic model used is unknown.
- **CVC-SOC-01** [P] P69 shows that whether a centralized influence network (one agent's estimate weighted by omega) improves the collective estimate over the plain mean depends on the distribution of initial estimates: it helps under overestimation bias or large dispersion and is reversed under low dispersion with underestimation; in a reanalysis of four experiments (2885 participants, 99 groups, 54 tasks, 687 collective estimates) the probability of improvement was explained by its heavy-tail measure (z = 4.16). *Operationalization:* SOC Convergence = estimates converging into a collective estimate. *Sources:* P69. *Limits:* Slips: '678 observations' vs 582 + 105 = 687 collective estimates; a logistic model is written with a Gaussian error term; the distributional parametrisation is in the SI (not read).
- **CVC-SOC-02** [P] With n 50, omega 1/3, theta 2 and a = theta exp(mu + sigma Z), the probability that the centralized estimate beats the mean was 0.681 under overestimation (mu 0.5, sigma 1), 0.7535 under large dispersion (mu 0, sigma 1.5) and 0.440 under low-dispersion underestimation (mu -0.3, sigma 0.3), reproducing P69's three qualitative regimes; unbiased low dispersion (mu 0, sigma 0.3) gave 0.340. *Operationalization:* SOC Convergence = wisdom of crowds under centralization. *Sources:* SIM-CV-SOC-0/iter-1#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-0/iter-1#H1;SIM-CV-SOC-0/iter-1#H2;SIM-CV-SOC-0/iter-1#H3. *Limits:* The log-normal parametrisation is the agent's, not P69's SI.
- **CVC-SOC-03** [P] Under overestimation (mu 0.5, sigma 1) centralization beat the plain mean with probability 0.596, 0.645, 0.683 and 0.690 for groups of 5, 10, 50 and 200: always above one half and rising with group size, as the mean's bias dominates its noise in larger groups. *Operationalization:* SOC Convergence = collective estimation and group size. *Sources:* SIM-CV-SOC-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-CV-SOC-A/iter-1#H2. *Limits:* Agent's log-normal parametrisation.
- **CVC-SOC-04** [P] Under large dispersion (mu 0, sigma 1.5) Omega stayed at 0.755, 0.751 and 0.744 for centralization weights 0.1, 0.5 and 0.9: the advantage barely depends on how strongly the central estimate is weighted. *Operationalization:* SOC Convergence = centralization weight. *Sources:* SIM-CV-SOC-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-A/iter-2#H1. *Limits:* Agent's parametrisation; one context.
- **CVC-SOC-05** [P] If the log-normal is mean-unbiased (mu = -sigma^2/2) centralization hurts: Omega 0.233 at sigma 0.3 and 0.283 at sigma 1.0, so in this model centralization helps only by countering a bias of the mean, consistent with P69's account. *Operationalization:* SOC Convergence = when centralization helps. *Sources:* SIM-CV-SOC-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-A/iter-3#H1. *Limits:* Context is the agent's, not one of P69's.
- **CVC-SOC-06** [P] Whenever the plain mean was biased upward, centralization beat it: Omega 0.571, 0.652, 0.756 and 0.773 for groups of 5, 10, 50 and 200 at mu 0, sigma 1.5; 0.680, 0.682 and 0.687 for weights 0.1, 0.5 and 0.9 at mu 0.5, sigma 1; and 0.597 at mu 1, sigma 0.5. CVA's 0.683 (mu 0.5, sigma 1, n 50) replicated on a new seed (0.681). *Operationalization:* SOC Convergence = collective estimation under centralization. *Sources:* SIM-CV-SOC-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-CV-SOC-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-B/iter-2#H1;SIM-CV-SOC-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-B/iter-5#H1;SIM-CV-SOC-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-B/iter-6#H1. *Limits:* Agent's log-normal parametrisation (P69's SI not read).
- **CVC-SOC-07** [P] With a mean-unbiased log-normal, centralization hurt at both small and large dispersion (Omega 0.232 at sigma 0.1, 0.314 at sigma 2.0), and its disadvantage grew with group size: 0.422, 0.379, 0.283 and 0.173 for n = 5, 10, 50 and 200 (mu -0.5, sigma 1). *Operationalization:* SOC Convergence = when centralization hurts. *Sources:* SIM-CV-SOC-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-CV-SOC-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-B/iter-4#H1. *Limits:* Contexts are the agent's, not P69's.
- **CVC-SOC-09** [P] Centralization contexts, x9: with large dispersion the advantage persists in large groups (Omega 0.769 at n 500, 0.772 at n 2000); a single dictator (omega 1) in the overestimation context gives 0.678 (agent arithmetic ~0.69); with a mean-unbiased low-dispersion crowd it is 0.23. Two hypotheses were not supported: underestimation with low dispersion (mu -1 and -0.5, sigma 0.3) gives Omega 0.448 and 0.440, below 1/2, which agrees with P69's own statement that the relationship is reversed under low dispersion and underestimation (the agent's hypothesis had extrapolated P69's 'not initially centered' sentence instead); and tiny groups are mixed (n 2: 0.480, n 3: 0.534). *Operationalization:* SOC Convergence = collective estimation. *Sources:* SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-5#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-5#H1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-6#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-6#H1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-7#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-7#H1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-8#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-8#H1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-9#V1;SIM-CV-SOC-C/iter-9#H1. *Limits:* Agent's log-normal parametrisation (P69's exact form in its SI, not read); the n = 2 result is specific to omega 1/3.
