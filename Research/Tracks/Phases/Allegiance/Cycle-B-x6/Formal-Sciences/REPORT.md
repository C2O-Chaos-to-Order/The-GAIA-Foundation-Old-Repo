## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **ALB-FOR-01** [E] P47 states five mechanisms under which natural selection can favour cooperation, each a benefit-to-cost threshold: kin selection r > c/b, direct reciprocity w > c/b, indirect reciprocity q > c/b, network reciprocity b/c > k, group selection b/c > 1 + n/m; for reciprocity, Table 1 adds stricter thresholds for risk-dominance and advantageousness, and the latter links to the '1/3 rule', which P47 says holds in the limit of weak selection. *Operationalization:* FOR Allegiance = cooperation stabilised by a mechanism. *Sources:* P47. *Limits:* Author manuscript of a short review; each rule is derived in a simplified model, and P47's 1/3 rule is stated only for weak selection.
- **ALB-FOR-07** [E] For the frequency-dependent Moran process under weak selection (N w << 1), a single A mutant has fixation probability above 1/N if and only if (N - 2)a + (2N - 1)b > (N + 1)c + (2N - 4)d (Nowak et al. 2004, restated in P58), which for large N becomes a + 2b > c + 2d, the one-third law. P58 explains the 1/3 by sojourn times: along an invasion path a player meets B-players twice as often as A-players. Outside weak selection the minimum basin of attraction a mutant needs goes from 2/3 (N w << 1) towards 1 (large N w), so strong selection makes invasion harder. *Operationalization:* FOR Allegiance = the one-third law and its finite-N form. *Sources:* P58. *Limits:* Author manuscript (accepted version), not the typeset article. Slips: 'explicitely', 'Genetaical', 'equilbiria', 'equilibirum'; reference 'Taylor, Iwasa & Nowak (2004)' in J Theor Biol 243 (volume 243 is 2006).
- **ALB-FOR-02** [P] Numerically solving P47's Table 1 matrices (w = q = r = 0.5, k = 4, n = 10, m = 20) reproduced every printed threshold exactly (ESS/RD/AD b/c = 2, 3, 4 for reciprocity). In a Moran process (N = 100, selection 0.005) the fixation probability of tit-for-tat in always-defect was below 1/N at b/c = 3.5 (0.00947) and above at 4.5 (0.01019), as the 1/3 rule predicts, but the crossing was at b/c 4.23, not within 5% of 4: the finite-N prediction is not supported at N = 100 (expected from the smoke test). *Operationalization:* FOR Allegiance = cooperation thresholds. *Sources:* SIM-AL-FOR-0/iter-1#V1;SIM-AL-FOR-0/iter-1#V2;SIM-AL-FOR-0/iter-1#H1;SIM-AL-FOR-0/iter-1#H2. *Limits:* The 1/3 rule is a large-N weak-selection limit; finite-N correction not sourced. Stochastic check at N = 20 matched the exact formula (0.0531 vs 0.0515).
