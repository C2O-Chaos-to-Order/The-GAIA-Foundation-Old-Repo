## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **ALD-FOR-01** [E] P47 states five mechanisms under which natural selection can favour cooperation, each a benefit-to-cost threshold: kin selection r > c/b, direct reciprocity w > c/b, indirect reciprocity q > c/b, network reciprocity b/c > k, group selection b/c > 1 + n/m; for reciprocity, Table 1 adds stricter thresholds for risk-dominance and advantageousness, and the latter links to the '1/3 rule', which P47 says holds in the limit of weak selection. *Operationalization:* FOR Allegiance = cooperation stabilised by a mechanism. *Sources:* P47. *Limits:* Author manuscript of a short review; each rule is derived in a simplified model, and P47's 1/3 rule is stated only for weak selection.
- **ALD-FOR-07** [E] For the frequency-dependent Moran process under weak selection (N w << 1), a single A mutant has fixation probability above 1/N if and only if (N - 2)a + (2N - 1)b > (N + 1)c + (2N - 4)d (Nowak et al. 2004, restated in P58), which for large N becomes a + 2b > c + 2d, the one-third law. P58 explains the 1/3 by sojourn times: along an invasion path a player meets B-players twice as often as A-players. Outside weak selection the minimum basin of attraction a mutant needs goes from 2/3 (N w << 1) towards 1 (large N w), so strong selection makes invasion harder. *Operationalization:* FOR Allegiance = the one-third law and its finite-N form. *Sources:* P58. *Limits:* Author manuscript (accepted version), not the typeset article. Slips: 'explicitely', 'Genetaical', 'equilbiria', 'equilibirum'; reference 'Taylor, Iwasa & Nowak (2004)' in J Theor Biol 243 (volume 243 is 2006).
