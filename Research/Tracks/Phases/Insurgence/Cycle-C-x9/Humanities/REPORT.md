## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **INC-HUM-01** [E] In P21's analysis of US Billboard Hot 100 songs 1960-2010, a style enriched for rap-related tags was very rare before the mid-1980s and then rapidly expanded to become the single largest style; P21 links this rise to the 1991 stylistic revolution. *Operationalization:* HUM Insurgence = a rare style rising rapidly to dominance. *Sources:* P21. *Limits:* Styles are clusters inferred from audio features and listener tags; chart songs only.
- **INC-HUM-02** [P] In a Wright-Fisher toy (1,000 songs, advantage 5%), a new style entering as a single copy took over in only 9.9% of 4,000 runs (Kimura 9.5%); the successful takeovers were S-shaped, with 10%-90% times of 90.9 generations against the logistic 90.1. Rare insurgent styles usually vanish, and the ones that win rise fast. *Operationalization:* HUM Insurgence = establishment and logistic takeover of a rare style. *Sources:* SIM-IN-HUM-0/iter-1#V1;SIM-IN-HUM-0/iter-1#H1;SIM-IN-HUM-0/iter-1#H2. *Limits:* Agent's toy; P21 gives frequencies, not a mechanism.
- **INC-HUM-03** [P] In the French Frantext corpus (1321 to the present) P28 studied 408 forms that underwent semantic expansion; 295 (over 70%) show at least one sigmoidal rise in frequency (p < 0.05 against random growth), and in 69% of sigmoidal growths the rise is preceded by a latency phase of near-constant low frequency lasting up to several centuries. *Operationalization:* HUM Insurgence = a new meaning taking over after a latency. *Sources:* P28. *Limits:* Single corpus and language; forms chosen by the authors (functional meanings); latency may be underestimated when the form is too rare to appear.
- **INC-HUM-04** [P] P28 models semantic expansion as a random walk of exemplar frequencies with a control parameter (the strength of the link from a related context) that tunes the system near a saddle-node bifurcation; latency is a first-passage time out of a trapping region of near-zero drift and the S-growth the fast convergence after it, and both durations are predicted to follow inverse-Gaussian-like distributions, as found in the corpus. *Operationalization:* HUM Insurgence = near-critical latency then S-growth. *Sources:* P28. *Limits:* P28 notes the inverse Gaussian fit is not perfect (KL divergence 0.10); model is mean-field, sociolinguistic factors backgrounded.
- **INC-HUM-05** [P] In P28's deterministic limit (beta = 0.808) the threshold was gamma_c = 0.378; below it the new form stuck at a low frequency (0.037 at delta = -0.05, 0.060 at -0.01) and above it took over, the transmission time falling from 100.2 at delta = 0.001 to 10.7 at 0.1 with log-log slope -0.486, the saddle-node bottleneck law also found in NAT (INA-NAT-03). *Operationalization:* HUM Insurgence = threshold for semantic invasion. *Sources:* SIM-IN-HUM-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-IN-HUM-A/iter-1#H1. *Limits:* gamma_c found numerically; P(x) built from P28's Eqs. 3.1-3.2.
- **INC-HUM-06** [P] In P28's random walk (M = 500) every one of 400 runs completed the change at the threshold (delta = 0), with mean latency 38.2 against mean growth 6.0 time units (latency CV 0.74). Just below threshold (delta = -0.02) the expectation that only some runs escape was not supported: all 400 escaped, but mean latency rose to 181 while growth stayed near 7.5. Noise removes the sharp threshold (as P28 states) and the distance to threshold sets mainly the length of the latency. *Operationalization:* HUM Insurgence = latency then S-growth. *Sources:* SIM-IN-HUM-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-IN-HUM-A/iter-2#H1;SIM-IN-HUM-A/iter-2#H2;SIM-IN-HUM-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-IN-HUM-A/iter-3#H1;SIM-IN-HUM-A/iter-3#H2. *Limits:* M = 500 (P28 used 5000), so noise is stronger than in P28; latency window x_c +/- 0.1 is the agent's.
- **INC-HUM-07** [P] P29 analyses S-curves of vocabulary replacement in Google-ngram data and finds they are not universal: German orthographic reforms and one irregular verb show dominant exogenous factors (exponential-like starts), while the romanization of Russian names and most verb regularizations show dominant endogenous factors (S-shaped starts); the shape of the start carries information on the adoption mechanism. *Operationalization:* HUM Insurgence = linguistic innovation spreading by push or imitation. *Sources:* P29. *Limits:* Few cases; method 1 decisions were not robust under bootstrapping; one corpus; P29's printed Bass assignment 'g endo = a, g exo = b rho' swaps the labels given in its own text (agent's reading).
- **INC-HUM-08** [P] Agent-level Bass simulations reproduced P29's Eq. 6 for the exogenous share G: 0.693 at a = b (Eq. 6: ln 2 = 0.693, so equal rates mean exogenous dominance, as P29 notes), 0.359 at P29's Fig. 2 values a = 0.1, b = 0.5 (0.358 well mixed; P29 reports 0.397 on a scale-free network), and 0.079 / 0.990 at the extremes. *Operationalization:* HUM Insurgence = push versus imitation. *Sources:* SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-1#H2;SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-2#H1;SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-2#H2;SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-3#H2. *Limits:* Well-mixed population only.
- **INC-HUM-09** [P] In P28's model with M = 2,000 all 200 runs completed at the threshold with mean latency 57.8 against growth 6.1 (longer latency than at M = 500); at delta = -0.05 only 13.5% of 400 runs escaped within the budget, with mean latency 840: the sharp threshold returns as noise weakens or distance grows. At beta = 0.5 the threshold moved (gamma_c = 0.248) but the delta^(-1/2) law held (slope -0.489). *Operationalization:* HUM Insurgence = latency then S-growth. *Sources:* SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-4#H1;SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-4#H2;SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-5#H1;SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-5#H2;SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-IN-HUM-B/iter-6#H1. *Limits:* M still below P28's 5,000.

- **INC-HUM-10** [PS] P43 defines transformative experiences across disciplines as brief, extraordinary experiences with durable or irreversible effects on self-conception, worldview and identity, marked by epistemic expansion and emotional complexity, emerging suddenly either apparently spontaneously or through elicitors; it also records that transformation can be gradual: James's volitional conversion builds 'piece by piece', and Carel and Kidd see TEs also as the result of cumulative small changes. *Operationalization:* HUM Insurgence = sudden or gradual personal transformation. *Sources:* P43. *Limits:* A conceptual analysis, not an empirical test; reference list not read.
- **INC-HUM-11** [H] Read through P41's fold drift, P43's distinctions have formal counterparts: an 'apparently spontaneous' switch without any parameter change follows Kramers' law (ln mean time linear in 1/sigma^2, r^2 0.997, slope 0.501 vs 0.5); a slow push changes a monostable state gradually (largest step 0.01) but makes a bistable one jump (1.06), and near the cusp the jump is smaller (0.30): cumulative small changes can end in a sudden turn, or not, depending on the system. *Operationalization:* HUM Insurgence = sudden versus gradual transformation (formal reading). *Sources:* SIM-IN-HUM-C/iter-1#V1;SIM-IN-HUM-C/iter-1#H1;SIM-IN-HUM-C/iter-1#H2;SIM-IN-HUM-C/iter-2#V1;SIM-IN-HUM-C/iter-2#H1;SIM-IN-HUM-C/iter-3#V1;SIM-IN-HUM-C/iter-3#H1. *Limits:* The mapping from experience to a one-variable model is the agent's interpretation; Kramers' law is a standard result used unsourced (P41 only names it); the fold itself was already examined in Divergence (P22).
