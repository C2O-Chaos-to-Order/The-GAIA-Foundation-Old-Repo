## Reading of this cycle

## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **INC-NAT-01** [E] In a transcritical bifurcation one equilibrium passes through another and they exchange stability: for dx/dt = r x - x^2 the equilibria are 0 and r, and at r = 0 they collide and swap stabilities, so for r > 0 the zero state becomes unstable and a new nonzero state takes over. *Operationalization:* NAT Insurgence = a new state rising when a threshold is crossed (exchange of stability). *Sources:* P22. *Limits:* One-variable normal form; reading it as 'insurgence' is this track's operationalization, not P22's term.
- **INC-NAT-05** [E] In classical nucleation theory the free energy of a cluster of n molecules has a surface cost growing like n^(2/3) and a cohesive gain growing like n; their competition gives a maximum at the critical nucleation cluster size n*, the nucleation barrier: smaller clusters dissociate, larger ones grow into the new phase. *Operationalization:* NAT Insurgence = a new phase appearing once a critical nucleus forms. *Sources:* P37. *Limits:* P37 is a short textbook summary; its printed derivative has a sign slip (+ instead of - before the cohesive term) and mixes gamma and gamma_0, while the stated n* and G* follow from the correct sign (agent's check).
- **INC-NAT-09** [E] Thermal explosion (autoignition) is a threshold phenomenon: a reacting slab is stable while the Frank-Kamenetskii parameter is below its critical value (0.88 for the planar case under static conditions), and P38 shows that a slab stable on average can still run away if the ambient temperature oscillates with enough amplitude; the critical amplitude rises nearly linearly with frequency and becomes frequency-independent as frequency goes to zero, and runaway can come after several oscillation periods. *Operationalization:* NAT Insurgence = ignition (thermal runaway). *Sources:* P38. *Limits:* P38 treats a zero-order reaction (no reactant consumption) in a 1D slab; P38's parameter estimates for real materials are stated as conservative and based on limited kinetic data.
- **INC-NAT-02** [P] From a seed of 0.001 the state rose to r for r = 0.05, 0.2 and 1 and decayed for r = -0.1; the time to reach r/2 matched the logistic value ln((r - x0)/x0)/r (77.84, 26.47, 6.91 time units) and was 11 times longer at r = 0.05 than at r = 1: near the threshold a new state rises slowly. *Operationalization:* NAT Insurgence = onset from a seed; slow onset near threshold. *Sources:* SIM-IN-NAT-0/iter-1#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-0/iter-1#H1;SIM-IN-NAT-0/iter-1#H2. *Limits:* Deterministic, no noise; RK4 step 0.01.
- **INC-NAT-03** [P] Just past a saddle-node bifurcation (dx/dt = r + x^2, P22's saddle-node type) the passage from x = -10 to +10 took 313.96, 99.15, 31.22 and 9.74 time units for r = 0.0001, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, matching 2 arctan(L/sqrt r)/sqrt r within 0.5%; the log-log slope was -0.503, the r^(-1/2) bottleneck law: the closer to threshold, the longer the new state lingers before breaking out. *Operationalization:* NAT Insurgence = break-out through a bottleneck just past threshold. *Sources:* SIM-IN-NAT-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-A/iter-1#H1. *Limits:* Normal form only; the closed-form time is the agent's integral, not P22's.
- **INC-NAT-04** [P] With noise sigma = 0.005 (larger than the seed 0.001) the transcritical onset at r = 0.05 came at 21.4 time units on average (SE 1.2) instead of the deterministic 77.8, with a coefficient of variation of 0.82; with sigma = 0.0005 the advance was not supported (76.8, SE 1.6), but onset timing still varied (CV 0.30). Noise makes the moment of onset unpredictable; it brings onset forward only when it is large relative to the seed. *Operationalization:* NAT Insurgence = onset timing under noise. *Sources:* SIM-IN-NAT-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-A/iter-2#H1;SIM-IN-NAT-A/iter-2#H2;SIM-IN-NAT-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-A/iter-3#H1;SIM-IN-NAT-A/iter-3#H2. *Limits:* Reflecting boundary at x = 0 pushes small x upward, so part of the strong-noise advance is a boundary effect (disclosed in the SPEC).
- **INC-NAT-06** [P] In a cluster-size random walk built on P37's free energy (A = 3, B = 1: n* = 8, G* = 4 kT) the mean time to grow from 1 to 16 molecules was 217.6 (SE 9.8) against the exact 204.9; across barriers of 1.8 to 6.3 kT the waiting time rose from 10 to 2,157, and ln(time) grew with slope 1.17 in G*: waiting for a nucleus grows roughly exponentially with the barrier. *Operationalization:* NAT Insurgence = rare onset over a barrier. *Sources:* SIM-IN-NAT-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-IN-NAT-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-B/iter-2#H1. *Limits:* Detailed-balance kinetics and the birth-death formula are the agent's, not P37's; slope above 1 reflects small-cluster prefactors.
- **INC-NAT-07** [P] A cluster started exactly at the critical size n* = 27 grew to 54 molecules in 42% of 1,000 walks (SE 1.5%), within 0.15 of one half; at n* = 8 (smoke run) the share was 0.33. The critical nucleus is close to a point of even odds, not a guaranteed take-off. *Operationalization:* NAT Insurgence = critical nucleus as tipping point. *Sources:* SIM-IN-NAT-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-B/iter-3#H1. *Limits:* Discrete small clusters; commitment depends on the end point chosen.
- **INC-NAT-08** [P] Onset advance grows with noise: mean onset 51.9 (sigma 0.002) and 20.0 (sigma 0.005, replicating INA's 21.4) against the deterministic 77.8, with CV 0.58 and 0.76. The bottleneck law replicated with L = 20 down to r = 1e-5 (993.4 vs 993.4; slope -0.500). *Operationalization:* NAT Insurgence = onset under noise; bottleneck (replications). *Sources:* SIM-IN-NAT-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-B/iter-4#H1;SIM-IN-NAT-B/iter-4#H2;SIM-IN-NAT-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-B/iter-5#H1;SIM-IN-NAT-B/iter-5#H2;SIM-IN-NAT-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-B/iter-6#H1. *Limits:* Reflecting boundary as in INA.
- **INC-NAT-10** [P] Re-implementing P38's Eq. 1-2: the exact static critical parameter is 0.8785 (P38: 0.88) and the simulated slab first runs away at 0.8784; with oscillating boundary temperature (delta = 0.2, Ar = 0.04, omega = 1) the critical amplitude was 1.915, close to P38's Fig. 7 bracket (bounded at 1.931802, runaway at 2.0). *Operationalization:* NAT Insurgence = ignition threshold reproduced. *Sources:* SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-1#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-1#H1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-2#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-2#H1. *Limits:* Semi-implicit scheme and a threshold runaway criterion (agent's) instead of P38's Crank-Nicolson and inflection criterion; initial condition theta = 0 assumed; 1.915 lies 0.017 below P38's last bounded amplitude, a small disagreement probably due to these choices.
- **INC-NAT-11** [P] All five qualitative results of P38 that were tested reproduced: critical amplitude rising linearly with frequency (1.12 to 2.83 for omega 2 to 10, r^2 = 0.9996), falling as delta approaches the static threshold (1.58, 0.94, 0.55, 0.23 for delta 0.2 to 0.8), falling with the Arrhenius number (0.40 to 0.23), nearly frequency-independent at low frequency (0.79-0.82 for omega 0.05-0.2), and P38's new regime of runaway after oscillations (delta 0.6, omega 10, A0 2.8: runaway at tau 1.77 after two maxima). *Operationalization:* NAT Insurgence = ignition by fluctuation; delayed outbreak. *Sources:* SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-3#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-3#H1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-4#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-4#H1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-5#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-5#H1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-6#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-6#H1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-7#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-7#H1. *Limits:* Same scheme caveats as INC-NAT-10; only planar symmetry and first-kind boundary conditions.

Nothing new beyond confirmation. *Operationalization:* NAT Insurgence = nucleation and noisy onset (replications). *Sources:* SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-8#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-8#H1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-9#V1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-9#H1;SIM-IN-NAT-C/iter-9#H2. *Limits:* Replications only.
