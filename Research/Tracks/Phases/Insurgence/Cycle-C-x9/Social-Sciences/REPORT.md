## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **INC-SOC-03** [E] P25 states that it draws on Granovetter's threshold models of collective behaviour: each individual has a personal threshold and joins collective action, such as a riot or a strike, if the number of people already participating exceeds it. *Operationalization:* SOC Insurgence = joining once enough others have joined. *Sources:* P25. *Limits:* Secondary report of Granovetter (1978), which was not read; the claim is about P25's text, classed interpretive because it reports a theoretical stance.
- **INC-SOC-01** [P] In P25's agent-based model of violent revolutions (citizens, revolutionaries, police on a 40 x 40 torus) spontaneous riots by the poorest citizens precede an organised uprising that starts when rebels outnumber police by a threshold n; the same start can end in successful revolution, anarchy or a return to the status quo, and revolutions arrive as 'random events' that cannot be anticipated, in line with Kuran's observation that most historical revolutions were unanticipated. *Operationalization:* SOC Insurgence = a threshold-triggered uprising with multiple possible outcomes. *Sources:* P25. *Limits:* Model results, not observations; historical parallels (Tunisia, Syria, Bahrain) are P25's illustrations; parameters mostly taken from Epstein's civil-violence model.
- **INC-SOC-02** [P] P25's design counts reproduce (8 x 9 x 4 x 60 = 17,280 runs; 480 per cell; 2,160 per n), and with its Table 2 coefficients all four link functions put the peak of k(0.9, 0.3, n) inside [1.0, 1.1] (1.03-1.09), as P25 states. The fall after the peak is not 'marked' under every link: from peak to n = 1.4 k drops by 0.16 (complementary log-log) but only 0.04-0.09 under the others, below the 0.05 threshold for the linear model (not supported). *Operationalization:* SOC Insurgence = optimal uprising threshold. *Sources:* SIM-IN-SOC-0/iter-1#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-0/iter-1#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-0/iter-1#H2. *Limits:* Uses rounded printed coefficients; 0.05 threshold is the agent's reading of 'markedly'.
- **INC-SOC-04** [P] In a threshold cascade (join when the number already participating reaches one's threshold) thresholds 0..N-1 gave a full cascade for N = 100 and 1,000, and raising one threshold from 1 to 2 stopped it at a single person. With normal thresholds (mean N/4) the final size went from near 0 to near N as the spread grew (N = 100: 15% at sd 12, 47% at 13, 75% at 14; N = 1,000: 21% at sd 120, 54% at 125, 85% at 130); the largest jump between adjacent spreads was 0.31 and 0.33. More dispersed thresholds (more low-threshold instigators) made a total cascade more likely. *Operationalization:* SOC Insurgence = threshold cascade. *Sources:* SIM-IN-SOC-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-A/iter-1#H2;SIM-IN-SOC-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-A/iter-2#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-A/iter-2#H2. *Limits:* Agent's toy; the sim uses 'reaches' where P25's wording is 'exceeds'; the uniform and one-change cases are constructions, not numbers from P25; jumps only just above the pre-set 0.3.
- **INC-SOC-05** [P] At another design cell (p = 0.3, r = 0.1) P25's four fitted links peak at n = 1.03-1.09, as at (0.9, 0.3); the fall by more than 0.05 to n = 1.4 under every link is again not supported (linear link 0.036). Because p and r enter P25's index additively, the optimal rebel-to-police ratio is a property of the specification and cannot vary with p or r. *Operationalization:* SOC Insurgence = optimal threshold of P25's fitted model. *Sources:* SIM-IN-SOC-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-A/iter-3#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-A/iter-3#H2. *Limits:* Foreknown by reasoning before the run (disclosed); says nothing about P25's underlying simulations.
- **INC-SOC-06** [P] P31 extends Moro's model (P25) to nonviolent revolutions, calibrates it to all 110 mass nonviolent campaigns of 1945-2014 that aimed to overthrow a national government, and finds that activists who concentrate protest near the regime's pillars of support outperform rapid mobilisation in producing defections even when the campaign is small: average success 48% with no strategy, 68% moving to the nearest pillar, 88% moving to the least loyal pillar. *Operationalization:* SOC Insurgence = a movement winning by inducing pillar defections. *Sources:* P31. *Limits:* Model results, not observed effects; the least-loyal strategy assumes intelligence about loyalties; P31 notes regime counter-strategies are not modelled. P31's text describes Percent Committed with the definition of its complement (Percent Fickle, per its S1 Table).
- **INC-SOC-07** [P] P31 reports that participation size is a less reliable predictor of success than thought: Bahrain's 2011 uprising mobilised up to 7% of the population and was defeated, Iraq's 1991 protests (over 8%) failed, while Mongolia's 1989-1990 movement succeeded with under 1%. *Operationalization:* SOC Insurgence = success not set by size alone. *Sources:* P31. *Limits:* Illustrative cases from P31's introduction (with P31's citations), not a systematic test.
- **INC-SOC-08** [P] In the agent's pillar toy, concentrating pressure on 10 of 50 pillars beat spreading it: at movement strength 0.1 spreading never succeeded while both concentrated strategies always did, and at 0.5 all succeeded (as P31 finds for large movements). Targeting the least loyal pillars gave no measurable gain over random concentration (0.103 vs 0.100 at strength 0.02; 1.0 vs 1.0 at 0.1; the strict ordering was not supported at 0.1). In the toy, concentration does the work that P31 attributes partly to information about loyalties. *Operationalization:* SOC Insurgence = concentration versus spread of pressure. *Sources:* SIM-IN-SOC-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-B/iter-2#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-B/iter-4#H1. *Limits:* Toy is the agent's; outcomes are near 0 or 1 (degenerate regime partly retuned after one smoke run, disclosed).
- **INC-SOC-09** [P] Threshold cascades replicated (uniform thresholds full, one change stops at 1), but the 'sudden jump' criterion (> 0.3 between adjacent spreads) was not supported on the new seed (0.298) nor with mean threshold 40 (0.223, jump near sd 22-26): INA's suddenness finding was marginal and does not replicate robustly. *Operationalization:* SOC Insurgence = threshold cascade (replication). *Sources:* SIM-IN-SOC-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-B/iter-5#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-B/iter-5#H2;SIM-IN-SOC-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-B/iter-6#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-B/iter-6#H2. *Limits:* Agent's toy; 100 draws per spread.
- **INC-SOC-10** [P] P39 finds that wars become large mainly by escalating after they start, not by starting big or lasting long: year-to-year escalation factors are symmetric and heavy-tailed (double Pareto, alpha 2.1), statistically alike in civil and interstate wars, and a model with unconditioned escalation reproduces Richardson's law for interstate wars, while large civil wars tend to de-escalate (around 500 deaths per year), limiting their size. *Operationalization:* SOC Insurgence = an armed conflict escalating to a large war. *Sources:* P39. *Limits:* Annual battle deaths only (civilian deaths excluded); the abundance of no-change years is partly a data artefact (P39); P39 does not explain initial sizes.
- **INC-SOC-11** [P] Re-implementing P39's Eq. 2 (100,000 wars per set): with escalation the tail exponent was 1.71 against 1.99 without (eta = 2, q = 0.47), and about 10 per 1,000 vs 2 per 1,000 wars exceeded a million deaths; without escalation, heavy-tailed starts alone (eta = 1.5) gave 1.50, Richardson-like as P39 says; with short wars (q = 0.7) escalation changed the tail little (1.85 vs 1.99). *Operationalization:* SOC Insurgence = escalation producing large wars. *Sources:* SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-1#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-1#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-2#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-2#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-5#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-5#H1. *Limits:* Double Pareto parameterisation and clamping at x_min are the agent's readings; tail exponent by MLE above 7,061 is a cruder measure than P39's Anderson-Darling comparison.
- **INC-SOC-12** [P] Three checks were not supported: at eta = 1.7 escalation lowered the tail exponent by only 0.12 (1.70 vs 1.57; 0.2 expected); with long wars (q = 0.3) the tail was 1.57, not heavier than Richardson's 1.53 as P39's Fig. S3E suggests; and P39's stated per-year risks (about 10% doubling, 1% tenfold) were not reproduced from alpha = 2.1 with 44% no-change years under either reading (13.1%/2.2% or 6.5%/0.2%). *Operationalization:* SOC Insurgence = escalation (checks not supported). *Sources:* SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-3#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-3#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-4#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-4#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-6#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-6#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-6#H2. *Limits:* May reflect the agent's parameterisation rather than P39; P39 may compute the risks from the empirical distribution; SI not read.
- **INC-SOC-14** [P] Replications: pillar toy small-movement ordering again (0, 0.09, 0.11); the cascade jump was again not supported (largest jump 0.291, below 0.3), now on three seeds: INA's 'sudden' cascade is marginal. *Operationalization:* SOC Insurgence = replications. *Sources:* SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-8#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-8#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-9#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-9#H1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-9#H2. *Limits:* Replications only.
- **INC-SOC-13** [H] Adding mean reversion of escalation toward 500 deaths per year (kappa = 0.3, agent's stand-in for P39 Fig. 2C) made the tail markedly lighter than unconditioned escalation (1.85 vs 1.52; share above a million deaths 0.3% vs 4.1%), in line with P39's account of why civil wars do not follow Richardson's law. *Operationalization:* SOC Insurgence = self-limiting civil wars. *Sources:* SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-7#V1;SIM-IN-SOC-C/iter-7#H1. *Limits:* Kappa chosen by the agent, not fitted.
