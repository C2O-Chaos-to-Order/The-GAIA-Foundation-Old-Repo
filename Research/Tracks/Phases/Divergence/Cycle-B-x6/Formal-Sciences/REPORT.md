## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **DVB-FOR-01** [E] In dynamical-systems usage, the global Lyapunov exponent is taken to represent the average rate of divergence of neighbouring trajectories, and the most popular candidate definition of chaos (Chaos_lambda) is a positive global Lyapunov exponent. *Operationalization:* Divergence = exponential separation of nearby trajectories. *Sources:* P09. *Limits:* P09 also reports objections: Lyapunov exponents are asymptotic quantities and Chaos_lambda is one of several definitions.
- **DVB-FOR-02** [E] There is no consensus on a precise definition of chaotic behaviour: mathematicians often prefer one definition and scientists another, so 'divergence of trajectories' is one operationalization among competitors, not the definition of chaos. *Operationalization:* Competing definitions of chaos (sensitive dependence vs topological definitions). *Sources:* P09. *Limits:* Reference-work summary of the debate.
- **DVB-FOR-08** [E] Divergence of trajectories is not sufficient for chaos: P09 notes that the map x_{n+1} = 1.1 x_n has only unstable, aperiodic orbits that never return near the start, so a definition based on instability and aperiodicity would count it as chaotic, yet it does not exhibit chaotic behaviour. *Operationalization:* Divergence (sensitive dependence) vs chaos. *Sources:* P09. *Limits:* Definitional point; P09 reports no consensus definition of chaos.
- **DVB-FOR-09** [E] In chaotic dynamics, stretching and folding mechanisms cause some trajectories to converge rapidly while others diverge rapidly; P09 also states that the Lyapunov definition of chaos is trivially false for finite uncertainties in real systems and calls the idea that chaotic systems are unpredictable a myth. *Operationalization:* Divergence co-occurring with local convergence. *Sources:* P09. *Limits:* Reference-work summary of a debated literature.
- **DVB-FOR-10** [E] Kullback-Leibler divergence is non-negative, not symmetric, zero only when the distributions match and possibly infinite; it equals the negative logarithm of the average multinomial likelihood of infinite data from p under the model q, so as observed frequencies diverge from the model the average likelihood falls toward zero. *Operationalization:* Divergence = statistical divergence D(p||q). *Sources:* P16. *Limits:* An expository arXiv note (not peer reviewed); the derivation uses Stirling's approximation.
- **DVB-FOR-03** [P] For the logistic map the computed Lyapunov exponent at r = 4 was 0.69313 (ln 2 = 0.69315) and negative at r = 3.2; two orbits started 1e-10 apart at r = 4 separated beyond 0.1 after a median 31 steps, close to the predicted ln(1e9)/ln 2 = 29.9. *Operationalization:* Divergence = trajectory separation rate. *Sources:* SIM-DV-FOR-0/iter-1#V1;SIM-DV-FOR-0/iter-1#V2;SIM-DV-FOR-0/iter-1#H1. *Limits:* Code and analytic check of a textbook map; it shows what 'trajectory divergence' means formally and nothing about any physical or social system.
- **DVB-FOR-04** [P] For 1,000 random pairs of distributions on 10 points, Kullback-Leibler divergence was never negative, was zero for identical distributions, matched its Monte Carlo estimate within 4 standard errors in 50 of 50 pairs, and was asymmetric (median relative difference between D(p||q) and D(q||p) 0.20). *Operationalization:* Divergence = asymmetric statistical dissimilarity (not a distance). *Sources:* SIM-DV-FOR-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-DV-FOR-A/iter-1#V2;SIM-DV-FOR-A/iter-1#V3;SIM-DV-FOR-A/iter-1#H1. *Limits:* Code and analytic check only; no source on KL divergence was read in this track, so no Established claim is made about its literature.
- **DVB-FOR-05** [P] Harmonic partial sums tracked ln n + gamma within 1/(2n) up to n = 100,000 and first exceeded 10 only at n = 12,367, while the p = 2 and alternating harmonic series converged within their bounds: a divergent series can grow extremely slowly. *Operationalization:* Divergence = partial sums without a finite limit. *Sources:* SIM-DV-FOR-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-DV-FOR-A/iter-2#V2;SIM-DV-FOR-A/iter-2#V3;SIM-DV-FOR-A/iter-2#H1. *Limits:* Deterministic floating-point check of standard results; H1 was predictable from general knowledge.
- **DVB-FOR-06** [P] For F = (x^3 + y, x y^2, z x^2) on the unit cube, a finite-difference divergence matched the analytic one to 1e-8, and the Monte Carlo volume integral of div F (1.8380, SE 0.0034) matched the outward flux 11/6 = 1.8333, as the divergence theorem requires. *Operationalization:* Divergence = net outflow per unit volume (div F). *Sources:* SIM-DV-FOR-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-DV-FOR-A/iter-3#V2. *Limits:* One polynomial field; a code check of the operator sense only.
- **DVB-FOR-11** [P] P16's relation held numerically: for counts from p, -(1/n) log L(c|q) approached D(p||q) with errors 0.12, 0.018, 0.0021, 0.00015 nats at n = 100 to 100,000 (K = 6) and 0.69 to 0.0026 (K = 50); the likelihood-ratio rate matched D within 4 SE at every n. With 50 categories the approximation needs about ten times more data for the same accuracy. *Operationalization:* Divergence = KL as likelihood penalty. *Sources:* SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-1#H2;SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-2#H1;SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-2#H2. *Limits:* One random (p, q) pair per K.
- **DVB-FOR-12** [P] The map x -> 1.1 x has exponent ln 1.1 = 0.095 but grows without bound and never brings nearby points closer; the logistic map at r = 4 (exponent 0.693) stays in [0, 1] and nearby separations shrank in 16.1% of steps, matching the analytic share of time spent where |f'| < 1 (0.1609; observed 0.1607). All finite-time exponents over 20,000 steps stayed positive. *Operationalization:* Divergence with folding vs divergence without it. *Sources:* SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-3#V2;SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-3#V3;SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-4#H1;SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-4#H2. *Limits:* One-dimensional maps.
- **DVB-FOR-13** [P] In the Lorenz system the vector-field divergence is -13.67 everywhere (phase volumes contract), yet the largest Lyapunov exponent was +0.90 at rho = 28 (nearby trajectories diverge) and -0.40 at rho = 14 (they converge): the operator sense and the trajectory sense of 'divergence' are different quantities. *Operationalization:* Divergence (operator) vs divergence (trajectories). *Sources:* SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-5#H1;SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-DV-FOR-B/iter-6#H1. *Limits:* No Lorenz source read; RK4, dt 0.01, T 500.
- **DVB-FOR-07** [H] The formal senses now covered (separation of trajectories, partial sums without a limit, asymmetric statistical dissimilarity, net outflow of a field) share a word, not a definition; this cycle found no common formal criterion that would make them one concept. *Operationalization:* Competing formal definitions of divergence. *Sources:* SIM-DV-FOR-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-DV-FOR-A/iter-2#H1. *Limits:* Absence of a common core in four examples is not proof that none exists; no source on the history of the term was read.
