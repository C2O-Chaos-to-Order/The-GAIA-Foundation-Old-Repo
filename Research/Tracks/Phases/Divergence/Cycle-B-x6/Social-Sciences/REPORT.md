Phase report: Divergence (`../../README.md`). Definitions and competing definitions: `../DEFINITIONS.md`. Labels: **E** Established · **P** Plausible · **PS** Philosophical/Spiritual · **H** Hypothetical. GAIA's Phase axis is a framework category (PS), kept separate from the findings.

## Reading of this cycle
x6 adds no source. The SIEM approximation still misses P03's no-event level, noise keeps opinions spread without erasing clustering, and a statistical toy shows that catch-up slopes can appear without any change in dispersion.

## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **DVB-SOC-01** [E] In opinion-dynamics models, bounded assimilative influence (only individuals with similar opinions influence each other) makes opinions fragment into two or more converging clusters, while adding noise broadens the distribution again. *Operationalization:* Divergence = fragmentation of a population into opinion clusters. *Sources:* P03. *Limits:* Describes model behaviour, not observed societies.
- **DVB-SOC-04** [E] Analysing World Values Survey data from 1981 to 2022 (n = 406,185; 76 national cultures), Jackson and Medvedev find global value divergence, sharpest for values emphasising tolerance and self-expression, especially between high-income Western countries and the rest of the world. *Operationalization:* Divergence = rising cross-country SD of item means (value variation) and rising distance from the global median (value distinctiveness). *Sources:* P11. *Limits:* 40 items only; samples not perfectly representative (e.g. indigenous populations); the period (1981-2022) may be historically specific, as the authors note.
- **DVB-SOC-05** [E] In the same data, geographic proximity became an increasingly strong correlate of value similarity (standardised effect 0.16 at the first timepoint, 0.35 at the seventh): values diverged globally but converged regionally. *Operationalization:* Divergence across regions with convergence within regions. *Sources:* P11. *Limits:* Correlational; country-pair regressions with cross-classified random effects.
- **DVB-SOC-02** [P] Condie and Condie propose, with an agent-based bounded-confidence model, that stochastic external events can sustain polarisation even when all interpersonal interactions are noisy and assimilative, because events act on much of the population at once. *Operationalization:* Divergence = persistent polarisation driven by shared external events. *Sources:* P03. *Limits:* A model-based possibility proof; empirical calibration against real opinion data is limited.
- **DVB-SOC-03** [P] In a noise-free Deffuant model (500 agents), confidence bound 0.5 gave consensus in 10/10 replicates and bound 0.15 gave three major clusters in 10/10, with the mean opinion conserved exactly. *Operationalization:* Divergence = number of separated opinion clusters. *Sources:* SIM-DV-SOC-0/iter-1#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-0/iter-1#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-0/iter-1#H2. *Limits:* No network, no events, no noise; cluster counting rule is a convention.
- **DVB-SOC-06** [P] GDP per capita was the only geopolitical variable that significantly predicted a country's value distinctiveness; the authors suggest rising wealth may be responsible for worldwide divergence but state that more research is needed to determine the causal relationship, if any. *Operationalization:* Candidate driver of value divergence. *Sources:* P11. *Limits:* The wealth effect varied by continent (non-significant in Africa and Asia); causal direction unknown.
- **DVB-SOC-07** [P] An independent approximation of P03's event model reproduced its central claim only in part. At confidence threshold 0.4, events raised long-run conflict from 0.27 to 0.58 (+0.31, supported) and the no-event level matched P03's Eq. 6 within 0.1, but the event level exceeded Eq. 6 (0.45) by more than 0.1 (not supported). At threshold 0.2, opinions stayed fragmented without events (conflict 0.55 vs Eq. 6 0.26, not supported) and events added only 0.01 (not supported). *Operationalization:* Divergence = event-sustained polarisation (SD of opinions). *Sources:* SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-1#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-1#H3;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-2#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-2#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-2#H3. *Limits:* Not P03's code: partner count k = 4, symmetric certainty drift and directed random partners are our choices; 1,000 steps; 5 replicates. The mismatch may reflect our implementation rather than P03.
- **DVB-SOC-08** [P] In a synthetic 60-country, 6-region panel with growing region effects, P11's value-variation measure rose overall (slope +0.002 per wave, after an initial dip from 0.105 to 0.085) while within-region variation fell from 0.102 to 0.042: global divergence and regional convergence can coexist and both measures register it. *Operationalization:* Global vs regional value variation. *Sources:* SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-3#H1. *Limits:* The regional drift was built into the generator; this shows the measures behave as intended, not why real values diverged.
- **DVB-SOC-09** [P] With 8 links per step our SIEM re-implementation again did not match P03's Eq. 6 without events (check not supported at both eps) (conflict 0.54 vs 0.26 at eps 0.2; 0.32 vs 0.20 at eps 0.4) while event runs were 0.60 and 0.65 (Eq. 6: 0.60, 0.45). The event check was not supported at eps 0.4 (0.65 vs 0.45); events added little conflict at eps 0.2 (0.06, 'events add > 0.1' not supported) and a lot at eps 0.4 (0.33). The mismatch is a property of our approximation, not evidence against P03. *Operationalization:* Divergence = sustained polarisation. *Sources:* SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-1#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-1#H3;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-2#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-2#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-2#H3. *Limits:* Not P03's code; k, partner choice and certainty drift are our interpretations; one replicate at eps 0.4 without events stayed polarised (0.61).
- **DVB-SOC-10** [P] In a bounded-confidence model with opinion redraws, small noise (0.001 per interaction) left opinion spread close to the noise-free value (0.240 vs 0.227 at eps 0.2; 0.162 vs 0.132 at eps 0.3), while heavy noise (0.1) raised it but not to the uniform value 0.289 (0.262 and 0.201; the pre-registered 'near uniform' check was not supported in either run): noise maintains dispersion without removing homophily's pull. *Operationalization:* Divergence = dispersion maintained by noise. *Sources:* SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-3#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-6#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-6#H2. *Limits:* Noise rule is the agent's choice (unsourced); SD only.
- **DVB-SOC-11** [P] A negative catch-up slope does not show convergence: in a stationary AR(1) toy the growth-on-initial-level slope was -0.64 (expected rho^T - 1 = -0.64) while cross-unit dispersion stayed constant (ratio 1.001); only when initial dispersion was twice the stationary value did dispersion actually shrink (ratio 0.59) with the same slope. Divergence and convergence claims should be stated in dispersion terms (as P11 does with SDs) rather than inferred from slopes. *Operationalization:* Divergence/convergence = change in dispersion, not regression slope. *Sources:* SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-4#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-4#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-5#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-5#H2. *Limits:* Statistical toy, not an economic model; no source on beta/sigma convergence read.
