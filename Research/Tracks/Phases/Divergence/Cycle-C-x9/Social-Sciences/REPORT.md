# Divergence in the Social Sciences — Cycle-C-x9 (DVC)

Phase report: Divergence (`../../README.md`). Definitions and competing definitions: `../DEFINITIONS.md`. Labels: **E** Established · **P** Plausible · **PS** Philosophical/Spiritual · **H** Hypothetical. GAIA's Phase axis is a framework category (PS), kept separate from the findings.

## Reading of this cycle
x9 sources economic divergence (P19): divergence big time overall, convergence inside the rich club; a toy shows that selecting today's rich countries manufactures convergence; P19's printed 1870 ratio does not reproduce.

## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **DVC-SOC-01** [E] In opinion-dynamics models, bounded assimilative influence (only individuals with similar opinions influence each other) makes opinions fragment into two or more converging clusters, while adding noise broadens the distribution again. *Operationalization:* Divergence = fragmentation of a population into opinion clusters. *Sources:* P03. *Limits:* Describes model behaviour, not observed societies.
- **DVC-SOC-04** [E] Analysing World Values Survey data from 1981 to 2022 (n = 406,185; 76 national cultures), Jackson and Medvedev find global value divergence, sharpest for values emphasising tolerance and self-expression, especially between high-income Western countries and the rest of the world. *Operationalization:* Divergence = rising cross-country SD of item means (value variation) and rising distance from the global median (value distinctiveness). *Sources:* P11. *Limits:* 40 items only; samples not perfectly representative (e.g. indigenous populations); the period (1981-2022) may be historically specific, as the authors note.
- **DVC-SOC-05** [E] In the same data, geographic proximity became an increasingly strong correlate of value similarity (standardised effect 0.16 at the first timepoint, 0.35 at the seventh): values diverged globally but converged regionally. *Operationalization:* Divergence across regions with convergence within regions. *Sources:* P11. *Limits:* Correlational; country-pair regressions with cross-classified random effects.
- **DVC-SOC-12** [E] Within the 17 'advanced capitalist' countries, incomes converged: the standard deviation of log per capita income fell from .41 in 1870 to .27 in 1960 to .11 in 1994 (Maddison data, as reported in P19). *Operationalization:* Club convergence. *Sources:* P19. *Limits:* P19 stresses that this sample is selected on being rich now, which makes convergence 'almost tautological' (De Long 1988).
- **DVC-SOC-14** [E] P19 reports that among developing countries in 1960-1990 growth ranged from -2.7% to +6.9% a year: 11 of 108 grew faster than 4.2%, 16 had negative growth, and 81 of 103 decelerated; it finds 'no obvious reversal in divergence'. *Operationalization:* Heterogeneous divergence among poorer countries. *Sources:* P19. *Limits:* Penn World Tables 5.6 era data; countries without statistics drop out (P19 notes this bias).
- **DVC-SOC-02** [P] Condie and Condie propose, with an agent-based bounded-confidence model, that stochastic external events can sustain polarisation even when all interpersonal interactions are noisy and assimilative, because events act on much of the population at once. *Operationalization:* Divergence = persistent polarisation driven by shared external events. *Sources:* P03. *Limits:* A model-based possibility proof; empirical calibration against real opinion data is limited.
- **DVC-SOC-03** [P] In a noise-free Deffuant model (500 agents), confidence bound 0.5 gave consensus in 10/10 replicates and bound 0.15 gave three major clusters in 10/10, with the mean opinion conserved exactly. *Operationalization:* Divergence = number of separated opinion clusters. *Sources:* SIM-DV-SOC-0/iter-1#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-0/iter-1#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-0/iter-1#H2. *Limits:* No network, no events, no noise; cluster counting rule is a convention.
- **DVC-SOC-06** [P] GDP per capita was the only geopolitical variable that significantly predicted a country's value distinctiveness; the authors suggest rising wealth may be responsible for worldwide divergence but state that more research is needed to determine the causal relationship, if any. *Operationalization:* Candidate driver of value divergence. *Sources:* P11. *Limits:* The wealth effect varied by continent (non-significant in Africa and Asia); causal direction unknown.
- **DVC-SOC-07** [P] An independent approximation of P03's event model reproduced its central claim only in part. At confidence threshold 0.4, events raised long-run conflict from 0.27 to 0.58 (+0.31, supported) and the no-event level matched P03's Eq. 6 within 0.1, but the event level exceeded Eq. 6 (0.45) by more than 0.1 (not supported). At threshold 0.2, opinions stayed fragmented without events (conflict 0.55 vs Eq. 6 0.26, not supported) and events added only 0.01 (not supported). *Operationalization:* Divergence = event-sustained polarisation (SD of opinions). *Sources:* SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-1#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-1#H3;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-2#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-2#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-2#H3. *Limits:* Not P03's code: partner count k = 4, symmetric certainty drift and directed random partners are our choices; 1,000 steps; 5 replicates. The mismatch may reflect our implementation rather than P03.
- **DVC-SOC-08** [P] In a synthetic 60-country, 6-region panel with growing region effects, P11's value-variation measure rose overall (slope +0.002 per wave, after an initial dip from 0.105 to 0.085) while within-region variation fell from 0.102 to 0.042: global divergence and regional convergence can coexist and both measures register it. *Operationalization:* Global vs regional value variation. *Sources:* SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-A/iter-3#H1. *Limits:* The regional drift was built into the generator; this shows the measures behave as intended, not why real values diverged.
- **DVC-SOC-09** [P] With 8 links per step our SIEM re-implementation again did not match P03's Eq. 6 without events (check not supported at both eps) (conflict 0.54 vs 0.26 at eps 0.2; 0.32 vs 0.20 at eps 0.4) while event runs were 0.60 and 0.65 (Eq. 6: 0.60, 0.45). The event check was not supported at eps 0.4 (0.65 vs 0.45); events added little conflict at eps 0.2 (0.06, 'events add > 0.1' not supported) and a lot at eps 0.4 (0.33). The mismatch is a property of our approximation, not evidence against P03. *Operationalization:* Divergence = sustained polarisation. *Sources:* SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-1#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-1#H3;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-2#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-2#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-2#H3. *Limits:* Not P03's code; k, partner choice and certainty drift are our interpretations; one replicate at eps 0.4 without events stayed polarised (0.61).
- **DVC-SOC-10** [P] In a bounded-confidence model with opinion redraws, small noise (0.001 per interaction) left opinion spread close to the noise-free value (0.240 vs 0.227 at eps 0.2; 0.162 vs 0.132 at eps 0.3), while heavy noise (0.1) raised it but not to the uniform value 0.289 (0.262 and 0.201; the pre-registered 'near uniform' check was not supported in either run): noise maintains dispersion without removing homophily's pull. *Operationalization:* Divergence = dispersion maintained by noise. *Sources:* SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-3#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-6#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-6#H2. *Limits:* Noise rule is the agent's choice (unsourced); SD only.
- **DVC-SOC-11** [P] A negative catch-up slope does not show convergence: in a stationary AR(1) toy the growth-on-initial-level slope was -0.64 (expected rho^T - 1 = -0.64) while cross-unit dispersion stayed constant (ratio 1.001); only when initial dispersion was twice the stationary value did dispersion actually shrink (ratio 0.59) with the same slope. Divergence and convergence claims should be stated in dispersion terms (as P11 does with SDs) rather than inferred from slopes. *Operationalization:* Divergence/convergence = change in dispersion, not regression slope. *Sources:* SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-4#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-4#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-5#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-B/iter-5#H2. *Limits:* Statistical toy, not an economic model; no source on beta/sigma convergence read.
- **DVC-SOC-13** [P] Pritchett estimates that from 1870 to 1990 the ratio of richest to poorest country incomes rose from 8.7 to 45 and the SD of log per capita income from .51 to 1.06, concluding that 'the last 150 years have seen divergence, big time' and that divergence 'is the dominant feature of modern economic history'. *Operationalization:* SOC Divergence = widening cross-country income dispersion. *Sources:* P19. *Limits:* 1870 values are backcast from a P$250 lower bound; data end in 1990/1994; later convergence (e.g. post-2000 growth in poorer countries) is outside this source.
- **DVC-SOC-15** [P] Selection alone manufactures convergence: with no true convergence, the 17 countries richest at the end showed a catch-up slope of -0.74 (full sample -0.005); with smaller shocks -0.42; with real convergence (beta -0.02, expected slope -0.215, observed -0.217) selection exaggerated it to -0.80; selecting 60 still gave -0.53. Full-sample dispersion widened 1.64x under pure random growth, but not supported (1.09x) with small shocks. *Operationalization:* Measurement of convergence vs divergence. *Sources:* SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-1#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-1#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-1#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-2#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-2#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-2#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-3#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-3#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-3#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-4#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-4#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-4#H2. *Limits:* Random-walk toy, not P19's data; the artefact is the one P19 attributes to De Long.
- **DVC-SOC-16** [P] A converging rich club (within-club SD ratio 0.64) coexisted with overall divergence (total SD ratio 1.77); with equal mean growth the rest's heterogeneity alone still widened dispersion (1.57), but a growth gap with homogeneous shocks gave only 1.31 (not supported >1.5). The DVB beta/sigma result replicated with 125 units (beta -0.640 vs -0.642 expected; SD ratio 1.01). *Operationalization:* Club convergence with global divergence. *Sources:* SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-6#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-6#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-6#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-7#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-7#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-7#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-8#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-8#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-8#H2;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-9#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-9#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-9#H2. *Limits:* Toy parameters, not estimates.
- **DVC-SOC-17** [P] P19's 1960 and 1990 richest/poorest ratios reproduce from its printed levels (9895/257 = 38.50; 18054/399 = 45.25), but the printed 1870 ratio 8.7 does not (2063/250 = 8.25; hypothesis not supported), and the US 1870-1960 factor from Table 2 is 4.80, not 'about four-fold' within 0.5 (not supported). The discrepancies are small and do not change P19's conclusion. *Operationalization:* Source consistency check. *Sources:* SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-5#V1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-5#H1;SIM-DV-SOC-C/iter-5#H2. *Limits:* Table digits from an OCR-like conversion, though the Figure 1 inset also prints 8.7; P19 may have used a different 1870 comparison (e.g. its truncation variant).
