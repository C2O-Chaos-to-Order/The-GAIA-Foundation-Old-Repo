- **ASA-APP-01** [P] Among 105 travellers treated for high-altitude illness at Mustang district hospital (Nepal), 74% had acute mountain sickness (61% mild, 36% moderate, 3% severe); in a Bayesian ordered logistic model of severity (None, Mild, Moderate or Severe), faster ascent rate (km/day) was associated with greater severity (coefficient 1.14, odds ratio 3.13) and smoking with less (odds ratio 0.16). P88's counterfactual says a 1-day Pokhara-Muktinath trip (2978 m gain) doubles the risk of moderate or severe AMS compared with 3 days. *Operationalization:* APP Ascendence = ascent to altitude and ascent rate. *Sources:* P88. *Limits:* Hospital register sample (P88 notes selection by Thorong La and airlift of severe cases); one site; credible intervals reported as 99% for headline effects. P88 calls the 2.069 counterfactual 'odds' in Fig. 2 and 'risk' in the abstract; see AS0-APP-02.
- **ASA-APP-03** [P] In 770 young lowland men ascending to 3650 m by air (fast, n 424) or by bus over two days (slow, n 346), and then by bus to 4400 m, fast ascent was a risk factor for AMS at 3650 m (adjusted OR 1.52 old Lake Louise score, 1.47 new) but protective at 4400 m (OR 0.67, 0.63); in 272 propensity-matched pairs AMS went from 37.9% to 35.7% (fast) and from 26.5% to 44.5% (slow). *Operationalization:* APP Ascendence = ascent protocol and AMS. *Sources:* P91. *Limits:* Men aged 18-45 only; self-reported symptoms; ascent mode not randomised, and the fast cohort had longer at altitude before the second ascent (P91's own limitation). Slips: ethics approval numbers PLA967-GC2023-021 vs -068; '34,400 m'; Table 4 'Under high school 1.88 (0.56, 1.36)'; percentages audited in ASA-APP-05.
- **ASA-APP-04** [P] With only the ascent-rate coefficient uncertain (N(1.14, 0.40)), the 1-day/3-day risk ratio has mean 1.97, median 2.03 and 90% interval 1.62-2.14, so P88's 2.069 lies between the point value (2.095) and these averages; the pre-registered closeness of the mean and of the ratio of means (1.96) to 2.069 was not supported. P(moderate or severe) falls with every extra ascent day for all four profiles (baseline 0.87, 0.56, 0.42, 0.35, 0.31 for 1-5 days), but a smoker's 1-day risk (0.53) is not below a non-smoker's 3-day risk (0.42): not supported. *Operationalization:* APP Ascendence = ascent rate and AMS risk. *Sources:* SIM-AS-APP-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-AS-APP-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-AS-APP-A/iter-1#H2;SIM-AS-APP-A/iter-1#H3;SIM-AS-APP-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-AS-APP-A/iter-2#H1;SIM-AS-APP-A/iter-2#H2;SIM-AS-APP-A/iter-2#H3. *Limits:* P88's full posterior is not available; the curve uses point estimates.
