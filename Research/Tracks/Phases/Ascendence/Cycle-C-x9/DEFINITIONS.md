# Ascendence — operationalizations and competing definitions

Status: Proposed working definitions for the GAIA Phase axis (Research/README.md §9.3, §12 Q1, Q4). No repository document defines "Ascendence" (Research/README.md only lists it as a phase; the spelling is the repository's), so each field's reading below is this track's choice, taken in the sense of ascent: movement upward toward a higher level, peak or optimum. Competing definitions are recorded, not resolved.

Owner context (recorded 2026-10-24; does not change methods): the owner maps phases to upgrade levels (Base = Divergence, Meta = Insurgence, Super = Allegiance, Hyper = Convergence, Ultra = Ascendence, Magic = Transcendence). "Ascendence corresponds to Ultra / Ultraphysical" is the owner's **Hypothetical** claim; it is not tested here and no claim depends on it.

## Cross-field observations (Baseline)
- **Ascent is local unless something moves it on**: adaptive walks stop at any peak with no fitter neighbour (P85, AS0-NAT-02), and in the ladder toy a lover stays on a rung unless an uncovered case meets the disposition to resolve it (PL-63, Hypothetical).
- **Hill climbing is discrete gradient ascent**: on a smooth landscape every walk reaches the single peak, as a contracting gradient flow reaches its optimum (PL-61, Established).
- **Headline numbers need checking**: P87's Table 5 reduction and P88's 'odds' wording do not hold as printed, while the underlying findings stand (PL-62, Plausible).

## Cross-field observations (x3)
- **Ascent has a safe speed**: gradient steps converge only below a step-size limit, and faster physical ascent raises AMS risk at the first stage, though P91 finds the order reversed at the second (PL-66, Hypothetical).
- **Stuck below the top**: walks stop at local peaks and conformist copying removes minority variants; reaching a higher or minority state needs more than local improvement or copying (PL-65, Hypothetical).
- **Structure is not enough**: with fixed 1901 inheritance, changing class margins barely raises total mobility; P87's rise also needs weaker inheritance (ASA-SOC-03).
- Source caution: P91's 4400 m percentages use 770 rather than 717 as denominator, and several percentages do not match counts (ASA-APP-05).

## Cross-field observations (x6)
- **Where an ascent ends depends on where it starts**: high peaks have large basins, and plain gradient steps can diverge from some starts even below the local step limit (PL-67, Hypothetical).
- **Upward change meets inherited structure**: uptalk's meaning depends on listener age, a toy minority variant rises only without majority amplification, and class or educational ascent grows as inheritance weakens (PL-68, Hypothetical).
- Source caution: P92 lists p = .0503 as significant; P93's '26 per cent' is 26.7%; P91's within-cohort P values ignore pairing (ASB-HUM-04, ASB-SOC-06, ASB-APP-07).

## Cross-field observations (x9)
- **Correlation makes ascent reliable**: uphill paths to the global peak are almost always available on correlated landscapes but usually not on uncorrelated ones (P94), as natural-gradient steps converge from every start while plain steps fail from some (PL-69, Plausible).
- **Rate and structure both bound an ascent**: rapid physical ascent raises AMS risk on top of altitude (P95), and structural change bounds class ascent on top of inheritance (PL-70, Hypothetical).
- Source caution: P95's stated range and Table 2 participant counts do not match its Table 1; P92's residual df implies unreported model terms (ASC-APP-09, ASC-HUM-06).
