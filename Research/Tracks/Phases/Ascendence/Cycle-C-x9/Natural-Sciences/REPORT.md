## Claims (ledger: `claims/claims.csv`)
- **ASC-NAT-01** [P] P85 estimates the probability that adaptive walks (each step to a random fitter neighbour, ending at a peak) reach the highest 1, 5, 14 or 25% of peaks: it varies widely among six empirical landscapes, is generally comparable to or smaller than in Rough Mount Fuji landscapes of similar ruggedness, and falls with ruggedness (RMF P14% from 96.3% at sigma 0.5 to 26.4% at sigma 10; NK from 100% at K 0 to 24.8% at K 8; House of Cards about 25%); peak height and basin size correlate positively in every landscape (R_ph-bs 0.69-0.97) but this does not guarantee a large probability of reaching a high peak. *Operationalization:* NAT Ascendence = adaptive walks climbing a fitness landscape. *Sources:* P85. *Limits:* Empirical landscapes are a few protein/RNA systems; P85 notes P14% cannot be fairly compared with very few peaks (NK K 0-1). P85 also reports that a 1/28 chance calculation in the study it re-examines (Papkou et al.) was incorrect.
- **ASC-NAT-02** [P] On 6-site four-state landscapes (one walk from every genotype, 10 landscapes per type) the chance of reaching the top 14% of peaks fell with RMF ruggedness (0.84 at sigma 0.5, 0.32 at sigma 2, 0.21 at sigma 10), was 0.21 for House of Cards (P85: about 25%), exceeded 0.14 for every landscape type, and peak height correlated positively with basin size in every landscape (minimum r 0.43; mean 0.93 at sigma 0.5 falling to 0.49-0.50 for sigma 10 and HoC). NK K 4 gave 0.243 vs 0.252 at K 1, so the pre-registered 'lower at K 4' held only narrowly; in the seed-99 smoke test it went the other way (0.240 vs 0.202), and with about 10 peaks at K 1 'top 14%' is the global peak alone, so this comparison is not robust. *Operationalization:* NAT Ascendence = adaptive walks to high peaks. *Sources:* SIM-AS-NAT-0/iter-1#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-0/iter-1#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-0/iter-1#H2;SIM-AS-NAT-0/iter-1#H3;SIM-AS-NAT-0/iter-1#H4;SIM-AS-NAT-0/iter-1#H5. *Limits:* Much smaller landscapes than P85's 9 sites; model landscapes only; the NK K comparison is fragile and seed-dependent (disclosed).
- **ASC-NAT-03** [P] At P85's own size (9 four-state sites, 262,144 genotypes) the agent's walks reproduce P85's RMF values: P14% 0.963 at sigma 0.5 (P85: 96.3%) and 0.221 at sigma 10 (P85: 26.4%, within the pre-registered 0.10); House of Cards gave 0.216, inside the 0.15-0.35 band but below P85's 'about 25%'. *Operationalization:* NAT Ascendence = adaptive walks to high peaks. *Sources:* SIM-AS-NAT-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-A/iter-1#H2;SIM-AS-NAT-A/iter-1#H3. *Limits:* Two landscapes per type; model landscapes only; P85's supplementary figures not read.
- **ASC-NAT-04** [P] With 30 NK landscapes per K (6 four-state sites), P14% was 1 at K 0 (one peak), then 0.249, 0.269, 0.277, 0.253 and 0.203 for K 1-5: lower at K 5 than K 2 as pre-registered, but the fall over K 2-5 was not supported as monotone (K 3 above K 2). The AS0 'lower at K 4 than K 1' result (AS0-NAT-02) is thus not a stable trend at this size: P14% peaks at intermediate K before falling, and the global-peak probability falls steadily (0.24 to 0.008). *Operationalization:* NAT Ascendence = adaptive walks on NK landscapes. *Sources:* SIM-AS-NAT-A/iter-2#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-A/iter-2#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-A/iter-2#H2;SIM-AS-NAT-A/iter-2#H3. *Limits:* 6 sites, not P85's 9; P85 warns that K 0-1 are not fairly comparable.
- **ASC-NAT-05** [P] On House of Cards landscapes of 5, 6, 7 and 8 four-state sites, P14% was 0.195, 0.198, 0.218 and 0.214: within 0.15-0.35 at every size and varying by only 0.023, consistent with P85's statement that the number of sites has minimal impact, though about 0.2 rather than P85's 'about 25%'. *Operationalization:* NAT Ascendence = adaptive walks on maximally rugged landscapes. *Sources:* SIM-AS-NAT-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-A/iter-3#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-A/iter-3#H2. *Limits:* Sizes 5-8 only (P85: 5-10); five landscapes per size.
- **ASC-NAT-06** [P] At P85's size (9 four-state sites) the probability that a walk ends on a top-14% peak falls with ruggedness: 0.671 at RMF sigma 1, 0.365 at sigma 2 and 0.243 at sigma 5, close to House of Cards (0.216 in ASA) and to NK K 8 (0.218; P85: 24.8%, within the pre-registered 0.10). The global peak is reached in 6.5% of walks at sigma 1 and in under 0.05% at sigma 2 or more. *Operationalization:* NAT Ascendence = adaptive walks to high peaks. *Sources:* SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-2#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-2#H2;SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-5#H1. *Limits:* Two landscapes per setting; model landscapes only.
- **ASC-NAT-07** [P] Walks favour high peaks because high peaks have large basins. On House of Cards (8 sites) the share of walks ending on the top 1%, 5%, 14% and 25% of peaks was 0.015, 0.080, 0.215 and 0.368, each above its uniform share. Peak height and basin size were positively correlated in every landscape: mean r 0.93 (RMF sigma 1) vs 0.50 (HoC) at 7 sites, and 0.98, 0.85 and 0.47 for RMF sigma 0.5, sigma 2 and HoC with 12 two-state sites, where P14% fell from 0.87 to 0.33 to 0.20. *Operationalization:* NAT Ascendence = peak height and basin size. *Sources:* SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-3#H2;SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-4#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-4#H2;SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-6#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-B/iter-6#H2. *Limits:* Two-state sites are the agent's extension (P85 uses four-state sites); basins computed for up to 1000 peaks.
- **ASC-NAT-08** [P] Reaching the top by uphill steps is easier than local peaks suggest: P94 finds that for all but uncorrelated (HoC) landscapes the probability of at least one accessible (fitness-increasing shortest) path to the global maximum rises with the number of loci, and the number of alternative accessible pathways grows without bounds, so repeatability is limited; in an 8-locus Aspergillus niger landscape, models with intermediate ruggedness describe the A. niger data best and about half of the 4-locus subgraphs have no accessible path. *Operationalization:* NAT Ascendence = accessible uphill paths to the global peak. *Sources:* P94. *Limits:* P94's notion of accessibility (shortest paths, strong selection / weak mutation) is restrictive; one organism; several formulas are lost in the PMC text.
- **ASC-NAT-09** [P] The agent's path counts reproduce P94's exact and qualitative results at small L: HoC has on average 1 accessible path at every L (0.94-1.15, within 3 SE), as does binary NK with K = L - 1 (0.95); the chance of no path rises with L for HoC (0.63, 0.81, 0.83, 0.88 at L 4-10); RMF theta 1 has 5.6, 59, 1341 and 50,346 paths on average at L 4-10, with no-path probability 0.022 at L 10 (HoC 0.883), falling with correlation (0.69, 0.05, 0 for theta 0.25, 1, 4 at L 8); holey landscapes match L! p^(L-1) (23.1 vs 22.5; 318 vs 315). The pre-registered check that, with the antipode as global minimum, the no-path probability would decline by L 10 was not supported (0.23 at L 6, 0.33 at L 10); P94 reports the decline at larger L. P94 Table 1's subgraph counts (C(8, n)) and '70 missing' (256 - 186) check out. *Operationalization:* NAT Ascendence = accessible uphill paths. *Sources:* SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-1#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-1#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-2#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-2#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-3#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-3#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-4#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-4#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-5#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-5#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-5#H2;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-6#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-6#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-7#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-7#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-7#H2;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-8#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-8#H1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-9#V1;SIM-AS-NAT-C/iter-9#H1. *Limits:* L up to 10 only; RMF form and NK partners are the agent's choices.
