- **ASC-APP-01** [P] Among 105 travellers treated for high-altitude illness at Mustang district hospital (Nepal), 74% had acute mountain sickness (61% mild, 36% moderate, 3% severe); in a Bayesian ordered logistic model of severity (None, Mild, Moderate or Severe), faster ascent rate (km/day) was associated with greater severity (coefficient 1.14, odds ratio 3.13) and smoking with less (odds ratio 0.16). P88's counterfactual says a 1-day Pokhara-Muktinath trip (2978 m gain) doubles the risk of moderate or severe AMS compared with 3 days. *Operationalization:* APP Ascendence = ascent to altitude and ascent rate. *Sources:* P88. *Limits:* Hospital register sample (P88 notes selection by Thorong La and airlift of severe cases); one site; credible intervals reported as 99% for headline effects. P88 calls the 2.069 counterfactual 'odds' in Fig. 2 and 'risk' in the abstract; see AS0-APP-02.
- **ASC-APP-03** [P] In 770 young lowland men ascending to 3650 m by air (fast, n 424) or by bus over two days (slow, n 346), and then by bus to 4400 m, fast ascent was a risk factor for AMS at 3650 m (adjusted OR 1.52 old Lake Louise score, 1.47 new) but protective at 4400 m (OR 0.67, 0.63); in 272 propensity-matched pairs AMS went from 37.9% to 35.7% (fast) and from 26.5% to 44.5% (slow). *Operationalization:* APP Ascendence = ascent protocol and AMS. *Sources:* P91. *Limits:* Men aged 18-45 only; self-reported symptoms; ascent mode not randomised, and the fast cohort had longer at altitude before the second ascent (P91's own limitation). Slips: ethics approval numbers PLA967-GC2023-021 vs -068; '34,400 m'; Table 4 'Under high school 1.88 (0.56, 1.36)'; percentages audited in ASA-APP-05.
