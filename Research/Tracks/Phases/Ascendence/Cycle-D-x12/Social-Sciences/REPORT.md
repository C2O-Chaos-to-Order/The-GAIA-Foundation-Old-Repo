- **ASD-SOC-01** [P] P87 finds that male social mobility in Scotland was already extensive in 1901 (52% mobile, 30% upwardly) and grew over the twentieth century (64% and 42% in 1974; 67% and 45% in 2001; downward mobility 22% throughout), about 1.5 percentage points per decade, driven largely by structural change (the professional and managerial class rising to 46.9% of destinations in 2001); relative mobility also increased (unidiff parameters 0 in 1901, -0.24 in 1974, -0.48 in 2001). In 1901, 57% of the salariat came from manual origins. *Operationalization:* SOC Ascendence = upward intergenerational class mobility. *Sources:* P87. *Limits:* Men only; 1901 data are weighted linked records (11% of cases unclassifiable); comparisons over time use a five-class scheme. Arithmetic issues in AS0-SOC-02.
- **ASD-SOC-03** [P] Holding P87's 1901 class-inheritance parameters fixed and fitting each year's Table 2 margins, total mobility moves only from 57.5% (1901) to 62.0% (1974) and 59.4% (2001), so structural change alone does not reproduce Table 1's rise from 52% to 67%; all three pre-registered checks were not supported (rise of at least 5 points; 1901 within 5 points of 52%; half of the rise). On 2001 margins, total mobility falls from 76.4% (no inheritance) through 68.1, 59.4, 51.3 to 44.4% as inheritance is scaled 0.5-2, always above the 23.2% structural minimum. In this model, the rise in total mobility needs weaker inheritance as well, consistent with P87's unidiff estimates (-0.24, -0.48). *Operationalization:* SOC Ascendence = structural vs relative mobility. *Sources:* SIM-AS-SOC-A/iter-1#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-A/iter-1#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-A/iter-1#H2;SIM-AS-SOC-A/iter-1#H3;SIM-AS-SOC-A/iter-3#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-A/iter-3#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-A/iter-3#H2. *Limits:* P87's statement concerns upward mobility, which needs a class ranking and was not modelled; Table 1 may use a different class scheme; Table 6 is fitted to 1901 only.
- **ASD-SOC-05** [P] Weaker inheritance closes most of the gap that structural change leaves. Scaling the 1901 inheritance parameters by P87's unidiff factors raises total mobility from 62.0% to 65.6% on 1974 margins (exp(-0.24)) and from 59.4% to 66.0% on 2001 margins (exp(-0.48)), close to Table 1's 67%. On 1901 margins an inheritance scale of 1.2 gives 53.2% (Table 1: 52%), and on 1974 margins mobility falls monotonically with scale (76.7, 70.0, 62.0, 53.4% at 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5). *Operationalization:* SOC Ascendence = structural vs relative mobility. *Sources:* SIM-AS-SOC-B/iter-1#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-B/iter-1#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-B/iter-2#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-B/iter-2#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-B/iter-2#H2;SIM-AS-SOC-B/iter-4#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-B/iter-4#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-B/iter-6#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-B/iter-6#H1. *Limits:* Unidiff applied to QPM diagonal parameters is the agent's model, not P87's five-class layer model; total, not upward, mobility.
- **ASD-SOC-06** [P] For women's education, absolute expansion did not mean much relative ascent: P93 finds considerable intergenerational stability in education levels relative to each cohort (ECC) across three generations of 90 Oxford Biobank women, of whom 50 held degrees but only 24 had a higher ECC than their mothers; 47 (52%) obtained their highest qualification as mature students. Audit: P93's percentages follow from its counts except '26 per cent of all subjects' (24/90 = 26.7%, truncated rather than rounded; pre-registered check not supported). P87 Tables 3-4 rows and columns sum to 100 within rounding (99-101). *Operationalization:* SOC Ascendence = women's educational mobility. *Sources:* SIM-AS-SOC-B/iter-3#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-B/iter-3#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-B/iter-3#H2;SIM-AS-SOC-B/iter-5#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-B/iter-5#H1;P93. *Limits:* P93's own caveats: one regional cohort; ECC compares relative scarcity, not attainment; footnote 29 describes the 'midpoint' as max minus min (method wording unclear).

- **ASD-SOC-08** [P] Structure sets bounds that inheritance moves within: the structural minimum rises from 11.7% (1901 margins) to 23.2% (2001), and % mobile stays between it and independence (72.4, 59.4, 35.0% at scales 0.25, 1, 3 on 2001 margins; 21.5% at scale 4 on 1901 margins); on 1974 margins independence is 14.7 points above fixed 1901 inheritance. - **ASD-SOC-09** [P] A competing pattern to P87's British rise: in the US, intergenerational mobility declined substantially over the past 150 y, but more slowly than previously thought, with father-son occupational rank-rank correlations rising from about 0.17 (1830 cohort) to 0.27 (1900) and 0.32 (1980), mostly before 1900 and largely through the farm-to-factory shift; net of farm origins relative mobility was largely stable, while absolute upward mobility rose for cohorts born before 1900 and fell after 1940. P96: absolute mobility changed more over time than relative mobility. Audit (SOC iteration 8): 0.01 per decade, 165 years and 16 cohorts follow from its endpoints; two-thirds of the correlation rise came by 1900. *Operationalization:* SOC Ascendence = upward mobility (US, long run). *Sources:* P96. *Limits:* White native-born men; linked samples differ in method (P96's caution); 'marginally significant' is used for P = 0.032; SI not read.
- **ASD-SOC-10** [P] In the agent's rank toy (70 equal occupations, P96's 7.5-point threshold), raising the father-son correlation from 0.17 to 0.32 cuts the upward share only from 41.3% to 39.8%, whereas shifting sons up by 0.3 SD (structural upgrading) raises it to 49.9% (down 30.2%) with the rank correlation unchanged (0.309 vs 0.308) and downgrading by 0.2 SD reverses it (up 33.4%, down 46.4%): absolute mobility tracks structure, relative mobility tracks inheritance, as P96 and P87 both argue. Rank correlations match (6/pi) asin(rho/2); with a 15-point threshold only 35.7% stay (pre-registered 'more than half stay' not supported). QPM calibration: 1901 52% between scales 1.25 and 1.3 (51.2%); 2001 67% at 0.55 (67.2%); 1974 64% near 0.9 (63.7%). *Operationalization:* SOC Ascendence = absolute vs relative mobility. *Sources:* SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-1#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-1#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-2#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-2#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-3#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-3#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-4#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-4#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-5#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-5#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-6#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-6#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-7#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-7#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-8#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-8#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-8#H2;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-9#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-9#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-10#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-10#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-11#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-11#H1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-12#V1;SIM-AS-SOC-D/iter-12#H1. *Limits:* Toy statuses are bivariate normal with equal-sized occupations; QPM scaling is the agent's model.
