## Evidence and finding

Established textual description [TOG1-C0-07-T]: “Access to personal, sensitive, confidential, community-governed, or third-party material requires appropriate authority, consent where required, and protection. Public availability alone does not confer unlimited reuse rights.” [S01 §4.7]

Plausible interpretation [TOG1-C0-07-A]: UNESCO calls for a legitimate aim and valid legal basis, including informed consent, rather than saying consent is the sole possible legal basis. The charter’s phrase “where required” is material. Public access and permission to reproduce or repurpose are different questions. [S02;S07;S08; Universal Regulation ETH-2–3; UNESCO paras32–34; UDHR arts12,27]

## Competing considerations and counterevidence

This is not jurisdiction-specific legal advice. Individual consent may not resolve collective cultural authority. A withdrawn consent requires review of future processing and obligations, not an unsupported promise that all lawful records must always be deleted.
