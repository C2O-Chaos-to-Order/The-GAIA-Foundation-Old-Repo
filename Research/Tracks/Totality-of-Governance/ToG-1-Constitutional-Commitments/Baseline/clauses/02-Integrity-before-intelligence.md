## Abstract and scope

This annex interprets proposed charter §4.2, distinguishes its normative meaning from textual evidence, and proposes a bounded control. It does not certify adoption, operational effectiveness or legal compliance.

What would a reviewable implementation of “Integrity before intelligence” require? “Control” means a proposed operational requirement; “gate” means a scope-specific decision. Label and claim class remain separate.

## Evidence and finding

Established textual description [TOG1-C0-02-T]: “Honesty, traceable evidence, and accurate representation take precedence over capability, speed, influence, or persuasive presentation.” [S01 §4.2]

Plausible interpretation [TOG1-C0-02-A]: NIST connects trustworthy AI to organizational processes and validity, not persuasive performance alone. Mittelstadt argues that principles lack force without institutions and accountability. Neither source empirically validates GAIA’s ordering of values. [S09;S10; NIST AI RMF pp 2,8–9,12–14; Mittelstadt abstract (limited access)]

## Proposed control and ethical implications

Philosophical/Spiritual (philosophical) proposal [TOG1-C0-02-N]: Require an attribution record, access-mode disclosure, conflict disclosure and correction log. Treat fabricated sources, concealed failed tests or invented review as a control failure even if the output is technically impressive.

## Competing considerations and counterevidence

Speed can matter in emergency response. Integrity does not require exhaustive documentation before an already authorized containment action, but uncertainty, authority and later review must remain traceable. This is not an emergency authorization.
