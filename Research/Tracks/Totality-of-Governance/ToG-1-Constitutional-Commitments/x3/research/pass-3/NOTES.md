# Research pass 3 — Continuity, revocation, incidents and minimized records

Date2026-10-25. Single author Perplexity; one bounded analytical pass, not independent review.

## Question
Can previously valid approval remain safe to reuse after time,scope,basis or risk changes?

## Source portions actually examined
E03 GOVERN1.5,1.7;MANAGE2.1,2.3–2.4,4.1–4.3;E02 paras32–38;E04 pp5–8,15,18–19;R01 §§7,12–13

## Added/deepened
Ongoing monitoring, documented deactivation responsibilities and post-deployment feedback appear in the newly read NIST Core. The OHCHR guide distinguishes actual impact requiring remediation from potential impacts needing prevention/mitigation. The guide’s risk concepts emphasize risks to people, not solely business consequences.

## Challenge and alternatives
Revocation of the sole synthetic permission basis is not a universal law that all records must be erased or every other lawful processing basis disappears. Immutable history must be minimized and protected; hashes alone do not anonymize or establish lawful retention.

## Output
Lifecycle state model, reauthorization template, retention/correction register and horizon-four trace stress test; appendix preserves all prior uncertainty rather than claiming closure by repetition.

## Limits
No live system, recovery drill, retention legal determination or consent study. Four-event horizon is not arbitrary-time verification.
