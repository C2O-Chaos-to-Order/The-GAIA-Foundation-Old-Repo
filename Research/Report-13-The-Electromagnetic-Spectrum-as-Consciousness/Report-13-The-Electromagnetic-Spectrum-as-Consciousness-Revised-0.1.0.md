## 1. Definition and central distinction

**Consciousness**, as used in this report, means subjective experience: there being something it is like to see, feel, think, or undergo a state. It is not equivalent by definition to information processing, measurable neural activity, or the presence of an electromagnetic field. The **hard problem** asks why and how physical processes are accompanied by experience at all. Chalmers’ formulation is a standard philosophical statement of this problem [S01].

The **electromagnetic spectrum** is the range of electromagnetic radiation described by frequency or wavelength. It is a physical classification of radiation, not an experiencing subject. The spectrum does not, by itself, have an interior point of view, bind information, or establish consciousness. Assigning the spectrum itself to the Consciousness slot is therefore either a declared metaphor or a category error.

The narrower scientific question is whether the **endogenous electromagnetic field generated by neural activity** contributes causally to the integration or binding of conscious contents, and if so, whether that contribution is enabling, constitutive, or identical with experience. The field is a physical phenomenon associated with the living brain; its relation to phenomenal experience remains contested.

**Central claim:** the cosmic electromagnetic spectrum is not consciousness. Endogenous brain electromagnetic fields are candidate physical contributors to neural integration, but evidence that they are identical with subjective experience is not established. The Earth-system therefore receives **no consciousness analogue** in this report. The spectrum is a medium of physical interaction, not a subject of experience.

## 2. Theories and evidence

### 2.1 Conscious Electromagnetic Information (CEMI) theory

Johnjoe McFadden’s CEMI theory proposes that the brain’s endogenous electromagnetic field plays a constitutive role in consciousness. In the theory, the field integrates information and can influence neuronal activity, including motor-neuron firing; the proposal is stronger than the claim that the field is merely a passive correlate [S03; S04].

CEMI is a theoretical account, not an established consensus. Its claims should be separated into independently assessable questions:

1. Does neural activity generate measurable electromagnetic fields? **Yes**, as a matter of established neurophysics.
2. Can endogenous fields influence neural activity under relevant conditions? **There is evidence for ephaptic and field-mediated effects in some contexts**, with magnitude and generality depending on the conditions.
3. Do those fields perform integration not adequately explained by synaptic, cellular, and network mechanisms? **Open and contested.**
4. Is the field itself subjective experience? **Not established by demonstrating field generation or causal influence.**
5. Does CEMI solve the hard problem? **No consensus solution is established.**

The distinction between a physical mechanism and a theory of phenomenal experience must remain explicit.

### 2.2 The binding problem

The binding problem concerns how distributed neural processes contribute to unified perceptual, cognitive, or experiential contents. Electromagnetic-field theories propose that fields may provide a physically integrated medium for interactions across neural populations. This is a candidate explanation, not proof that an electromagnetic field is the unique or sufficient solution.

Evidence of synchronization, field effects, or spatially distributed neural activity does not alone show that the field constitutes experience. A theory must specify its mechanisms, make discriminating predictions against synaptic/network alternatives, and identify observations that would count against it. [S03; S05; S07]

### 2.3 External electromagnetic carrier versus endogenous neural field

Claims sometimes grouped under “electromagnetic consciousness” are not equivalent:

- **External-carrier claim:** consciousness requires an external electromagnetic signal or carrier, such as light or radio waves.
- **Endogenous-field claim:** fields generated within the brain contribute to neural processing or integration.
- **Photonic-communication claim:** ultraweak photon emissions or optical processes carry functionally significant information within living tissue.
- **Electromagnetic-environment claim:** external fields alter neural or physiological conditions without themselves being consciousness.

The first claim is not supported as a general requirement for ordinary awareness: people can experience visual imagery, dreams, and other conscious states without current visual input. This observation does not refute every possible electromagnetic-field theory, because an endogenous brain field is distinct from an external carrier. Shielding and altered environmental fields must be interpreted according to what is actually shielded, at what frequencies and strengths, and which physiological process is measured. They cannot be treated as a universal test of all field theories.

The supplied source labelled “Functional Consciousness: Pillar 27: The Electromagnetic Question of Consciousness” is a 2026 Zenodo item according to the provided description. Its exact record, author, methods, and wording require primary-source verification before quotations or strong conclusions are attributed to it [S05].

### 2.4 Zero-point-field (ZPF) proposal

Keppler’s framework proposes that conscious states arise through coupling between the brain and selected modes of the quantum-electrodynamic zero-point field, with the brain modeled as a resonant system [S06]. This is a speculative theoretical proposal. It does not establish that the electromagnetic spectrum is conscious, nor that the brain’s coupling to the ZPF has been empirically demonstrated to generate phenomenal experience.

A credible evaluation would need to specify quantitative predictions, plausible coupling strengths, noise and decoherence constraints, measurement procedures, and outcomes that distinguish the proposal from conventional neural models. Formal development alone is not empirical confirmation.

### 2.5 Biophotons and coherence

Ultraweak photon emission from biological tissue has been reported and studied in relation to biochemical processes, including oxidative metabolism. The existence of ultraweak photon emission does not establish that the brain uses a structured photonic code for cognition or that such emission constitutes consciousness.

The supplied “Unified Coherence Field Theory” is described as integrating Orch-OR, biophotons, and gamma activity. Until its primary record, methods, and evidential status are checked, it must be treated as a hypothesis rather than an established finding [S08; S09]. Claims of coherence must define the physical quantity, spatial and temporal scales, measurement method, and functional prediction.

### 2.6 The hard problem remains open

Even if a field were shown to integrate neural information or causally influence neuronal firing, a further question would remain: why should that process be accompanied by subjective experience? A theory may address neural correlates, access, integration, or report without settling the metaphysical or explanatory problem of phenomenal consciousness.

This report therefore separates three levels:

- **Correlation:** field properties covary with neural or behavioral states.
- **Causal contribution:** interventions on the field reliably change a specified neural or behavioral outcome.
- **Constitution or identity:** the field is claimed to constitute or be subjective experience.

Evidence for one level does not automatically establish the next.

## 3. Philosophical perspectives

### Physicalism

Consciousness depends on or is identical with physical processes. An endogenous electromagnetic field could be one candidate physical process. Physicalism alone does not identify which process explains experience or resolve the hard problem.

### Functionalism

Consciousness is associated with functional roles such as access, integration, discrimination, and report, depending on the theory. An electromagnetic field might implement some roles, but the existence of a field does not establish that it realizes all relevant functions or that it is the only possible substrate.

### Panpsychism

Panpsychist views treat experience or proto-experience as fundamental or widespread. Assigning experience to electromagnetic fields could be developed as a metaphysical position, but it is not a finding established by the existence of the spectrum. This report does not adopt panpsychism as a scientific conclusion.

### Critical distinction

“The electromagnetic spectrum is consciousness” conflates a physical domain with subjective experience. “The brain’s endogenous electromagnetic field may contribute to neural integration” is a narrower, testable physical hypothesis. “That field is identical with experience” is a stronger theoretical claim requiring additional argument and evidence.

## 4. Relationships within the GAIA architecture

- **Body:** neural electromagnetic fields are part of the brain’s physiology and arise through physical processes in neural tissue.
- **Mind:** cognition, memory, and emotion are studied through multiple explanatory levels. An electromagnetic field may contribute to their neural implementation; it is not automatically equivalent to the mind.
- **Soul:** in the current Earth-system metaphor, the geomagnetic field occupies the structural Soul slot, denoting persistence and boundary. This is a declared analogy and does not establish a literal planetary soul. In the person-framework, Soul remains a philosophical and cross-tradition construct distinct from consciousness.
- **Spirit:** the electromagnetic spectrum previously served as an Earth-system Spirit analogue because it represents physical exchange and connection. This report does not transfer the spectrum to the Consciousness slot. If the architecture later revises the Spirit mapping, that change should be separately proposed and versioned rather than forced by this report.
- **Subconscious:** Report 12 uses geomagnetic variability as a structural analogue for hidden, multiscale dynamics. That analogy is not evidence that Earth has a literal subconscious.
- **Earth:** the Earth-system has measurable electromagnetic phenomena, geomagnetic fields, and interacting subsystems. Those facts do not establish Earth has subjective experience.
- **Chaos and Order:** neural criticality and edge-of-chaos hypotheses are research topics, not settled requirements for consciousness. A field’s presence or coherence does not prove that a neural system occupies a critical regime.
- **Meta-wisdom:** the thirteenth element requires the architecture to distinguish analogy, mechanism, correlation, causation, constitution, and identity, and to revise its claims when evidence demands it.

## 5. Architectural decision

**Decision for this revision: Consciousness remains unassigned in the Earth-system.**

The electromagnetic spectrum is a physical medium and not a subject of experience. An endogenous neural field may be investigated as a candidate contributor to the binding of conscious contents, but it must not be promoted to confirmed identity with consciousness. If a future version assigns an endogenous field as a structural analogue, it must specify the organism, field variables, target construct, proposed mechanism, discriminating predictions, and failure conditions.

The architecture retains these distinctions:

### Sources checked against the claims

1. **CEMI primary theory article:** McFadden, [“Integrating information in the brain’s EM field: the cemi field theory of consciousness,” *Neuroscience of Consciousness* (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niaa016), available via [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7507405/). It is evidence of the proposal and its rationale, not independent proof that the field is identical to subjective experience. Its own framing must be distinguished from independent validation.
2. **Direct theory comparison:** the COGITATE Consortium, [“Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness,” *Nature* 642 (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1), reports tests of specified predictions from IIT and GNWT. It does not directly test or settle CEMI, zero-point-field accounts, or the metaphysical identity of consciousness with the cosmic electromagnetic spectrum. It is useful here as a model of adversarial, prediction-level testing rather than as a universal verdict on consciousness.
3. **Contemporary theory overview:** the 2026 review [“Consciousness in the Brain: An Integrative Review of Contemporary Theories”](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci16070745) surveys multiple theories and states that a comprehensive account of conscious experience has not emerged. As a review, it is secondary literature; individual empirical claims still require their underlying primary sources.
4. **Physics boundary:** NASA’s [Earth’s Radiation Budget](https://science.nasa.gov/ems/13_radiationbudget/) describes electromagnetic radiation as energy entering, being reflected/absorbed by, and leaving the Earth system. This grounds the distinction between the physical spectrum and a conscious subject; it is not evidence about brain consciousness and is not cited for that purpose.

### Claim-level disposition

- **Established physical claim:** neural activity generates measurable electromagnetic fields; field generation alone does not establish phenomenal experience.
- **Plausible but scope-limited empirical claim:** endogenous fields can influence neuronal activity under some conditions. The magnitude, generality, and explanatory importance of such effects require mechanism-specific evidence and comparison with synaptic, cellular, and network explanations.
- **Theory, not settled finding:** CEMI proposes that the brain’s electromagnetic field is causally active and constitutive of consciousness. The 2020 article is the theory’s own exposition, not an independent adjudication.
- **Unestablished stronger claim:** that the field is identical with subjective experience, that the cosmic electromagnetic spectrum itself is conscious, or that Earth has consciousness because it has electromagnetic phenomena.
- **Unverified/unfinished source items:** the report’s supplied ZPF, “Functional Consciousness,” “Unified Coherence Field Theory,” and biophoton-cognition references still require exact record, author/version, methods, and claim-level checks. The present source pass does not validate those sources or their stronger conclusions.

### Minimum discriminating test for a field-constitutive account

A defensible study must preregister a prediction that differs from a specified conventional neural-network account; quantify the endogenous field and the intervention; control for direct cellular, synaptic, thermal, sensory, and stimulation artifacts; use sham and dose-response conditions where applicable; define a conscious-content measure independently of the field measure; and report null results and uncertainty. Evidence that a field modulates neural firing would support causal influence under the tested conditions, but would not alone establish identity with experience or solve the hard problem.
