This directory is the research home of **The GAIA Foundation**. It exists to develop, test, challenge, document, and connect the foundations that inform GAIA. The purpose is not to make the strongest claim possible; it is to establish what the evidence warrants, identify what remains unknown, and make every important conclusion traceable to its sources and checks.

Repository overview: [The GAIA Foundation README](../README.md).

## 1. Purpose and scope

Research in this corpus may address biological, cognitive, psychological, social, planetary, technological, ethical, philosophical, historical, spiritual, or theological questions. Cross-disciplinary work is welcome, but disciplinary boundaries must remain visible.

Every report must distinguish among:

- **Empirical claims** — observations or findings supported by identifiable data and methods.
- **Mathematical or computational models** — formal representations whose assumptions, parameters, and limits are stated.
- **Historical claims** — claims supported by primary or reliable secondary sources.
- **Interpretive or philosophical claims** — reasoned analysis that does not pretend to be an experimental result.
- **Spiritual or theological claims** — clearly identified within their relevant traditions, premises, or interpretive frame.
- **Hypotheses and speculation** — ideas that remain to be tested, falsified, or bounded.

A claim may move between categories only when new evidence and reasoning justify the change. Metaphor, analogy, belief, model output, and established mechanism are not interchangeable.

## 2. The responsibility ladder

The motto is a governance rule, not decoration. The greater the capability, reach, autonomy, or potential impact of a proposal, the stronger its required safeguards, evidence, accountability, and review.

| Power / capability level | Minimum responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Power | Explain purpose, foreseeable effects, and accountability. |
| Meta Power | Assess effects on the systems that govern or amplify power. |
| Super Power | Evaluate cross-domain consequences, misuse, and affected parties. |
| Hyper Power | Test cascading, large-scale, and failure-propagation risks. |
| Ultra Power | Require independent scrutiny, containment, reversibility where possible, and explicit authorization. |
| Magic Power | Apply the same discipline to extraordinary, spiritual, speculative, or unfamiliar claims: label assumptions, protect people and ecosystems, and never use extraordinary language to bypass evidence or ethics. |

No capability level waives responsibility. The framework prioritizes life, human dignity, non-harm, truthfulness, consent, privacy, environmental stewardship, and accountable governance.

## 3. The integration mandate: “quantum entangle” the work

For this project, **“quantum entangle all of the work” is the integration mandate**: every research product should be connected to its relevant sources, claims, assumptions, related reports, dependencies, and validation records so that the corpus functions as a coherent, auditable system rather than a collection of isolated documents.

Implement this through **explicit traceability**, including:

1. **Stable identity:** Give each report, revision, claim, source, experiment or simulation, and review record a durable identifier.
2. **Bidirectional cross-links:** Link related reports and claims in both directions where useful; record why the relationship exists.
3. **Provenance:** Record source title, author or issuing body when known, date, stable URL or locator, access date when relevant, and a digest when files or datasets need integrity checks.
4. **Claim-to-evidence mapping:** Identify which source directly supports which claim. A source's general relevance is not proof of a particular statement.
5. **Dependency and contradiction tracking:** Record assumptions, upstream dependencies, disagreements, alternative explanations, and unresolved contradictions.
6. **Reproducibility:** For every claimed computation or simulation, preserve the method, version, inputs, parameters, environment, execution record, outputs, limitations, and instructions sufficient for independent rerun.
7. **Change control:** Preserve prior revisions, explain substantive changes, and record impact on downstream reports and conclusions.
8. **Review and closure gates:** Record the reviewers, the review scope, findings, remediation, and a clear Pass / Fail / Indeterminate decision. **Indeterminate is not Pass.**
9. **Integrity checks:** Where appropriate, use checksums, manifests, link checks, and automated validation to identify missing, mismatched, or changed artifacts.
10. **Privacy and safety:** Do not bring sensitive personal data, restricted material, or unlicensed content into a research artifact without the necessary authority and protections.

This operational use of “entanglement” is a **knowledge-graph and governance metaphor**. It does not assert that reports, minds, Earth systems, software, or people are literally quantum-entangled. A literal quantum-entanglement claim requires an appropriate physical system, an operational definition, valid measurements, and evidence specific to that claim.

## 4. Research lifecycle

Use the following lifecycle unless a report documents a justified alternative:

**Define → Research → Model → Simulate → Verify → Falsify → Stress Test → Peer Review → Risk Assess → Mitigate → Document → Repeat**

Apply the Research → Simulate → Verify cycle as applicable, with three distinct checks:

- **Internal consistency:** Are definitions, calculations, assumptions, and conclusions coherent?
- **Independent challenge:** Can a reviewer who did not produce the result identify weaknesses, competing explanations, or missing evidence?
- **Reproducibility and regression:** Can the stated process be rerun, and do previously validated behaviors remain intact?

Repeating a process does not automatically make it independent, valid, or true. Report the number and nature of actual executions; do not imply runs occurred when they were only proposed or designed.

## 5. Report requirements

Each substantive report should include, proportionate to its scope:

- Title, stable ID, owner or contributors where applicable, status, revision, and dates.
- Purpose, research questions, boundaries, definitions, and explicit exclusions.
- Executive summary and table of contents.
- Method, source-selection approach, and evidence register.
- Claims with source links, confidence or uncertainty, assumptions, and alternatives.
- Relevant cross-disciplinary links and a description of each relationship.
- Model or simulation design, if any, plus actual execution records and results.
- Limitations, falsification criteria, risks, and unresolved questions.
- Independent review findings and the status of each required gate.
- Glossary, references, change history, and next actions.

Use explicit status language: **proposed, drafted, researched, simulated, executed, reviewed, verified, rejected, or indeterminate**. These terms must describe actual work completed, not intentions.

## 6. Repository organization

Preserve existing numbered report directories and historical revisions. New structure should clarify provenance and not silently relocate, overwrite, or duplicate existing source materials.

Recommended organization as the corpus grows:

- **Research/README.md** — this canonical index and governance entry point.
- **Research/Report-<number>-<topic>/** — source report files and retained revisions.
- **Research/Validation/** — validation code, test fixtures, execution records, and limitations.
- **Research/Claims/** — claim registers and evidence-to-claim mappings, if maintained separately.
- **Research/Provenance/** — manifests, source inventories, digests, and import records, if maintained separately.
- **Research/Reviews/** — independent review and challenge records, if maintained separately.

These are organizational recommendations, not a claim that every listed folder already exists. Check the current tree before creating new locations. Keep private or restricted material in appropriately protected locations; do not assume that research belongs in a public release lane.

## 7. Evidence, simulations, and safety gates

- Cite sources close to the claims they support, and prefer primary sources when suitable.
- Record negative findings and failed tests as well as successful outcomes.
- Separate a test fixture or synthetic smoke test from empirical validation of a report's claims.
- Do not present a plan, design, dry run, or unexecuted script as an executed simulation.
- Do not treat correlation, conceptual similarity, or ordinary system coupling as proof of causation or quantum entanglement.
- Do not infer consciousness, intention, sentience, or subjective experience from fluent language or system behavior alone.
- Keep proposed, documented, implemented, tested, and independently verified work distinct.
- Do not authorize implementation, deployment, publication of sensitive information, or irreversible changes merely because research has advanced. Obtain the appropriate explicit approval and review.

A gate must identify its scope, criteria, evidence, decision, reviewer or decision authority, timestamp, and remaining blockers. When evidence is missing or inconsistent, record **Indeterminate** and retain the blocker.

## 8. Working principle

GAIA's intelligence is only as trustworthy as the integrity of its foundations. Every connection in this corpus must make the work more understandable, more accountable, easier to challenge, and safer to use.

**Connect the work. Preserve the evidence. Test the claims. State the uncertainty. Accept responsibility.**
