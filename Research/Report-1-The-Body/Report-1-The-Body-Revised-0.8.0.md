## 1. Definition and scope

The **body** is the organized physical embodiment of a living organism: cells, tissues, organs, and physiological systems that interact with one another and with their environment. In humans, the brain is part of the body rather than an external controller of a passive container. The present report concentrates on human anatomy and physiology; it does not assume that all forms of life possess human organs or nervous systems. [S01–S03; S27–S29]

The body–mind distinction can be useful for organizing questions—for example, distinguishing physiological mechanisms from lived experience—without assuming that body and mind are two independently existing substances. Whether a physical account exhausts the nature of persons or subjective experience remains a philosophical and scientific question; it is not settled by anatomical description alone. [S05; S09; S30]

**Scope boundary:** This is a foundational conceptual report, not a medical reference, diagnostic instrument, or exhaustive organ-system textbook. General physiological descriptions cannot establish the health status of any individual.

## 2. Evidence and explanatory model

### 2.1 Nervous system and distributed regulation

The human central nervous system comprises the brain and spinal cord. The peripheral nervous system connects the central nervous system with the rest of the body, carrying sensory information toward central structures and motor and autonomic signals to effectors. Peripheral nerves contribute to sensation, movement, and functions involving organs, blood vessels, digestion, and other body systems. These descriptions are consistent with National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke educational material. [S27; S28]

Physiological regulation is distributed across systems. The hypothalamus contributes to temperature, fluid, endocrine, and other regulatory processes; it does not independently control every aspect of homeostasis. Perception, memory, emotion, and action depend on interacting neural networks and bodily processes. Brain-region labels identify important contributions, not isolated, sufficient causes of complex functions. Claims that a single region contains a person's identity, morality, or soul go beyond what anatomical localization establishes. [S02; S27; S28]

### 2.2 Homeostasis, allostasis, and feedback

**Homeostasis** refers to regulated maintenance of internal variables within viable ranges, not a perfectly fixed or motionless state. Regulatory loops can include sensors, integration or control processes, and effectors. Negative feedback tends to counter a deviation; positive feedback reinforces a process until a terminating condition occurs. Childbirth is a common positive-feedback example. Real physiological control can also involve anticipatory, feedforward responses and multiple interacting loops. [S03; S29]

**Allostasis** is a related concept emphasizing stability through adaptive change. It is useful to distinguish it from the narrower textbook shorthand that homeostasis means simply returning one variable to a single set point. The concepts overlap in some literature and are not always used identically; the report should avoid presenting them as universally agreed, mutually exclusive categories. [S29]

### 2.3 Embodied, embedded, enactive, and extended cognition (4E)

The 4E family of approaches asks how cognition relates to bodily organization, environmental setting, action, and tools or other external supports:

- **Embodied:** bodily form, sensory capacities, movement, and physiological states can shape cognitive processes.
- **Embedded:** cognition takes place within physical, social, and cultural environments that constrain and support it.
- **Enactive:** cognition is approached through an organism's active engagement and sense-making in its environment.
- **Extended:** in some accounts, tools and external resources can become functionally integrated into cognitive activity, rather than merely influencing it from outside.

These are related but not identical theoretical claims. “Embodied” does not automatically prove that every cognitive process is constituted by the whole body; “extended” does not mean every tool is literally part of the mind. The degree to which body, environment, and external resources constitute cognition—rather than causally influence or support it—remains debated. [S30–S32]

**2026 update.** Alexopoulos's open-access 2026 commentary applies 4E cognition and critical phenomenology to aging, disability, environmental fit, and real-world cognitive support. It argues for considering brain–body–environment interactions and ecological assessment rather than interpreting all functional change as isolated brain decline. This is a conceptual/clinical commentary, not a new controlled experiment proving every 4E thesis. [S30]

A 2026 review of ambulatory virtual reality and ecological cognition describes methodological opportunities for studying perception and memory during movement and action while balancing experimental control with ecological validity. It supports the value of richer research designs, but it does not establish that all real-world cognition is best explained by one 4E theory. [S31]

A 2026 open-access philosophical analysis argues that 4E cognition is internally diverse and that some versions of enactivism make more radical claims than other embodied or extended approaches. This is a useful caution against treating “4E” as one settled theory. [S32]

## 3. Perspectives and interpretations

Anatomy describes structure; physiology investigates function and regulation; cognitive science studies processes such as perception, learning, memory, and action; phenomenology examines the structure of lived experience. Embodied approaches can connect these levels, but they should not collapse them into one type of explanation.

Spiritual traditions may understand bodily life as sacred, ensouled, or a context for transformation. Those interpretations are relevant to meaning, ethics, identity, and lived practice. They should be represented accurately as philosophical, religious, or experiential perspectives, not substituted for anatomical evidence. Aristotle's account of soul as the form or actuality of a living body differs substantially from a view of the soul as a separable occupant; neither position can be resolved by anatomy alone. [S05; S09]

## 4. Relationships to the wider framework

- **Body–mind:** Sensation, memory, emotion, learning, and action interact with bodily and neural processes. The body–mind distinction is analytically useful without itself deciding the metaphysics of mind.
- **Body–consciousness:** Neural, bodily, and environmental conditions are important to explanations of changing states of awareness. Their relevance does not, by itself, settle the philosophical problem of subjective experience.
- **Body–soul:** Religious and philosophical traditions differ on embodiment, personal identity, and death. Anatomical evidence does not select one metaphysical account.
- **Body–spirit:** Spiritual practices and meanings are lived through bodily action and social relationships. A spiritual interpretation does not imply a newly discovered anatomical structure.
- **Body–Earth:** Human organisms depend on air, water, food, microbial ecologies, climate, and built and natural environments. Humans are participants in ecological systems, not physiologically or ecologically self-sufficient.
- **Body–technology:** Devices, notes, calendars, mobility aids, and digital tools can support memory, perception, planning, or action. Whether a specific tool counts as part of an extended cognitive system depends on the theory and the functional relationship; usefulness alone does not settle that classification. [S30–S32]

## 5. Uncertainty and limitations

1. This report is a conceptual foundation, not medical advice or a complete anatomy and physiology reference.
2. Average descriptions do not determine an individual's physiology or clinical condition.
3. Functional brain regions and named systems are simplifications of interacting, context-sensitive processes.
4. The 4E label covers multiple approaches with different commitments. Evidence for bodily influence or environmental scaffolding is not automatically evidence for every strong constitutive claim.
5. The two 2026 sources supplied by the author have different evidentiary standing: S25 is a peer-reviewed commentary; S26 is an arXiv preprint proposing a model.
6. Neither bodily dependence nor the 4E literature, by itself, proves or disproves a detachable soul, a spiritual ontology, or a complete solution to consciousness.
7. The inherited source identifiers S01–S24 were present in the supplied text, but their full bibliography was not included. Their mappings must be reconciled against the original reference list before the report can be considered fully source-auditable.

## 7. Conclusions

The evidence supports an **embodied foundation**: human mental and experiential life depends on coordinated biological functioning and ongoing exchange with the environment. Contemporary 4E work gives a productive framework for investigating how bodily action, environmental setting, and external supports shape cognition. The framework is diverse, and the stronger claim that body or environment constitutes every cognitive process remains contested.

This report does not infer a metaphysical conclusion from physiological facts. It keeps biological findings, theoretical models, philosophical arguments, and spiritual interpretations in relationship while preserving their differences.

## Research Pass 1 — Empirical Update (2025–2026)

### 4E cognition: distinguish the claims

Mendes's 2026 analysis argues that only autopoietic/autonomist enactivism clearly rejects the full set of computationalist, representationalist, functionalist, internalist, and realist assumptions targeted in the article; other 4E positions can retain some of those commitments. This is one author's critical philosophical analysis, not a field-wide consensus that 4E cognition is “dominant” or that all other variants are merely cognitivism. The practical correction is to disaggregate embodiment claims: (a) bodily state correlates with cognition, (b) bodily state causally modulates cognition, (c) bodily processes are necessary for a specified capacity, and (d) bodily/environmental organization partly constitutes a cognitive process. These claims require different evidence. [S40]

### Deep embodiment and visceral pathways

Monti and Aglioti's 2026 review surveys evidence concerning cardiac-cycle phase and baroreceptor firing, respiration-locked neural dynamics, gastrointestinal rhythms, and vagal afferents in perception, attention, learning, affect, decision-making, and social cognition. Treat this as a review of converging, task-specific evidence—not proof that every cognitive function is uniformly determined by visceral input. Where a report names a pathway such as vagal afferents or nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS) to insula, link that anatomical pathway to a specific primary study or an explicitly scoped review claim. [S41]

**Updated status:** bodily regulation is established; specific visceral influences are empirically supported within tasks and methods; strong constitutive claims about cognition remain theory-dependent and need capacity-specific evidence.

## Version 0.5.0 — Empirical Update and Model-Specification Addendum
**Revision date:** 2026-10-23  
**Change type:** Research integration; embodied-learning model scope; simulation-specification corrections  
**Evidence status:** Research-updated draft; independent disciplinary review and numerical simulation remain pending.

### 1. New evidence: an integrative embodied-learning scaffold

Yu et al. (2026) review neural and computational mechanisms of embodied learning and propose the Integrative Embodied Learning Framework (IELF). It organizes the account across computational mechanisms, overlapping neural systems, and behavioral outcomes, including predictive coding/active inference, reinforcement learning, Hebbian plasticity, sensorimotor and parietal–premotor systems, cerebellum, basal ganglia, and hippocampal/interoceptive/affective networks. This is a useful **mechanistic scaffold**, not proof that one universal arbitration algorithm has been validated across development, tasks, and populations. See S54.

The September 2026 preprint *Toward a Science of Full-Body Intelligence* argues for a falsifiable organism-level account integrating interoception, predictive processing, neurovisceral regulation, sensorimotor grounding, and environment. It is a preprint proposing a research program, not an independently validated consensus model. See S55.

### 2. Model upgrade and constraints

A minimal IELF-class model may represent:
- exteroceptive observations (s_t);
- interoceptive observations (i_t);
- latent bodily/allostatic state (x_t);
- action (a_t), separating autonomic and skeletomotor actions where the model requires it;
- distinct predictive-error, reward-learning, and Hebbian-plasticity channels.

**Required correction:** a softmax/logistic arbitration function must output a normalized vector of channel weights if it is described as a weight vector; a scalar sigmoid alone is insufficient. Prediction errors also require explicit definitions, units, precision estimates, and an update rule for each channel. A generative factorization is not executable until transition and observation likelihoods, initial conditions, policy/action selection, learning rules, noise, and measurement mappings are specified.

The proposed parameter ranges in the supplied research pass are **exploratory design choices**, not established physiological ranges unless supported by an empirical source or dataset. In particular, an interoceptive-precision sweep of 0.1–10 is a dimensionless modeling range only if precision is normalized and defined that way. It must not be called physiologically calibrated by default.

### 3. Falsification and validation plan

Predefine the primary behavioral signatures before examining results:
1. whether channel weights change with controlled shifts in uncertainty;
2. whether interoceptive-channel ablation changes a prespecified affective-bias or uncertainty measure;
3. whether the model predicts held-out behavior better than simpler alternatives;
4. whether parameter values remain identifiable and stable under sensitivity analysis.

Compare IELF-class behavior against ablations and baselines (predictive-only, reward-learning-only, fixed-weight hybrid, and a model without an interoceptive channel). Use parameter recovery, out-of-sample prediction, sensitivity analysis, and multiple random seeds. A model that can fit any outcome by freely retuning weights is not a strong test.

The cardiac-phase memory claim should be treated as a separate empirical hypothesis requiring a specific task, preregistered timing measure, physiological recording, and replication—not as an automatic consequence of IELF.

### 4. Boundaries

This model concerns candidate computational/physiological mechanisms. It cannot, by itself, establish that the model is conscious, that human interoception is equivalent to a simulated channel, or that a soul or spirit exists. A model specification is not a numerical simulation; no code execution or result is claimed in this revision.

## Version 0.6.0 — Interoceptive Prioritisation and Evidence Boundaries
**Revision date:** 2026-10-23. **Status:** research addendum; implementation and numerical testing pending.

### Update
The 2026 preprint *Two Routes to Interoceptive Prioritisation* distinguishes two computational routes: increasing likelihood precision (sharpening inference about bodily signals) and reweighting preferences (changing how strongly the agent values outcomes that resolve a bodily need). In its simulated foraging task, these routes had different effects on perception, survival, and the body model learned; results on difficult layouts did not establish every predicted preference benefit. This supports treating the routes as dissociable hypotheses, not as a universal biological law. (S72)

### Model improvement
In the IELF model, define separate observables for interoceptive discrimination, chosen action, physiological state, and preference satisfaction. Manipulate precision and preference independently in a factorial design. Include a no-prioritisation baseline, precision-only, preference-only, and combined conditions. Assess calibration and held-out prediction in addition to survival/reward; do not infer a human physiological parameter range directly from a gridworld.

### Source
- S72. St John Grimbly, N. et al. (2026). “Two Routes to Interoceptive Prioritisation: Dissociating Likelihood Precision and Preference Reweighting in Active Inference.” arXiv:2610.18617. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18617. Preprint, submitted 13 October 2026.

**Limit:** this is a computational-agent result, not evidence that human interoception works identically, and not evidence about consciousness or soul.

# Research and Improvement Addendum — Revised 0.7.0

## 0.7.0 findings

### Embodiment is a multi-scale architecture, not a single mechanism
The September 2026 review by Yu et al. organizes embodied learning across sensorimotor/parietal-premotor integration, cerebellar forward-model and error processes, basal-ganglia/reward learning, and hippocampal/interoceptive-affective contextualization. Treat this as an integrative review framework, not a settled claim that every learning task uses each component equally. Predictive processing, reinforcement learning, and Hebbian plasticity are complementary candidate descriptions whose relative contribution must be estimated against task-specific baselines.

A relevant scale-specific example is Thornburg et al.'s 4D spatial-and-kinetic model of the genetically minimal bacterium JCVI-syn3A. It integrates genetic information processes, metabolism, growth, and division across a simulated cell cycle and recovers several measured properties. It is a model of a minimal bacterium, not a complete human-body model and not evidence for consciousness. It illustrates how multi-scale biological models can be constrained by data while remaining limited to their represented system.

### Two routes to interoceptive prioritisation
Grimbly et al. (IWAI 2026; arXiv:2610.18617) distinguish increasing likelihood precision from reweighting preferences for bodily outcomes. In their gridworld agent, precision sharpens interoceptive posteriors and improves survival; preference reweighting shifts the bodily states visited and the distribution over which the agent learns. The predicted preference benefit on difficult layouts was not established, so avoid presenting the routes as universally beneficial or already confirmed in humans.

For GAIA, define **interoceptive precision** as confidence/weight assigned to sensory evidence about bodily state, and **preference weight** as the relative desirability assigned to outcomes. These variables must be manipulated independently in a factorial model; do not collapse them into one “body priority” parameter.

## Required model-design corrections
1. Normalize and report the units/scales of precision and preference before combining them. A weighted sum is not interpretable if the terms are on incompatible scales.
2. Separate outcomes: interoceptive discrimination/calibration, action choice, survival or homeostatic regulation, and learning coverage.
3. Include null and ablation baselines, parameter-recovery tests, held-out environments, and sensitivity analyses.
4. Do not infer phenomenal experience from accurate bodily-state estimation or successful regulation.

## Revised 0.8.0 — Research Update: Embodied Learning and Interoceptive Routes

The IELF synthesis is retained as a candidate multilevel architecture, not a uniquely validated theory. Sensorimotor/parietal-premotor networks, cerebellar prediction-error learning, basal-ganglia reward updating, and hippocampal/interoceptive-affective context should be tested as separable contributions.

Grimbly et al.'s October 2026 preprint distinguishes increasing likelihood precision from reweighting outcome preferences. In a foraging gridworld with three lethal needs, both routes improved average survival; precision sharpened interoceptive posteriors, while preference reweighting changed visited states and the body-model coverage. The predicted preference benefit on difficult layouts was not established. This is computational evidence, not yet a universal human mechanism.

**Improved test:** independently manipulate precision and preference; measure discrimination, choice, survival, and learned-state coverage separately; compare predictive-processing, reinforcement-learning, and Hebbian baselines on held-out tasks; preregister ablations and failure criteria. Do not infer consciousness from adaptive performance.
