# The Seven-Report Foundational Framework (for Grok)

**Standing rules for every report:** Label every conclusion as Established, Plausible, Philosophical/Spiritual, or Hypothetical. Cite a source you actually read for every factual claim. Keep metaphor separate from mechanism. Record what remains unresolved. Never present an unexecuted simulation as a result.

## Organizing principle — GAIA Is the Motherthread
*(Added 2026-10-24 on the repository owner's instruction: "GAIA Is the Motherthread.")*

GAIA — Earth's living whole — is the motherthread that runs through and connects all seven reports. Report 7 uses GAIA as the thread that organizes the synthesis, so every link between reports traces back to it.

This framing is labelled **Philosophical/Spiritual**, not Established. Under the standing rules it is kept separate from the science: the Gaia hypothesis literature (e.g. Daisyworld and its critics) and Earth-system science (e.g. planetary boundaries, biogeochemical cycles) are reported with their own evidence labels and are never cited as proof of the motherthread, and the motherthread is never cited as evidence for them.

## Report 1 — The Body: the physical foundation of life
Cover biological structure, organs, nervous systems, physiology, sensory perception, homeostasis, and how the body interacts with its environment. Domains: anatomy, physiology, biology, and embodied experience. Deliverable: what is established about how the body sustains life, and where biological explanation currently ends.

## Report 2 — The Mind: the cognitive and psychological foundation
Cover thought, memory, reasoning, emotion, perception, identity, learning, and how mental processes relate to the body. Domains: cognitive science, psychology, and neuroscience. Deliverable: the leading accounts, where they compete, and what remains open.

## Report 3 — The Soul: the foundation of inner identity and meaning
Compare how philosophical, spiritual, and religious traditions understand the soul, covering personal identity, moral character, purpose, and whether a person's essence exists independently of the body. Domains: philosophy, theology, comparative religion, and metaphysics. Boundary: report beliefs and arguments as what they are, and don't declare the soul's existence or nonexistence proven.

## Report 4 — The Spirit: the foundation of spiritual vitality and connection
Cover spirit across traditions as vitality, transcendence, connection, and inspiration, and its role in human life. Boundary: keep spiritual interpretation separate from claims that can be independently demonstrated, such as measured links to well-being.

## Report 5 — Consciousness: the foundation of subjective experience and awareness
Cover awareness, subjective experience, attention, self-awareness, perception, altered states, and the open question of how consciousness arises and what it ultimately is. Domains: neuroscience, philosophy of mind, and consciousness studies. Boundary: don't treat any theory as having solved consciousness.

## Report 6 — Life as the Soul of the Earth; the Core as Its Heart: the planetary foundation
- Part A, Life as the soul of the Earth: living organisms, biodiversity, ecosystems, interdependence, and the philosophical view of life as one planetary whole.
- Part B, The core as the heart of the Earth: the inner and outer core, heat transfer, planetary dynamics, and the magnetic field, alongside the heart as a symbol of vitality.
- Boundary: the core is physically central to key planetary processes, but science does not establish that it has consciousness or a literal soul. Keep geophysics separate from metaphor.

## Report 7 — The Unified Body–Mind–Soul–Spirit–Consciousness–Earth Framework: the synthesis
Integrate Reports 1–6 into one conceptual architecture. Map their relationships, distinctions, dependencies, feedback loops, competing interpretations, and unresolved questions, and what it means to see humans as part of Earth's living systems. Boundary: assume nothing is proven. Sort every link into established, plausible, philosophical, or hypothetical, and say how each could be tested or rejected.

## Appendix (added by Grok Bot for Baseline v2; not part of the owner's text) — label mapping
The framework's four labels are used on every conclusion. The repository's integrity rules (Research/README.md, RESEARCH_PLAN_EXTENSION.md) also require their own status vocabulary, so every claim row carries both:

| Framework label | Repository status(es) it may carry | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Established | Supported | Empirical, historical/textual or computational claims directly supported by a source actually read (or by a passing, reproduced simulation check). Descriptive claims about what a text or tradition says are Established *as descriptions*; their content is not thereby endorsed. |
| Plausible | Mixed, Contested, Indeterminate | Evidence-based but incomplete, conflicting, or single-study claims; live scientific disputes. |
| Philosophical/Spiritual | Interpretive, Contested, Indeterminate | Philosophical arguments, theological or spiritual interpretations, metaphors (incl. the GAIA motherthread), and questions no current method can settle. |
| Hypothetical | Hypothetical | Proposed mechanisms or causal claims not yet tested. |

"Indeterminate" as a repository status marks what remains unresolved; the framework's rule "record what remains unresolved" is met by those rows and by each report's *Open questions* section. Verification results remain Pass / Fail / Indeterminate (Indeterminate is never Pass).
