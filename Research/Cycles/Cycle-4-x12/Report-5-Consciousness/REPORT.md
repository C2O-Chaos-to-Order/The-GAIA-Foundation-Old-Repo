## Abstract
Cycle 4 takes the carried item 'better criticality measure'. Wilting & Priesemann 2018 (Y01, full text) show spatial subsampling biases conventional branching-ratio estimates and derive a subsampling-invariant multistep-regression (MR) estimator (E); applied to rat, cat and monkey spikes it finds a reverberating, slightly subcritical state (P), consistent with Priesemann et al. 2014 (Y02, P). SIM-R5-C4 (12 iterations) replicates Y01's central result (conventional 0.32 vs MR 0.898 at true 0.9 with 10% sampling), shows avalanche exponents are distorted by subsampling and vanish under drive, and finds a weakness: slowly varying input inflates MR to 0.993 when the truth is 0.95, so non-stationary drive can mimic near-criticality. None of this says what consciousness is, or that any theory has solved it.

## Limitations
Y01 and Y02 come from the same group; animal data; no human consciousness measure was involved.

## Open questions and falsification
Does the MR estimate track loss of consciousness in humans when input non-stationarity is controlled? Rejection: no change of MR estimate across anaesthesia levels once drive is controlled.

## Conclusion and gate
