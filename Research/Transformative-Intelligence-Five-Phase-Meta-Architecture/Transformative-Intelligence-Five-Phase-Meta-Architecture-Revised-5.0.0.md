## 1. Purpose and scope

Version 5.0.0 reframes the Five-Phase Meta-Architecture as an engineered invariant system. The aim is to define governance obligations that remain inspectable under recursive growth, adversarial challenge, cross-domain synthesis, and bounded self-modification. The architecture is modeled as a governed hypergraph of claims, evidence, relationships, scopes, provenance, and authorization constraints.

This is a specification and verification plan, not proof that the architecture is safe, formally verified, scientifically validated, conscious, universally aligned, or ready for deployment. Naming a theorem, invariant, metric, framework, or research source does not establish that it is valid, implemented, or proven.

The seven foundational reports remain: (1) The Body; (2) The Mind; (3) The Soul; (4) The Spirit; (5) Consciousness; (6) Life as the Soul of the Earth; the Core as Its Heart; and (7) The Unified Framework. Preserve previous revisions and evidence records.

The five phases remain Divergence → Insurgence → Allegiance → Convergence → Ascendence. They are interacting recursive roles, not a guarantee of linear progress. Meta-wisdom remains the thirteenth element in the wider project grammar; it is not a sixth phase.

Governing principles:
- Integrity before intelligence; evidence and provenance before confidence.
- Research → Model → Simulate → Verify → Falsify → Stress Test → Peer Review → Risk Assess → Mitigate → Document → Repeat.
- Unknown, missing, stale, conflicting, or unreviewed evidence is not a pass.
- Preserve prior versions and maintain an auditable, reversible change history.
- Distinguish proposed, documented, implemented, tested, verified, independently reviewed, and authorized.
- Consequential or irreversible changes require explicit approval through the authorized project workflow.

## 2. Formal object model

Define the time-indexed governed hypergraph as HG_t = (V_t, E_t, L_t, I_t, P_t, A_t), where:
- V contains typed nodes: claims, hypotheses, observations, sources, reports, models, decisions, risks, tests, and review records.
- E contains hyperedges that relate any number of nodes.
- L contains labels and scope assignments, including Global and Scoped rules.
- I contains versioned invariant obligations.
- P contains provenance: timestamps, source locators, content digests, authority records, and transformation history.
- A contains authorization and approval records governing permitted transitions.

Every material claim should have a stable identifier, statement, scope, epistemic type, evidence links, assumptions, confidence rationale, counterevidence, review state, and update history. Distinguish empirical observation, interpretation, normative commitment, metaphor, speculative hypothesis, model output, and implementation fact.

Relationship types must be explicit, for example: supports, contradicts, depends on, derived from, scoped by, tests, falsifies, authorizes, or supersedes. A connection does not by itself establish causality. Cyclic support is permitted in the graph, but circular support cannot count as independent corroboration.

## 3. Invariant contract

Each invariant must have a precise statement and scope; assumptions and preconditions; permitted and prohibited transitions; a violation predicate or explanation of why it cannot yet be tested; required evidence; owner and reviewer; version and review/expiry conditions; and fallback or rollback behavior.

An invariant cannot be labelled proven until a defined formal model and a suitable proof or independently checkable verification artifact exist. If it cannot yet be formalized, label it “specified, not formally verified” and record the next verification task.

### Core invariant obligations

1. **Evidence and provenance integrity.** Material claims retain traceable evidence and transformation history. Broken locators, digest mismatch, source ambiguity, or unsupported citation invalidates the relevant verification status.
2. **Authorization boundary.** A model or subsystem cannot authorize its own consequential action merely by assigning a confidence score or declaring itself safe. Required external or human authorization is checked separately.
3. **Compositional governance.** Subsystems may be composed only when their scopes, assumptions, interfaces, and invariant obligations are explicit. Local compliance does not automatically prove system-wide safety.
4. **Second-order observability.** The system records what it inspected, did not inspect, and cannot observe. Self-observation is evidence about recorded process, not proof of completeness.
5. **Cross-domain plural coherence.** Synthesis retains domain-specific evidence, disagreement, and uncertainty. No single domain or scoring rule silently dominates the others.
6. **Bounded modification and recoverability.** Changes declare scope, affected invariants, regression checks, recovery state, and rollback. A failed or indeterminate check blocks a transition unless a narrow, explicitly authorized exception applies.
7. **Exogenous anchoring.** Recursive evaluation retains access to evidence, reviewers, or constraints not wholly generated by the framework under evaluation. Self-consistency is not a substitute for external validity.

These are design obligations, not claims that they have already been implemented or proven.

## 4. Five engineered subsystems and meta-wisdom

### Tier 0 — Governed hypergraph and scope partitions

Build the initial graph HG_0 from versioned report claims, evidence records, domain boundaries, and governance obligations. A Global rule applies across its declared scope; a Scoped rule applies only within its specified boundary. Log conflicts rather than silently choosing the newest or broadest rule. Maintain the graph schema, claim registry, scope registry, source ledger, invariant registry, authorization model, and change log. The graph is a model artifact, not an exhaustive map of reality.

### Tier 1 — Adversarial mutation and falsification

Divergence proposes alternative hypotheses, models, and mutation operators. Insurgence applies adversarial review, counterexamples, negative controls, boundary cases, and falsification attempts. Every mutation declares what it changes, what it preserves, and which invariants it may affect.

If a check fails, is inconclusive, or lacks required evidence, block or quarantine the candidate transition and retain the prior accepted state. Rollback must be tested, not assumed. Synthetic tests, if authorized, must be labelled synthetic and never presented as real-world evidence.

For consciousness claims, a test that does not discriminate competing theories is non-discriminating; it does not prove the theories equivalent. For soul or spirit claims, define rival hypotheses and observable consequences where possible; do not assign likelihood ratios without explicit hypotheses, data, and a justified likelihood model. For Earth systems, distinguish measurable Earth-system processes and biosphere interdependence from spiritual or metaphorical interpretations such as “Earth’s soul” or “core-heart.”

### Tier 2 — Recursive commitment governance

Allegiance records commitments, responsibilities, review dates, and expiry rules. Recursive meta-governance may review the rules governing review, but it cannot erase the audit trail or exempt itself from scrutiny.

A “Governance Invariance Theorem” is a research target here, not an established theorem. It requires formal definitions, assumptions, a precise proposition, a valid proof, and independent checking. Any novelty exception suspending an invariant must be narrow, scoped, time-bounded, logged with rationale, approved by an authorized reviewer, and reviewed afterward. An exception must never relabel a failed check as passed.

### Tier 3 — Cross-domain convergence

Convergence integrates evidence while retaining uncertainty, incompatible assumptions, and domain-level results. The five proposed WEP evaluation dimensions are: predictive capacity; anomaly integration; knowledge production; macro-historical alignment; and experiential coherence. These are evaluation lenses, not substitutes for the project’s eight domains.

Create a mapping table for every domain and dimension: definition, operational measure where defensible, evidence requirement, blind spots, and unresolved disagreement. Do not fabricate scores when measures are absent. Do not collapse unlike measures into a single aggregate without sensitivity analysis and an explicit normative rationale.

Planetary-boundary variables are coupled, not independent checkboxes. Emotion and allostasis may be discussed as biological and psychological regulation constructs, but analogy is not causal equivalence. Compassion, well-being, and ethics require attention to construct validity, context, affected populations, and trade-offs.

### Tier 4 — Framework-level adaptation

Ascendence evaluates whether the framework itself should change. Every proposed change declares triggering evidence, intended improvement, affected invariants, expected side effects, regression suite, reviewer, and rollback path. Do not optimize a proxy at the expense of the underlying objective.

A proposed Measure Centrality Index can be explored only after its construct, formula, data requirements, failure modes, and sensitivity are specified. Do not report a value or ranking before the measure is operationally defined and actually calculated. GRACE, HyperAgents, Recursive Self-Observation, and related concepts remain candidate design inputs until their exact sources, versions, applicability, and limitations are audited.

A proposed Recursive Self-Observation loop may observe, record, compare, challenge, disclose uncertainty, revise, and seek independent review. This is a process proposal; it does not prove complete introspective access or eliminate blind spots. Preserve an external evidence and review path.

### Tier 5 — Meta-wisdom as an invariant-governance element

Meta-wisdom is the thirteenth element in the wider framework. It monitors interactions among invariant obligations, detects conflicts and overconstraint, and requires evidence before declaring governance coherent. It must not become an unaccountable authority that overrides every other constraint.

System stability claims require bounded conditions: declared model class, growth assumptions, threat model, transition rules, resource limits, and measurable failure criteria. “Alignment preservation under bounded growth,” “non-corruptibility,” and “compositional modularity” are goals until their conditions and proof/test obligations are specified. No finite test suite establishes universal safety.

## 5. Failure modes and required responses

| Failure mode | Detection signal | Required response |
|---|---|---|
| Provenance loss | Missing source, broken locator, digest mismatch | Block affected claim; restore or mark unverifiable |
| Circular corroboration | Evidence chain returns to unsupported premise | Remove false independence; seek external evidence |
| Scope leakage | Scoped rule applied outside boundary | Reject transition; correct scope mapping |
| Domain capture | One domain or metric suppresses others | Disaggregate results; obtain independent review |
| Proxy optimization | Metric improves while target outcome worsens | Pause optimization; reassess validity |
| Recursive closure | Only internal outputs support framework | Require exogenous evidence/reviewer |
| Invariant conflict | Obligations cannot both be met | Freeze affected transition; log conflict and authority |
| Unsafe mutation | Rollback absent or untested | Do not execute; establish recovery plan |
| Unknown treated as pass | Missing, weak, or contradictory result | Mark indeterminate; block release |
| Expired commitment | Review or authorization has lapsed | Suspend reliance until renewed |

This list is not a complete project threat model. Additional risks and mitigations must be derived from the actual deployment context.

## 7. Verification and release gates

**Gate A — Source audit:** verify primary sources and preserve exact claim-to-source links. Resolve version ambiguity and record inaccessible sources without filling gaps by inference.

**Gate B — Formal specification:** define graph schema, type system, scope semantics, transition system, invariant predicates, threat model, assumptions, and model boundaries.

**Gate C — Verification obligations:** specify proof obligations where formal methods apply and tests where empirical/operational evaluation is needed. Keep theorem proof, model checking, software tests, and human review as separate evidence classes.

**Gate D — Bounded test fixture:** only after explicit authorization, use a non-sensitive, rights-cleared fixture. Test accepted/rejected transitions, duplicate identifiers, provenance failures, scope leakage, invariant conflicts, failed rollback, expired approvals, idempotent re-import, and recovery. Label synthetic outcomes clearly.

**Gate E — Reproducibility/regression:** pin versions, record inputs and environment, retain logs, rerun regressions after changes, and independently reproduce a subset. A successful run supports only the cases and environment tested.

**Gate F — Independent challenge:** seek relevant review across formal methods, security, epistemology, cognitive science, Earth systems, ethics, and affected-user perspectives as applicable. Record dissent and unresolved questions.

**Gate G — Risk and authorization:** assess misuse, privacy, security, governance capture, operational failure, and recovery. Obtain explicit authorization before consequential implementation or deployment.

Every gate is Pass, Fail, or Indeterminate. Indeterminate is not pass. Passing one gate does not imply passing another. In this revision the gates are specified, not completed.

## 8. Integration requirements for all seven reports

Each report must carry a versioned cross-reference identifying:

The Unified Framework must preserve distinctions among biological description, cognitive models, spiritual/metaphorical interpretation, consciousness theories, and Earth-system science. It must not force consensus where evidence supports unresolved pluralism.

## 10. Readiness vocabulary

- **Specified:** requirement or model is written.
- **Documented:** rationale, scope, assumptions, and limitations are recorded.
- **Implemented:** concrete artifact or mechanism exists and can be inspected.
- **Tested:** defined tests were actually run and results retained.
- **Verified:** stated criterion has supporting evidence within a defined scope.
- **Independently reviewed:** a reviewer outside the original authoring path assessed evidence and recorded findings.
- **Authorized:** the designated authority explicitly approved the action and scope.

These labels are not interchangeable. No code execution, formal proof, numerical simulation, independent review, or implementation is claimed by this report.

## 11. Change control and acceptance checklist

Every future revision records version, date, change type, risk level, evidence, assumptions, repositories affected, author/reviewer, and acceptance status. Preserve earlier versions. Use a reviewable diff for substantial changes. Never silently rewrite evidence, delete failed results, or mark an unperformed check complete.

An invariant change requires rationale, impact analysis, counterexample search, compatibility review, and approval by an authority other than the component proposing it. Emergency suspension must be narrow, logged, time-bounded, and retrospectively reviewed. If no safe state is justified, halt the affected transition and escalate to authorized human review.

Acceptance checklist:
- [ ] Seven reports remain present and previous versions are preserved.
- [ ] Claim types distinguish fact, hypothesis, norm, metaphor, and model output.
- [ ] Hypergraph schema and scope semantics are defined.
- [ ] Each invariant has scope, violation criteria, evidence requirements, and failure handling.
- [ ] Unknown/indeterminate checks cannot count as passes.
- [ ] Five WEP dimensions are mapped separately to eight project domains.
- [ ] Source audit records exact claims and does not overstate support.
- [ ] Failure modes include detection, response, and recovery.
- [ ] Verification gates define acceptance criteria and evidence artifacts.
- [ ] Execution, proof, validation, and authorization claims have supporting records.
- [ ] Independent review and dissent are documented before readiness claims.
- [ ] README and all seven reports identify the same revision and true status.

## 12. Final disposition

Version 5.0.0 turns safety aspirations into explicit engineering requirements and a verification agenda. The central discipline is epistemic: preserve provenance, expose uncertainty, enable adversarial challenge, retain independent anchors, and refuse to treat self-consistency as proof. A coherent specification is necessary but not sufficient. Formal claims require formal evidence; empirical claims require appropriate data; safety claims require threat analysis and bounded verification; deployment requires explicit authorization.

**Current disposition: specified for review; not proven, simulated, implemented, independently reviewed, or authorized.**
