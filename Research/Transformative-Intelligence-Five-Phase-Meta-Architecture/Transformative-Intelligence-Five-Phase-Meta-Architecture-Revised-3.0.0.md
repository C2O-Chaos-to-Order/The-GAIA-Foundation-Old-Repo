## Executive summary

Version 3.0.0 applies the five-phase pattern not only to the seven foundational reports, but to governance of the pattern itself. The five phases become epistemic governance functions: Divergence governs generation; Insurgence governs contestation; Allegiance governs stabilization; Convergence governs integration; Ascendence governs meta-evaluation. The thirteenth element, **Meta-wisdom**, recursively audits these functions.

The proposal aims to make perspective generation traceable, challenges falsifiable, commitments reviewable, synthesis losses visible, and meta-level conclusions accountable to first-order inquiry. It is not yet an empirically validated universal architecture.

## 1. Architecture and governance functions

### Tier 0 — Base reports as Super-Divergence
The seven reports—Body, Mind, Soul, Spirit, Consciousness, Earth, and Synthesis—preserve distinct explanatory levels and methods. The twelvefold structures for Good, Love, and Emotion and the eight Greater-Good domains add axiological, relational, affective, and practical perspectives.

- **Function:** generate and catalogue relevant perspectives without premature consensus.
- **Risk:** semantic proliferation, opaque claims, and self-referential systems that are difficult to check externally.
- **Control:** each materially considered perspective states its claim, claim type, evidence/provenance, uncertainty, rival accounts, and revision conditions. This does not require treating all claims as equally credible.

### Tier 1 — x3 as Super-Insurgence
Three-pass review is structured contestation of core assumptions, not merely surface proofreading.

- **Function:** challenge claims using predeclared criteria and preserve losing or unresolved positions.
- **Risk:** destructive critique, collision, or replacing one orthodoxy with another.
- **Control:** state the target, evidence and method, success criterion, possible disconfirmation, scope, and disposition of the challenged position.

### Tier 2 — x6 as Super-Allegiance
Six-pass review consolidates provisional commitments, shared terms, methods, and evidence standards.

- **Function:** enable cumulative work without equating commitment with truth.
- **Risk:** ossification and endless auxiliary adjustment to avoid meaningful failure.
- **Control:** commitments carry an owner, scope, review date, anomaly triggers, and renewal/revision/retirement decision. Expiration triggers review; it does not automatically invalidate a conclusion.

### Tier 3 — x9 as Super-Convergence
Nine-pass review integrates reports and disciplines while retaining differences that affect interpretation or decisions.

- **Function:** create traceable crosswalks among concepts, evidence, methods, and applications.
- **Risk:** lossy compression, false equivalence, or hidden power asymmetries.
- **Control:** every consequential synthesis produces a **Loss Ledger** recording distinctions retained, merged, omitted, incomparable, or unresolved; the rationale; and downstream consequences.

### Tier 4 — x12 as Super-Ascendence
Twelve-pass review evaluates the architecture's assumptions, comparison rules, and falsification criteria.

- **Function:** assess process calibration and reconnect meta-level claims with first-order evidence.
- **Risk:** detachment and meta-theoretical inflation.
- **Control:** every meta-level conclusion specifies a re-entry condition: an observation, replication, stakeholder review, comparison, or decision outcome that could strengthen, weaken, or revise it.

### Tier 5 — x13 as Super-Meta-Ascendence
The thirteenth element recursively governs the architecture itself.

**Four controller functions:**
1. **Rigidity detection:** detect overconfidence, unacknowledged anomalies, one-foundation dominance, and repeated ad hoc repairs.
2. **Conflict adjudication:** make value conflicts explicit; identify constraints, trade-offs, uncertainty, affected parties, and appeal routes without reducing all values to one score.
3. **Loss documentation:** maintain the Loss Ledger for consequential integrations.
4. **Expiration enforcement:** trigger review of time-indexed commitments and record whether they are renewed, revised, or retired.

Meta-wisdom is itself subject to the five phases: generate alternatives, invite challenges to its rules, adopt provisional procedures, integrate feedback, and evaluate whether revisions improve calibration. This is a design intention, not proof of self-correction.

## 2. Governance control matrix

| Phase | Control | Minimum record | Failure condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Divergence | Generation register | Perspective, claim type, evidence, uncertainty, relevance | Perspective is privileged or omitted without rationale |
| Insurgence | Challenge protocol | Target, criteria, counterevidence, result, disposition | Criteria shift after results or rival view is erased |
| Allegiance | Expiration/review clause | Owner, scope, review date, anomaly triggers | Commitment persists without review or is retired arbitrarily |
| Convergence | Loss Ledger | Merged/omitted distinctions, rationale, consequences | Integration hides material differences |
| Ascendence | Re-entry condition | Testable consequence, evidence path, reviewer, review point | Meta-level claim cannot be affected by first-order inquiry |
| Meta-wisdom | Controller audit | Trigger log, conflict decisions, appeals, calibration record | Controller is exempt from the audit it imposes |

### Authority and accountability
Each application must specify who proposes perspectives, evaluates evidence, approves provisional commitments, adjudicates conflicts, hears appeals, and audits the controller. No single AI model should be the sole arbiter of truth or the security boundary. Consequential uses require accountable authority, domain expertise, appropriate stakeholder input, and a documented appeal route.

## 3. Claim types and evidence boundaries

Distinguish at least:
1. **Empirical finding** — observation or measurement within stated methods and limits.
2. **Model result** — formal/computational output with assumptions and validation status.
3. **Methodological proposal** — a procedure proposed for testing, not a proven result.
4. **Philosophical interpretation** — an argument with explicit premises and inferential steps.
5. **Normative commitment** — a value or rule requiring justification; not derived from data alone.
6. **Metaphysical/spiritual interpretation** — preserved respectfully, but not called scientifically established without discriminating evidence.

Neural correlates do not alone prove or disprove metaphysical claims. Planetary-boundary assessments do not establish that Earth literally has a soul. A neuro-symbolic safety proposal does not demonstrate universal AI alignment.

## 4. Research anchors and source audit

### 4.1 Consciousness and empirical discipline
Owen and Goodale's 2026 *Neuron* perspective argues that major advances in consciousness research have often been driven by rigorous empirical work rather than overarching theoretical frameworks. This supports prioritizing measurable predictions and transparent falsifiers; it is a perspective article, not a consensus verdict against every grand theory.

- Owen, A. M., & Goodale, M. A. (2026). “Have grand theories of consciousness earned their keep?” *Neuron*, published online 6 October 2026. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.08.020
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42838059/

### 4.2 Planetary boundaries
The *Planetary Health Check 2026* reports seven of nine planetary boundaries transgressed. This is relevant to cross-domain risk governance; it does not validate the five-phase framework or prove planetary consciousness.

- Planetary Boundaries Science / Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research (2026). *Planetary Health Check 2026: A Scientific Assessment of the State of the Planet*. https://doi.org/10.48485/pik.2026.25
- Official overview: https://www.planetaryhealthcheck.org/
- Publication record: https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/faces/ViewItemOverviewPage.jsp?itemId=item_35009

### 4.3 GRACE as a proposed AI governance architecture
Jahn et al. introduce GRACE, a proposed neuro-symbolic containment architecture separating a Moral Module, Decision-Making Module, and Guard. It is a design precedent for separating normative constraints from instrumental action selection, not a settled universal solution to alignment.

- Jahn, F., Muskalla, Y., Dargasz, L., Schramowski, K., & Baum, P. (2026). “Breaking Up with Normatively Monolithic Agency with GRACE: A Reason-Based Neuro-Symbolic Architecture for Safe and Ethical AI Alignment.” arXiv:2601.10520. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10520

### 4.4 Multi-scalar compassion
Ozawa-de Silva and Mascaro propose a multi-scalar compassion framework spanning individual, group/institutional, and structural levels. This supports an analogy for governance across individual and institutional scales; it does not validate the exact twelve foundations of Love.

- Ozawa-de Silva, B. R., & Mascaro, J. S. (2026). “What’s next for compassion science? A multi-scalar framework for measurement and public health action.” *International Journal of Wellbeing*, 16(2). https://doi.org/10.5502/ijw.v16i2.5635

### 4.5 MKS–Thronaxis and Convergent Epistemology/WEP
Public materials describe MKS–Thronaxis as a research architectonic focused on stratified ontology and epistemic governance, and Convergent Epistemology/WEP as a multi-domain worldview-evaluation proposal. Here they are treated as framework proposals, not independently established scientific consensus. Their independence, scoring reliability, validation data, and susceptibility to confirmation bias require separate review.

- MKS–Thronaxis: https://mksthronaxis.org/
- Convergent Epistemology / WEP: https://www.convergentepistemology.com/

### Source-audit conclusion
These sources support selected design motivations, not the claim that postmodern pluralism universally culminated in one structural crisis, that vertical ordering is always superior to horizontal aggregation, or that the exact tier sequence is complete. Those claims remain hypotheses requiring operational definitions, counterexamples, and comparative tests.

## 5. Proposed validation plan — not executed

No code was executed for v3.0.0. These are proposed tests, not results.

1. **Traceability audit:** independent reviewers classify claims and assess whether sources support the exact wording and claim type.
2. **Challenge reproducibility:** independent reviewers apply identical predeclared criteria to the same case and compare dispositions.
3. **Expiration-review test:** assess whether review detects stale assumptions without mechanically discarding supported conclusions.
4. **Loss-ledger audit:** independent reviewers identify distinctions lost in integration and compare them with the recorded ledger.
5. **Re-entry test:** determine whether each meta-level recommendation yields a checkable observation, decision criterion, or accountable review.
6. **Conflict-adjudication test:** test conflicts among truthfulness, compassion, justice, autonomy, stewardship, and safety; retain plural outcomes when evidence does not justify a single winner.
7. **Controller attack test:** test adversarial framing, selective evidence, metric gaming, and self-protective revision.
8. **Baseline comparison:** compare against unstructured expert review, a conventional evidence table, and a fixed decision protocol using preregistered criteria.
9. **Calibration and harm audit:** measure error rates, uncertainty calibration, reversals after new evidence, dissent retention, decision latency, and distribution of harms.

**Preregistration fields:** question and hypotheses; unit of analysis; inclusion/exclusion rules; baselines; outcomes; scoring and missing-data rules; stopping and failure criteria; reviewer independence and conflicts; reproducibility materials and hashes; revision/abandonment conditions.

## 6. Limitations and open questions

- The phase order is a heuristic, not a demonstrated universal law; inquiry can branch, overlap, reverse, or skip phases.
- Pass counts x3, x6, x9, x12, and x13 are project conventions, not evidence of optimality.
- A generation quota must not become arbitrary quota-filling; relevance and evidence standards still matter.
- Expiration triggers review, not automatic belief retirement.
- A Loss Ledger cannot guarantee that every unrecognized loss will be detected.
- Cross-domain convergence can mislead when domains share data, assumptions, institutions, or sources; independence must be assessed.
- Empirical evidence cannot settle every normative conflict.
- Meta-wisdom is not exempt from the audit it applies to other layers.

## Conclusion

The Super-Upgrade reframes Transformative Intelligence as an epistemic governance proposal. Its core contribution is procedural: make perspective generation traceable, challenges falsifiable, commitments reviewable, synthesis losses visible, and meta-level claims accountable to first-order inquiry. Judge it by whether it improves traceability, calibrated uncertainty, fair treatment of dissent, and decisions under conflict—not by the elegance of its tier structure alone.
