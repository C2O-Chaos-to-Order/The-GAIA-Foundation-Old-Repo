## 1. Definition: distinguish scale and function

This framework assigns two different interpretive roles to electromagnetic phenomena. **Spirit** is the outward-facing pole of connection, vitality, and exchange with what exceeds a system. The cosmic electromagnetic spectrum—from radio waves to gamma rays—is a physical family of radiation through which Earth exchanges energy and information with its environment. In this architecture, that spectrum is a structural analogue for Spirit; it is not itself a conscious or spiritual agent.

**Consciousness** is subjective experience and the unity of an experienced scene. The brain produces endogenous electrical and magnetic fields as a consequence of neural currents. Those fields can influence neural activity under some conditions, so they are a legitimate subject of research into neural communication and possible mechanisms of integration. They are not, however, established as the substrate or identity of subjective experience. The endogenous field is therefore treated here as a **candidate physical contributor to binding**, not as proven consciousness.

| Dimension | Spirit — cosmic electromagnetic spectrum | Consciousness — endogenous neural electromagnetic field |
|---|---|---|
| Physical referent | Electromagnetic radiation across wavelengths/frequencies, including solar radiation and radiation exchanged with space | Electric and magnetic fields associated with neural currents and tissue conductivity |
| Scale | Astronomical, planetary, environmental | Local to organismal, neural tissue and nervous-system dynamics |
| Proposed architectural function | Connection, exchange, vitality, environmental coupling | Candidate contribution to neural coordination and integration |
| What is measured | Radiation flux, spectra, wavelength/frequency, absorption and emission | Electric potentials, magnetic signals, field strength, neural responses and coupling |
| Evidential boundary | Does not experience or intend | Field-to-neuron effects do not establish that the field is identical to experience |

The report's “medium versus organ” distinction is useful as a metaphor, but it is not a literal physical taxonomy. A brain's endogenous field is not simply a local packet or excitation of the entire cosmic spectrum. Both are described by electromagnetism, but their sources, boundary conditions, frequency ranges, spatial scales, and functions differ.

## 2. The cosmic spectrum as Spirit: Earth's exchange with its environment

NASA's overview of Earth's energy budget reports an average incoming solar flux at the top of the atmosphere of about **340 W/m²**, averaged over Earth's spherical surface. Approximately 29% is reflected to space, while about 71% is absorbed by the atmosphere and surface. The absorbed energy is ultimately balanced, over sufficiently long timescales, by outgoing radiation and other energy transfers. This is a useful physical foundation for the framework's metaphor of connection and exchange.

The numbers require precise wording. It is not accurate to say that all of the approximately 70% absorbed energy is immediately “re-emitted as thermal infrared” in the same place or by the same process. Energy is redistributed between surface, atmosphere, and ocean; the planet's top-of-atmosphere energy balance is maintained by reflected solar radiation plus outgoing longwave radiation. Greenhouse gases absorb and emit infrared radiation, shaping the vertical and spatial pathways of energy flow.

Atmospheric transparency also depends on wavelength, frequency, atmospheric composition, altitude, water vapor, clouds, and observing geometry. The optical/near-infrared region contains important transmission windows, but not one universally open optical band. Radio transmission is similarly windowed and frequency-dependent; a single range such as 5 MHz–300 GHz should not be presented as a uniform atmospheric window because absorption and opacity vary substantially across it. Use a frequency-resolved atmospheric transmission source for any numerical boundary claim.

Within the architecture, the cosmic spectrum can be called **Spirit in a structural or symbolic sense** because it represents exchange between Earth and the larger physical environment. This is a philosophical mapping, not a conclusion of astrophysics. The radiation has physical effects; those effects do not demonstrate that radiation is literally spirit, has intention, or possesses vitality as an intrinsic mental property.

## 3. Endogenous electromagnetic fields and neural integration

Neural activity generates extracellular electric fields and magnetic fields. These signals are measurable using methods such as EEG, MEG, and intracranial recordings, each with distinct spatial sensitivity, temporal resolution, and limitations. Extracellular fields can also feed back onto neuronal membrane potentials and influence spike timing; this is often discussed under ephaptic coupling or field effects.

A 2026 *Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience* article by Tam Hunt, “The goo that binds us: how field resonance solves neuroscience’s binding and criticality problems,” advances an electromagnetic-field hypothesis of neural integration. It discusses field-mediated entrainment, a reported 0.74 mV/mm threshold from cited experiments, and proposed advantages of field effects for large-scale integration. This is a **hypothesis-and-theory article**: it proposes an explanatory framework and interprets existing findings; it is not itself a demonstration that electromagnetic fields generate subjective experience or that the binding and criticality problems have been solved.

The report therefore distinguishes three claims:

1. **Established physical claim:** neural currents generate measurable endogenous electromagnetic fields.
2. **Empirical, bounded claim:** endogenous electric fields can influence neural activity in some experimental contexts; effect size and relevance depend on field geometry, frequency, tissue, neuronal state, and experimental design.
3. **Unsettled consciousness claim:** the endogenous field constitutes, explains, or is identical to subjective experience. The first two claims do not entail the third.

The “5,000-fold speed advantage” and approximate 50 km/s field-propagation figures discussed by Hunt depend on a specific interpretation of ephaptic/diffusive effects and comparisons with neural conduction. They must not be conflated with electromagnetic waves travelling at the speed of light, nor treated as an independently established whole-brain information-processing advantage. Propagation speed alone does not measure information capacity, causal integration, or phenomenal unity. Claims about “volumetric processing,” exponential capacity, or billions-fold computational advantages require formal definitions, quantitative derivations, and empirical tests before they can be treated as results.

The endogenous field is thus a plausible research target for questions about neural coordination and integration. It is not currently the “leading candidate” by universal scientific consensus, and the report should not say that it “most plausibly does what consciousness does” without specifying the theory and evidence standard being used.

## 4. The external-carrier hypothesis, biophotons, and zero-point fields

The strong claim that consciousness must be carried by external light or electromagnetic radiation is not supported by the evidence summarized here. People can remain conscious in darkness and in settings that reduce particular external electromagnetic inputs. These observations constrain theories that require a continuous external radiation carrier, though they do not exclude all possible electromagnetic contributions to neural activity.

Ultraweak photon emission from biological tissue is a research topic, but detecting photons is not the same as demonstrating a structured photonic code that supports cognition or conscious experience. The 2026 review by Pospíšil and Prasad, “Biofield in neural communication: A review and conceptual framework,” proposes possible roles for a neural biofield and biophotons. It is a review and conceptual framework, not proof that photons mediate cognition at the speed of light or that a cognition-sufficient code has been established.

Zero-point-field (ZPF) approaches are theoretical proposals and remain speculative. They must be kept separate from ordinary endogenous neural fields, measured biophoton emission, and the cosmic radiation spectrum. These are physically distinct referents. A future claim must state which phenomenon is meant, what causal mechanism is proposed, and what observation would distinguish it from conventional neural-network, synaptic, and field-coupling models.

## 5. Perspectives and what each does—and does not—establish

**Physicalist perspective.** Conscious experience depends on physical processes. Endogenous fields may be among the processes relevant to neural coordination. This does not by itself identify which physical process is sufficient for experience or explain why experience has a subjective character.

**Functionalist perspective.** Consciousness may be characterized by functions such as global availability, integration, flexible control, and reportability. An electromagnetic mechanism could potentially implement some functions, but functional implementation and subjective experience are not interchangeable claims.

**Critical perspective.** The slogan “consciousness travels through light” collapses several distinct propositions: that external radiation is necessary for consciousness; that the brain uses internal fields for communication; that photons provide an internal signaling pathway; and that electromagnetic conditions modulate neural activity. Each requires different evidence. Failure of an external-carrier hypothesis does not prove an endogenous-field theory.

**Architectural perspective.** Cosmic radiation is assigned to Spirit because it symbolizes environmental connection and exchange. Endogenous neural fields are assigned to Consciousness as a candidate analogue for binding and integration. These are structural interpretations. The assignment itself does not establish a new physical entity, a spiritual mechanism, or a theory of subjective experience.

The so-called hard problem—why physical processes are accompanied by subjective experience—remains unresolved by showing that a field can integrate signals or causally influence neurons.

## 6. Relationships across the GAIA framework

- **Body:** endogenous neural fields arise from bodily neural currents and tissue properties. Solar radiation supplies a major part of Earth's external energy budget, but the body also depends on food, chemical gradients, internal regulation, and other environmental conditions.
- **Mind:** cognition and emotion are processes involving distributed neural systems. Field effects may contribute to coordination, but their role relative to synapses, network dynamics, and other mechanisms remains under investigation.
- **Soul:** in this architecture, the geomagnetic field is a structural analogue for identity, boundary, and persistence. It is not the same phenomenon as an endogenous brain field.
- **Spirit:** the cosmic electromagnetic spectrum is a structural analogue for connection and exchange. It is not a synonym for the brain's endogenous field.
- **Subconscious:** Report 13 assigns the geomagnetic temporal spectrum a structural analogy with hidden, multiscale processes. That analogy does not imply that geomagnetic fluctuations encode personal thoughts or memories.
- **Earth:** the cosmic spectrum describes radiation exchanged with the environment; the geomagnetic field describes a planetary magnetic structure; the geomagnetic spectrum describes time-varying magnetic signals. None establishes planetary consciousness.
- **Chaos and order:** neural criticality is a research area concerning system dynamics. “Edge of chaos” is not a universal synonym for optimal cognition, and the presence of a power law alone does not prove criticality or an electromagnetic cause.

## 7. Uncertainty and operationalization requirements

The strongest claim—**“the electromagnetic spectrum is consciousness”**—is not supported. The cosmic spectrum is not known to experience anything. The narrower claim—**“endogenous neural electromagnetic fields may contribute to binding or integration relevant to consciousness”**—is a legitimate research hypothesis, but its constitutive role remains contested.

Any future empirical test should predefine:

- the target construct (neural field, photon emission, external radiation, ZPF, neural integration, reportable awareness, or subjective experience);
- the causal claim (correlation, modulation, enabling condition, constitution, or identity);
- the field quantity and measurement method, including spatial configuration, frequency range, signal-to-noise ratio, and artifact controls;
- a baseline model using established neural-network and synaptic mechanisms;
- predictions that distinguish field-mediated accounts from competing explanations;
- appropriate controls, blinding where possible, sample-size justification, uncertainty analysis, and independent replication criteria.

For an architectural or philosophical claim that is not empirically testable, the completion criterion is clear definitions, fair representation of alternatives, and an explicit boundary between evidence and interpretation—not a fabricated numerical simulation.

## 8. Conclusions

The electromagnetic spectrum can hold two architectural assignments only if the assignments refer to distinct scales and functions. The **cosmic electromagnetic spectrum** is a structural analogue for **Spirit**: the medium of physical exchange between Earth and its environment. The **endogenous electromagnetic field** is a candidate physical contributor to neural integration and therefore a structural analogue for **Consciousness** in this framework, not an established identity or complete explanation.

The spectrum is not conscious. The brain's electromagnetic field is not proven to be consciousness. The hard problem remains open. The useful distinction is between environmental exchange and neural integration, while preserving the difference between physical measurements and the spiritual or philosophical language used to interpret them.
