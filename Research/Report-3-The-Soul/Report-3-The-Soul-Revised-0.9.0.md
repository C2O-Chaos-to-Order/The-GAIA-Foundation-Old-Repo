## Scope
“Soul” has religious, philosophical, cultural, and personal meanings. For empirical comparison, distinguish a **robustly separable-soul hypothesis** (some relevant capacities or identity persist independently of brain function) from **brain-dependent hypotheses**, while recognizing that some soul views allow all observable manifestations to be brain-mediated. These hypotheses must be specified before evidence can discriminate between them.

## Comparative burden
Systematic dependence of memory, personality, agency, attention, and self-integration on brain organization places pressure on strong claims that these capacities operate independently of the brain. Compatibility is not confirmation: if a soul hypothesis predicts the same observations as a brain-dependent rival, those observations do not distinguish them.

For a specified observation `O`, compare `Λ = P(O | H_S) / P(O | H_B)`, where `H_S` is a precisely defined separability hypothesis and `H_B` is a specified brain-dependent alternative. `Λ > 1` favours `H_S` over that rival; `Λ < 1` favours `H_B`; `Λ ≈ 1` is non-discriminating. The result depends on the rival, priors, observation quality, and likelihood model. It is not a universal numeric score for “the soul.”

## Candidate observations
| Candidate | Current interpretation | What would strengthen discrimination |
|---|---|---|
| Brain injury or disease changes memory/personality | Expected under brain-dependent accounts; compatible with some mediated-soul accounts | A distinct prediction from a specified separability model |
| Near-death experience reports | Important first-person reports; interpretation contested | Prospective design, independently verified target information, controls for timing and residual/recovering neural function |
| Anomalous information acquisition | An unexplained report alone is not proof | Preregistered protocols, blinding, independent corroboration, replication, complete reporting |
| Soul-specific causal effect | Hypothetical until operationalized | An intervention with predictions not equally expected under a plausible brain-dependent rival |

The evidential bar should be symmetric: do not dismiss observations solely because they challenge a preferred model, and do not treat them as confirmation before alternatives and replication are assessed.

## Decision rule
The separability hypothesis earns empirical preference only when a robust, independently replicated observation is appreciably more expected under its explicit predictions than under the best specified brain-dependent alternatives. If no such observation is established, the hypothesis may remain logically or spiritually meaningful without being empirically preferred. This is not proof of nonexistence.

A fully brain-mediated soul account may be logically compatible with neural dependence, but if it predicts no observations different from a brain-dependent rival, it is empirically underdetermined.
