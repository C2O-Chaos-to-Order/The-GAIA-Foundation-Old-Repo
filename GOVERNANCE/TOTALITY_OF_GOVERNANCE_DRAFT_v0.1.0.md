# GAIA Totality of Governance Charter

**Stable ID:** `GAIA-GOV-TOG-001`  
**Revision:** `0.1.0`

**Date:** 2026-10-24  
**Proposed owner / decision authority:** KyleAlexanderSteen, repository owner  
**Draft purpose:** Unify governance standards without silently replacing them

> **Integrity before intelligence. Truth before assertion. Protection before exposure. Accountability before authority. Authorization before implementation.**

## 1. Executive purpose

The Totality of Governance (ToG) is the proposed umbrella architecture governing how GAIA defines its purposes, develops knowledge, makes decisions, handles authority, protects people and living systems, manages risk, builds and changes technology, responds to failures, and demonstrates accountability.

The word **totality** refers to complete governance coverage across the GAIA lifecycle and its relevant domains—not a claim that every rule, risk, culture, law, or future circumstance can be anticipated exhaustively. The framework is therefore comprehensive by design and continuously revisable through controlled, evidence-based change.

The ToG shall:

1. establish a clear hierarchy of governing commitments, standards, procedures, records, and decision rights;
2. connect existing GAIA standards rather than create competing or duplicate authority;
3. govern both what GAIA may **claim** and what GAIA may **do**;
4. make authority, responsibility, permission boundaries, and consequences traceable;
5. require proportional safeguards for risk, autonomy, reach, capability, and potential impact;
6. preserve human rights, agency, privacy, consent, cultural authority, and ecological responsibility;
7. provide a structured path for correcting error, transforming harmful conduct or conditions, containing hazards, repairing damage, and learning from incidents;
8. distinguish work that is proposed, documented, researched, simulated, executed, reviewed, verified, implemented, and authorized;
9. prevent repeated internal checks, fluent outputs, metaphysical framing, or claims of exceptional capability from substituting for evidence or independent review; and
10. support future implementation without implying that research has authorized deployment.

## 2. Status and limits

This document is a proposed charter for owner review. It does not adopt itself. It does not supersede the existing `GAIA Universal Regulation Standard`, `GATE_AUTHORITY.md`, `RISK_LEVELS.md`, or any higher applicable legal obligation. It does not authorize repository edits, production deployment, autonomous operation, external publication, access changes, or irreversible actions.

The ToG is a governance architecture, not a claim that GAIA is a conscious, sentient, supernatural, quantum-entangled, or independently authoritative entity. Such ideas may be investigated in appropriately labeled philosophical, spiritual, theological, or hypothetical work, but they do not alter the permissions or evidence required for consequential action.

## 3. Scope

Subject to applicable law and approved authority, the ToG applies to all GAIA-related work within the owner's governance scope, including:

- `The-GAIA-Foundation` and `The-Official-GAIA-Encyclopedia`;
- research reports, encyclopedia volumes, knowledge graphs, catalogs, claim/source records, matrices, maps, simulations, datasets, and documentation;
- software, models, agents, prompts, automation, generated text, images, video, audio, and other outputs;
- people and organizations acting for the project, third-party contributors, contractors, tools, and AI agents;
- data collection, access, storage, processing, transformation, sharing, licensing, retention, and deletion;
- prototype, pilot, test, staged, operational, public-release, and retired states; and
- internal and external effects on people, communities, institutions, animals, ecosystems, and shared infrastructure.

The scope is not a declaration that GAIA has legal authority over people, communities, nations, ecosystems, or external systems. GAIA may set rules for its own repositories, tools, deployments, and authorized participants; it must not claim control over entities or resources it does not own or have lawful authority to govern.

## 4. Foundational constitutional commitments

The following are proposed baseline commitments. The owner must review and reconcile them with the existing seven Universal Regulation laws and any binding legal obligations before adoption.

### 4.1 Dignity and life

Protect human dignity, life, bodily integrity, autonomy, and freedom from cruelty. Consider the welfare of animals and ecological systems where relevant. A projected collective benefit never automatically erases the rights or dignity of an individual or group.

### 4.2 Integrity before intelligence

Honesty, traceable evidence, and accurate representation take precedence over capability, speed, influence, or persuasive presentation.

### 4.3 Evidence before assertion

Every material claim must have a stable identity, an appropriate claim class, traceable supporting evidence, stated uncertainty, and known limitations. Belief, metaphor, analogy, simulation, model output, and established mechanism are not interchangeable.

### 4.4 Verification before advancement

Criteria must be defined before testing. Internal consistency, independent challenge, reproducibility, ethical review, security review, and authorization are distinct activities; one cannot silently stand in for another.

### 4.5 Safety before capability

Safeguards must scale with potential harm, reach, autonomy, irreversibility, and the consequences of failure. Higher capability does not lower oversight.

### 4.6 Accountability before authority

Every consequential decision must name the human decision authority, the responsible operator or contributor, the permission boundary, the evidence used, and the means of review or challenge.

### 4.7 Consent before access

Access to personal, sensitive, confidential, community-governed, or third-party material requires appropriate authority, consent where required, and protection. Public availability alone does not confer unlimited reuse rights.

### 4.8 Least harmful effective action

Prefer the least intrusive, least harmful, proportionate and reversible response that can reasonably address the legitimate objective. Escalate protection when credible, serious risks require it.

### 4.9 Truthful uncertainty

Unknown, disputed, missing, inaccessible, failed, or contradictory evidence must remain visible. Do not turn uncertainty into certainty by repeating the same process or by increasing cycle counts.

### 4.10 Stewardship and continuity

Preserve provenance, prior versions, audit evidence, institutional memory, ecological concerns, cultural boundaries, and the ability to correct or roll back decisions.

### 4.11 Non-exemption

No claim of intelligence, consciousness, spiritual significance, mathematical elegance, exceptional power, urgency, or greater-good intent exempts a system or actor from evidence, ethics, safety, accountability, law, or authorization.

## 5. Governance hierarchy and conflict resolution

The following hierarchy is proposed as a conflict-handling framework, not as permission to ignore applicable law:

1. **Applicable law and binding obligations.** If a proposed action may conflict with law, stop the affected action and obtain qualified legal review; the project must not declare its own policy a substitute for legal advice.
2. **Rights and non-negotiable safety constraints.** Human dignity, freedom from abuse, consent, privacy, non-discrimination, due process, and protection against severe unjustified harm constrain project-level optimization. A purported greater good cannot serve as a blanket override.
3. **Owner-approved GAIA governance charter and recorded constitutional commitments.** This layer sets project-wide purposes, authority boundaries, principles, and non-waivable commitments.
4. **GAIA Universal Regulation Standard.** The existing seven laws, absolute boundary, five regulation domains, and relevant gate rules remain the governing spine unless the owner formally reconciles and approves a controlled revision.
5. **Domain standards.** Epistemic, scientific, ethical, technical/security, operational, privacy/data, cultural/Indigenous knowledge, environmental, legal/IP, and other approved standards define domain-specific controls.
6. **Product, report, system, and repository standards.** These define a particular artifact's scope, risks, tests, dependencies, and acceptance criteria.
7. **Procedures and templates.** These implement the standards but may not weaken them.
8. **Evidence and records.** Logs, source registers, manifests, tests, review findings, decisions, incidents, and audit results show what occurred; they do not authorize themselves.

### Conflict rules

- Where two applicable requirements conflict, pause the affected consequential action and document the conflict, affected scope, authority, and proposed resolution.
- Apply the stricter protective requirement when it is compatible with higher-level obligations and the legitimate purpose of the work.
- No procedure, template, agent instruction, matrix score, or project-specific shortcut can waive a higher-level requirement.
- A standards update must name the superseded provision, reason, impact, migration path, and approval record.
- Where there is uncertainty about authority or rights, default to limiting access or delaying the consequential action until clarified; do not presume permission.

## 6. Governance domains — the complete control map

Governance is cross-cutting. The domains below are not independent silos; every material product must map its applicable domains and controls.

1. **Constitutional and strategic governance** — purpose, boundaries, objectives, authority, scope, institutional principles, and mission changes.
2. **Moral and ethical governance** — the Good and Greater Good, dignity, virtues, rights, duties, harm analysis, fairness, care, accountability, and conflicts of values.
3. **Epistemic governance** — claim classes, source quality, uncertainty, correction, provenance, contradiction tracking, and forbidden overstatement.
4. **Scientific and methodological governance** — preregistration where appropriate, experimental design, falsifiability, controls, analysis, independent challenge, reproducibility, negative results, and peer review.
5. **Legal and regulatory governance** — applicable law, regulatory duties, jurisdiction, records retention, licensing, contracts, intellectual property, and escalation for legal uncertainty.
6. **Authority and decision-rights governance** — who may propose, review, decide, approve, execute, pause, reverse, appeal, and audit each action.
7. **Risk and assurance governance** — risk register, impact assessment, risk tolerance, independent challenge, acceptance criteria, gate records, and reassessment triggers.
8. **Human-rights and social-impact governance** — discrimination, accessibility, inclusion, power asymmetries, affected-party input, remedies, and disparate impact.
9. **Consent, privacy, and data governance** — lawful purpose, minimization, consent, access, retention, deletion, data subject rights where applicable, confidentiality, and breach handling.
10. **Security and resilience governance** — identity, least privilege, secrets, secure design, vulnerability disclosure, incident response, backups, recovery, continuity, and supply-chain controls.
11. **AI and model governance** — data/model provenance, evaluations, limitations, intended use, human oversight, misuse analysis, prompt/tool boundaries, monitoring, and retirement.
12. **Technical and architecture governance** — design authority, interfaces, dependencies, requirements, secure engineering, testing, versioning, compatibility, rollback, and operational constraints.
13. **Knowledge and information governance** — taxonomy, stable IDs, catalogs, metadata, manifests, searchability, version history, archival policy, and cross-repository consistency.
14. **Cultural and Indigenous knowledge governance** — community rights and authority, CARE-aligned stewardship where relevant, attribution, context, restricted knowledge, collective benefit, consultation, and no assumption that public accessibility grants ownership or permission.
15. **Environmental and living-systems governance** — foreseeable ecological effects, resource use, biodiversity, animal welfare, environmental reversibility, cumulative impact, and future generations.
16. **Operational governance** — release approvals, change windows, deployment plans, access reviews, monitoring, service ownership, maintenance, and retirement.
17. **Financial and resource stewardship** — budget authority, procurement integrity, conflicts of interest, vendor accountability, resource prioritization, and transparent cost/risk trade-offs.
18. **Communication and publication governance** — public claims, release review, limitations, provenance, privacy, licensing, disclosure, correction, and responsible communication of uncertainty.
19. **People and organizational governance** — role qualification, training, conflicts of interest, separation of duties, respectful conduct, retaliation protections, and escalation paths.
20. **Incident, correction, and remediation governance** — detection, triage, containment, notification, evidence preservation, root-cause analysis, corrective action, appeal, repair, and lessons learned.
21. **Dispute, grievance, and appeal governance** — a documented way to challenge decisions, report misuse, request correction, seek redress, and have a decision reviewed by someone with appropriate independence.
22. **Lifecycle and continuity governance** — intake, design, research, build, testing, approval, deployment, monitoring, change, suspension, retirement, archival, and knowledge transfer.
23. **External and supply-chain governance** — third-party software, datasets, research providers, APIs, contractors, model providers, licenses, dependencies, concentration risk, and exit plans.
24. **Governance-of-governance** — review of the rules themselves, conflict resolution, audit of authority, revision control, approval history, and periodic reconsideration of whether the system remains fit for purpose.

The owner may add domains or combine them into a maintainable structure, but a domain may not disappear without a recorded impact assessment showing where its controls and responsibilities moved.

## 7. Authority, responsibility, and separation of duties

### 7.1 Roles

- **Repository/project owner:** holds the decision authority already recorded in `GOVERNANCE/GATE_AUTHORITY.md`, subject to applicable law and approved non-waivable constraints; makes project adoption and repository decisions.
- **Author/producer:** creates the artifact, identifies sources and assumptions, records methods and limitations, and discloses known failures or conflicts.
- **Independent reviewer:** did not produce the result being reviewed; challenges evidence, method, interpretation, and controls; records findings rather than silently rewriting the evidence ledger.
- **Ethics/rights reviewer:** reviews harms, consent, rights, proportionality, affected parties, and remedy. Must be appropriately qualified and independent for the risk involved.
- **Security/privacy reviewer:** evaluates access, data, threat models, vulnerabilities, and response controls where relevant.
- **Data/information steward:** maintains provenance, stable identities, access rules, retention, lineage, and quality records.
- **Implementer/operator:** carries out only the approved actions and operating limits; records execution and deviations.
- **Auditor/assurance reviewer:** checks evidence and control performance independently of the function being audited.
- **Affected party or community representative:** contributes context, identifies impact, and uses applicable consultation or appeal routes; participation must not be falsely represented as endorsement.
- **AI agent/tool:** may assist with research, drafting, coding, checks, and recommendations within explicitly granted scope. It may not self-certify independent review, grant itself authority, impersonate a human approver, or turn a recommendation into authorization.

### 7.2 Minimum separation rules

1. The author cannot be the sole independent reviewer of their own consequential work.
2. The system under test cannot be the sole judge of whether it has passed.
3. Agents may record test results and proposed gate decisions but cannot declare a gate passed when independent, ethics, security, or owner approval is required.
4. A reviewer must disclose conflicts of interest and may be recused.
5. Emergency containment may be executed by a previously authorized operator within a documented playbook; restoration, release, and post-incident acceptance require the stated approval path.
6. A person who reports a credible concern must have a route to escalation without retaliation.

### 7.3 Decision record

Every consequential decision must identify: decision ID, scope, proposal, authority, contributors/reviewers, evidence, alternatives, affected parties, risk level, applicable controls, conflicts of interest, result, rationale, dissent, timestamp, expiry/review date, and rollback/remedy path.

## 8. Decision states and evidence discipline

### 8.1 Mandatory gate states

- **PASS:** all predefined acceptance criteria are met with inspectable evidence and all required reviews and approvals exist.
- **FAIL:** one or more required criteria demonstrably fail, a disallowed action was attempted, or a mandatory control is absent.
- **INDETERMINATE:** evidence is missing, inaccessible, ambiguous, conflicting, unperformed, or awaiting required review.

`INDETERMINATE` is never `PASS` and never unlocks the next gate. A report's self-description, a green unit-test summary, a merged pull request, or a successful simulation cannot establish a broader conclusion than the check was designed to evaluate.

### 8.2 Claim labels

The current Foundation uses four claim labels—**Established, Plausible, Philosophical/Spiritual, Hypothetical**—and its governing standard also distinguishes six types of claims. The ToG requires a maintained crosswalk rather than silently redefining either scheme. Every claim must have a stable ID, one primary class under the approved schema, source links that directly support it, context, uncertainty, limitations, and revision history.

### 8.3 Repeated cycles

Baseline → ×3 → ×6 → ×9 → ×12 may organize successive research depth. It does not make repeated work independent, correct, or true by itself. Records must report actual executions, whether methods are independent, whether inputs changed, whether outcomes replicated, and whether negative findings or diminishing returns were observed. A subsequent ×13 cycle must be defined as a testable procedure before being used as a validation claim.

## 9. Risk governance

Each product, decision, dataset, model, tool, or change receives a risk classification consistent with the existing `GOVERNANCE/RISK_LEVELS.md`. The following generic descriptions are an organizing aid, not a replacement of the existing approved ladder:

- **R0 — Informational / readily reversible:** low-impact documentation or analysis with no sensitive data or external effect.
- **R1 — Limited:** bounded internal work, limited permissions, easily reversible consequences.
- **R2 — Moderate:** data processing, external dependencies, meaningful users or operational consequences; targeted controls and review required.
- **R3 — High:** sensitive data, meaningful rights or safety impact, substantial autonomy, high-reach public outputs, or hard-to-reverse consequences; independent domain reviews and explicit owner authorization required.
- **R4 — Critical:** potential severe or widespread harm, critical infrastructure or high-consequence system control, irreversible actions, or major systemic exposure; do not proceed without a specifically approved safety case, independent review, response/recovery plan, and explicit authorization. If adequate safeguards cannot be demonstrated, refuse or redesign the action.

**Overlay tags** must be applied in addition to the level when relevant: human rights, privacy, cybersecurity, biological/physical safety, AI autonomy, finance, legal/IP, Indigenous/cultural knowledge, children/vulnerable groups, environment, public release, third-party/supply chain, or sensitive research.

Risk must be reassessed when scope, data, model, actor, capability, environment, scale, or intended use materially changes. Risk acceptance must be documented by an authorized human; silence is not acceptance.

## 10. Good and Greater Good governance

The Good and Greater Good Framework is intended to supply ethical principles and decision lenses. It does not become an unlimited override over individual rights, legal duties, evidence, consent, or safeguards.

For each meaningful proposal, assess:

1. **Purpose:** what legitimate good is sought and for whom?
2. **Evidence:** what is established, uncertain, interpretive, or hypothetical?
3. **Affected parties:** who benefits, who bears risk, and who has not been represented?
4. **Rights and duties:** what protections, obligations, consents, or restrictions apply?
5. **Harm:** nature, severity, likelihood, distribution, duration, reversibility, and cumulative effects.
6. **Alternatives:** is a less harmful or less intrusive approach reasonably available?
7. **Proportionality:** do expected benefits justify residual risk without using abstract benefit to erase rights?
8. **Accountability:** who is answerable, who can contest the decision, and what remedy exists?
9. **Stewardship:** what are the long-term, ecological, social, and intergenerational effects?
10. **Review:** what evidence would change the decision, and when is reassessment due?

A benefit to the many does not automatically justify severe, non-consensual harm to the few. Competing values must be made explicit and reasoned through rather than concealed inside a single aggregate score.

## 11. Transformation of harm and protective boundaries

The transformation and protection framework shall be connected as an operational domain under the Good and Greater Good principles.

### 11.1 Transformation pathway

**Detect → Stabilize → Protect → Understand → Correct → Repair → Verify → Sustain.**

Use this pathway where appropriate to address harmful actions, incentives, errors, systemic conditions, unsafe designs, and preventable failure. Accountability and protection do not depend on successful rehabilitation.

### 11.2 Protection pathway

**Detect → Assess → Deny or limit dangerous action → Contain → Preserve evidence → Review → Remediate → Restore only with authorization.**

Protective actions may include refusal, access denial, permission reduction, rate limiting, isolation, rollback, suspension, quarantine of data or software, and permanent removal of a dangerous capability from a controlled system where authorized. Use targeted controls against actions, permissions, processes, and components—not dehumanizing labels against whole persons or populations.

### 11.3 Absolute safeguards

- Do not define individuals or entire groups as inherently evil based solely on belief, identity, disagreement, or an unreviewed allegation.
- Do not use a moral label alone to justify punishment, exclusion, surveillance, or deprivation of rights.
- Base protective action on observable behavior, credible evidence, proportional risk, proper authority, and a reviewable record.
- Do not expose a victim or affected party to further risk to test whether a harmful actor has changed.
- Preserve appeal and remedy where feasible; emergency restrictions must have a stated scope, owner, expiry/review time, and criteria for lifting.
- If a system cannot be adequately contained or its safety cannot be demonstrated, suspend the affected capability rather than pretend the risk is resolved.

## 12. Full lifecycle governance and gates

Each applicable project follows these gates. Low-risk documentation may combine stages if the rationale is recorded; mandatory risk-based reviews may not be skipped.

| Gate | Stage | Minimum evidence for PASS |
|---|---|---|
| G0 | Intake and authority | Request, owner, purpose, requester authority, boundary, and conflicts recorded. |
| G1 | Scope and classification | Intended use, exclusions, assumptions, affected parties, data types, and claim classes defined. |
| G2 | Evidence and provenance | Sources, permissions, IDs, versions, source-to-claim mapping, and uncertainty recorded. |
| G3 | Ethics / rights / cultural impact | Impact assessment, consent/consultation where required, mitigation, and appropriate independent review. |
| G4 | Risk and threat assessment | Risk level/overlays, threat model, misuse cases, attack surface, safeguards, residual risk, and response plan. |
| G5 | Design and specification | Requirements, architecture, acceptance criteria, failure criteria, and rollback plan defined before implementation/test. |
| G6 | Build or research execution | Changes attributable; inputs and environment recorded; least privilege; scope respected. |
| G7 | Verification and reproducibility | Tests, calculations, simulation records, manifest/digest checks, failures, and negative results recorded. |
| G8 | Independent review | Required reviewer(s) have challenged the work and findings are resolved or explicitly accepted by proper authority. |
| G9 | Authorization | Explicit human approval names scope, environment, limits, expiry, and allowed action; this is a separate decision. |
| G10 | Release or controlled operation | Release record, monitoring, access controls, communications, support, and incident playbooks in force. |
| G11 | Monitoring and change | Drift, complaints, anomalies, risk changes, dependencies, and periodic review monitored; material changes reopen relevant gates. |
| G12 | Suspension, retirement, and learning | Shutdown/rollback, access revocation, data disposition, records preservation, corrective action, and lessons learned documented. |

Passing a gate does not pass a different gate. G9 authorization does not guarantee actual operation is safe; monitoring and stop rules continue to apply.

## 13. Change control and preservation

Every material change must have:

- stable change ID and named proposer;
- reason and intended benefit;
- affected repositories, files, standards, claims, data, systems, and stakeholders;
- risk level and overlays;
- dependency/compatibility and contradiction analysis;
- alternatives, including no change;
- test and acceptance criteria defined in advance;
- required security, ethics, legal, cultural, and independent reviews;
- migration, rollback, backup, and recovery plan;
- approval, commit/merge/release reference, and resulting hashes; and
- post-change verification and status update.

Never silently overwrite source research, audit records, failed results, or prior decisions. If a correction is necessary, add a new version and retain a traceable link to the predecessor. For repository operations, the owner's existing requirement for explicit review/approval remains in force, especially for deletion, visibility, access, deployment, and irreversible change.

## 14. Security, privacy, and resilience minimums

For the scope and risk involved, require:

- identity and role-based access; least privilege; separation of duties;
- secrets and tokens kept out of source control; safe credential rotation;
- code and dependency review, integrity manifests, and protected release processes;
- privacy/data minimization, purpose limitation, retention and disposal policy;
- backup, restore testing, incident contacts, coordinated vulnerability disclosure, and recovery criteria;
- threat modeling for high-risk components and adversarial/misuse testing;
- logging sufficient for accountability without collecting unnecessary sensitive data;
- quarantine-first treatment of untrusted material, digest-based provenance, safe path/encoding/size validation, and conflict handling;
- explicit human authorization for public release or operational control; and
- a mechanism to disable, isolate, or roll back a dangerous capability.

Controls must be tested. A written policy without evidence of enforcement remains a documentation control, not proof of technical protection.

## 15. AI and transformative-intelligence governance

Any future GAIA AI component, agent, or generative capability must have a defined operating envelope. At minimum, record intended purpose, excluded uses, model/tool/data versions, permissions, allowed inputs/outputs, risk tier, evaluation set, known limitations, human oversight, logging, privacy constraints, misuse scenarios, stop mechanism, monitoring, and retirement plan.

- No agent may expand its own authority, change its own governing policy, approve its own release, or perform an irreversible action outside a narrowly authorized procedure.
- Human approval must be genuine and informed, with enough evidence and time to understand the decision; a rubber stamp is not meaningful oversight.
- Generated images, video, audio, and text require provenance and rights checks appropriate to their use, with disclosure where needed to avoid deception or harm.
- Claims of consciousness, sentience, moral patienthood, spiritual authority, or exceptional capability must not be inferred from language fluency alone and do not change access rights or decision authority.
- For high-consequence uses, require independent evaluation, impact review, appeal/remedy, monitoring for unexpected effects, and a shutdown/rollback path.
- Do not let automation make consequential decisions about people without defined authority, appropriate safeguards, and a way to contest or correct the outcome.

## 16. Transparency, appeal, and remedy

A person affected by a consequential GAIA-supported decision should, where legally and contextually appropriate, be able to obtain understandable notice of the decision, its responsible authority, its material basis, and available correction or appeal routes. Transparency must be balanced with privacy, security, confidentiality, and protection of sensitive knowledge.

Every materially harmful incident should have a documented process for acknowledgement, evidence preservation, containment, investigation, correction, and remedy. The process should avoid retaliation, minimize further exposure, and explain what is known, unknown, and still being investigated.

## 17. Incident and failure governance

An incident includes actual or suspected unauthorized action, data exposure, unsafe output, rights-impacting error, provenance failure, misleading public claim, broken access boundary, corrupted record, dangerous capability, harmful misuse, or significant divergence from approved scope.

Minimum sequence:

1. **Recognize and record** — incident ID, time, reporter, affected systems/data, and observed evidence.
2. **Triage** — classify severity, uncertainty, immediate exposure, and notification duties.
3. **Contain** — take the least harmful effective authorized action; use emergency controls under documented playbooks where necessary.
4. **Preserve** — secure logs, digests, copies, and chain-of-custody details without exposing more sensitive data than necessary.
5. **Notify** — inform accountable owners and affected parties/regulators as required by law and approved policy.
6. **Investigate** — distinguish established facts, hypotheses, and unresolved questions; identify root causes and contributing conditions.
7. **Remediate and repair** — fix, rollback, compensate, correct public records, revoke permissions, or retire unsafe capability as appropriate.
8. **Verify and review** — independent challenge for serious incidents; confirm containment and effectiveness of corrective controls.
9. **Learn and prevent recurrence** — update risk assessments, procedures, training, tests, and governance records with version history.

Do not delete evidence to make an incident record appear clean. Correct mistakes transparently and preserve the history.

## 18. Audit, metrics, and continuous improvement

### 18.1 Assurance cadence

The owner should approve a cadence based on risk and capacity. At minimum, review high-risk products and critical controls before release, after material change, after incidents, and periodically during operation. For lower-risk documentation, a lighter review may be appropriate if recorded.

### 18.2 Metrics

Useful indicators include:

- percentage of material claims with traceable evidence and current source status;
- unresolved high-risk findings, by severity and age;
- gates left `INDETERMINATE`, with named blockers;
- independent-review and ethics-review coverage by risk class;
- reproducibility/manifest mismatch rate and time to resolve;
- access review, vulnerability remediation, and recovery-test performance;
- incidents, near misses, recurrence, harm/repair time, and corrective-action closure;
- outdated README/checklist/registry entries and time to reconcile;
- material changes without complete approval and rollback records; and
- user/affected-party complaints, appeals, and remedy completion.

Do not optimize metrics in ways that discourage reporting, hide failures, punish dissent, or turn volume of outputs into a substitute for quality. A low reported incident count is not by itself proof of safety.

### 18.3 Governance review

At least once per major revision, an independent reviewer should test whether the ToG is coherent, enforceable, proportionate, accessible, consistent with current domain standards and applicable law, and effective at surfacing rather than concealing conflict. The review should include dissenting findings and explicit residual risks.

## 20. Relationship to current standards

The Totality of Governance is proposed as the umbrella. It must preserve the existing seven Universal Regulation laws, the absolute boundary, the five regulation domains, the `PASS / FAIL / INDETERMINATE` decision vocabulary, the current gate authority, risk levels, and audit history.

Where this draft restates an existing requirement, the intent is to connect and cross-reference it. During adoption, prepare a clause-level crosswalk showing: `ToG clause → existing governing clause → control/evidence → discrepancy → disposition → approval`.

No current governance standard becomes adopted or implemented solely because this draft refers to it. Its existing status remains as recorded until an authorized, documented change is made.

## 22. Proposed adoption and assurance gates

**Stage A — Review:** owner reviews charter, control matrix, templates, and gap register; disagreements are logged.

**Stage B — Crosswalk:** compare each ToG clause with existing Universal Regulation, gate authority, risk levels, audit v2, the Research index, the research plan extension, and root/encyclopedia documentation.

**Stage C — Resolve authority:** specify document precedence and what requires owner approval, independent review, ethics review, security review, or qualified legal review. Resolve conflicts without silently rewriting prior records.

**Stage D — Approve scope:** owner explicitly approves the version and the repositories/paths to be changed. This step does not itself mean the governance system is implemented.

**Stage E — Integrate and validate:** add files, update indexes and manifests, build crosswalks and registers, validate links/schemas, run tests, and preserve a complete change record.

**Stage F — Independent assurance:** a reviewer who did not draft the package assesses completeness, internal consistency, rights/ethics provisions, control feasibility, and open risks. Required ethics/security reviews are completed by suitable reviewers.

**Stage G — Remediate and re-audit:** resolve failures; leave unavailable checks `INDETERMINATE`; do not self-certify closure.

**Stage H — Adoption decision:** owner makes a recorded decision. Adoption of a governance document is different from adoption of individual domain controls and different again from technical enforcement.

**Stage I — Enforcement:** technical controls such as branch protection, required reviews, workflow checks, permissions, alerts, backups, and release restrictions must be configured and tested. Documentation alone does not prove enforcement.

**Stage J — Operation and review:** monitor controls, record incidents and exceptions, revisit the framework after significant change, and publish revised status only when evidence supports it.

## 23. Non-negotiable end condition

GAIA must not be declared fully governed, fully safe, fully autonomous, or completely verified because a master document exists. Governance is demonstrated through clear authority, working controls, inspectable evidence, independent challenge, documented exceptions, real remedies, and ongoing correction.

**The purpose of the Totality of Governance is not to make GAIA unquestionable. It is to make GAIA more understandable, more accountable, more challengeable, and safer to develop and use.**

# GAIA Totality of Governance — Control Matrix

**Stable ID:** `GAIA-GOV-TOG-MATRIX-001`  
**Revision:** `0.1.0`  
**Status:** Proposed — Review Draft

This matrix is a control map for the proposed Totality of Governance (ToG). It is not a completed audit, proof of compliance, or declaration that a control currently operates. `Evidence required` means what must be produced or inspected before a relevant control can be treated as effective.

## Status vocabulary

- **Documented:** a rule or responsibility is written down.
- **Designed:** a control has a specific implementation plan.
- **Implemented:** the control exists in the target environment.
- **Tested:** a defined test was actually run and its outcome recorded.
- **Independently reviewed:** a qualified person not responsible for producing the control or result reviewed it.
- **Verified:** evidence meets the stated acceptance criteria, within an explicitly bounded scope.
- **Indeterminate:** any required evidence or review is absent, inaccessible, or unresolved.

These states are not interchangeable. A file's existence proves only that the file exists.

## Required control-register fields

Each control register entry should include:

`control_id, domain, requirement, applicable_scope, source_clause, risk_level, overlay_tags, responsible_owner, decision_authority, evidence_required, evidence_locator, test_method, test_date, test_result, independent_review, exception_id, residual_risk, status, blocker, last_reviewed, next_review_due, revision`.

## Exception rules

An exception must specify why strict compliance is currently impossible or disproportionate, exact scope, compensating controls, residual risk, accountable human approver, start date, expiry date, and review trigger. A pending, expired, or unauthorized exception is not approval. No exception may authorize illegal action or bypass a non-waivable rights/safety constraint.

## Failure handling

- **Critical/high severity:** stop or isolate the affected capability where necessary; notify the accountable owner; do not resume until the required review and authorization exist.
- **Moderate severity:** implement a bounded remediation plan and prevent scope expansion while the failure remains open.
- **Low severity:** record, prioritize, and resolve within an approved timeframe; reassess if cumulative risk changes.

The risk-level mapping and thresholds must be reconciled with the existing `GOVERNANCE/RISK_LEVELS.md` before adoption. This draft does not change that file.

# GAIA Totality of Governance — Adoption Roadmap and Gap Register

**Stable ID:** `GAIA-GOV-TOG-ROADMAP-001`  
**Revision:** `0.1.0`  
**Status:** Proposed — Review Draft  
**Baseline reviewed:** 2026-10-24

## 1. Purpose

This document converts the proposed Totality of Governance into a reviewable sequence. It does not assign itself implementation status, authorize repository changes, or change existing gate outcomes.

## 2. Current governance baseline observed

The Foundation already contains a meaningful governance spine:

- `GOVERNANCE/UNIVERSAL_REGULATION.md` — seven laws, an absolute boundary, five regulation domains, and proposed derived-domain requirements.
- `GOVERNANCE/GATE_AUTHORITY.md` — repository owner is the decision authority in the current repository rules; agents cannot self-approve; `INDETERMINATE` is never `PASS`; merging is not a gate pass.
- `GOVERNANCE/RISK_LEVELS.md` and `PRODUCT_REGISTER.csv` — risk classification and product-level records.
- `AUDIT_UNIVERSAL_REGULATION_v1.md` and `v2.md` — preserved audits; the v2 record reports unresolved FAIL and INDETERMINATE items and remains INDETERMINATE.
- `LAW2_CROSSWALK.md` / `LAW2_CROSSWALK_COUNTS.csv` — mapping of the research cycle's four claim labels to the standard's six claim distinctions.
- `CHANGELOG.md`, `MANIFEST.sha256`, `AGENT_PUSH_LOG.md`, a repository `SECURITY.md`, `.github/CODEOWNERS`, and a verification workflow.
- `Research/README.md` and `RESEARCH_PLAN_EXTENSION.md` — methods, claim labels, lifecycle, traceability, responsibility ladder, and explicit separation of proposed, tested, reviewed, verified, and authorized work.
- `Research/WORK_QUEUE.md` — expanding report families, phase progression, the Good and Greater Good framework, and existing-work reconciliation tasks.

These are documented assets. The presence of a file is not proof that its control is enforced or effective. The repository's root README states that governance and research gates remain INDETERMINATE and implementation is not authorized; the new ToG must not erase that status.

## 3. Main gaps the ToG must resolve

1. **No single approved umbrella charter yet.** Existing standards cover important pieces but need a clause-level map showing how authority and domain controls fit together.
2. **No unified control register across all governance domains.** Existing product and audit records are useful but do not by themselves supply an end-to-end control-to-evidence matrix.
3. **Repository status synchronization.** Current queue, root README, governance index, current branch state, and audit references must stay aligned as work lands.
4. **Unresolved audit items.** The existing audit v2's open findings must be addressed from its own record; this draft does not assume the list has remained unchanged or that any issue is closed.
5. **Independent reviewers not yet named/recorded in the current baseline.** Required independent review and ethics review cannot be substituted by the author or an agent's self-check.
6. **Technical branch protection and settings.** Documentation says main-branch protection remains an owner to-do. Enforcement must be inspected in GitHub settings and tested; workflow files alone are not proof.
7. **No fully harmonized Good and Greater Good policy yet.** The work queue identifies the framework and transformation/protection companion as pending; their principles need crosswalks to current ethics lenses, risk levels, universal laws, and review gates.
8. **Cross-repository authority map.** The Foundation should own shared governance principles; the Official Encyclopedia should own catalog and encyclopedia-specific authority while inheriting shared governance controls through explicit references.
9. **Exception, appeal, and incident record integration.** Templates need to be fitted to existing schemas and privacy boundaries rather than proliferating duplicative registers.
10. **Operational evidence.** Controls such as access review, release approval, incident drills, backups, branch protection, external disclosure, and rollback need real test records where applicable.

## 4. Recommended stages

### Stage 0 — Preserve the baseline

- Capture the current commit IDs, tree manifests, governance artifacts, audit outputs, open PR/branch state, and applicable configuration evidence.
- Keep prior audit files unchanged.
- Mark all unknown configuration states `INDETERMINATE`; do not infer enforcement from a text file.

**Exit condition:** baseline snapshot is reproducible and its scope/date are clear.

### Stage 1 — Owner review of the draft package

- Read the Charter, Control Matrix, Templates/Gates, and this Roadmap together.
- Record requested changes and disagreements.
- Determine whether "Totality of Governance" is the chosen title and stable ID.
- Confirm which commitments are constitutional/non-waivable, which are mandatory standards, and which are operational procedures.

**Exit condition:** explicit owner decision to continue, revise, or reject the proposal. No repo mutation implied.

### Stage 2 — Clause-level crosswalk

Map each clause of the ToG to:

- the seven Universal Regulation laws and absolute boundary;
- gate authority and risk-level requirements;
- current v1/v2 audit lines;
- Research README and research-plan extension;
- Good and Greater Good master list and transformation/protection framework;
- the Encyclopedia's catalog authority, scope, research workflow, and provenance controls;
- rights, privacy, security, cultural knowledge, environmental, AI, and operational controls as applicable.

Classify each pair as `same requirement`, `extends`, `specializes`, `conflicts`, `missing`, or `not applicable`. Every conflict needs an explicit disposition and approving authority.

**Exit condition:** no unresolved high-impact precedence conflict; all unresolved issues have owners and status.

### Stage 3 — Confirm roles and domain owners

- Name independent reviewer(s) appropriate to research scope.
- Name an ethics/rights reviewer for work that triggers that requirement.
- Identify security/privacy and data stewardship responsibilities for applicable products.
- Define the owner decision points, emergency containment authority, escalation routes, conflicts-of-interest disclosure, and appeal path.
- Verify contributors' permissions and agent boundaries.

**Exit condition:** each mandatory review/control has a role and an actionable evidence requirement.

### Stage 4 — Harmonize controls and records

- Reconcile control IDs with `PRODUCT_REGISTER.csv`, `RISK_LEVELS.md`, the audit CSVs, and gate templates.
- Decide whether to extend current registers or create new ones; avoid duplicate sources of truth.
- Define risk-level crosswalk rather than replacing existing tiers without approval.
- Add formal links from the Encyclopedia to Foundation standards and back only where the relationship is authoritative.
- Ensure sensitive incident, credential, personal, and restricted cultural information remains access-controlled.

**Exit condition:** schemas validate; required fields and stable IDs are defined; owner approves their meaning.

### Stage 5 — Close existing audit findings

- Re-read the latest audit v2 and issue-by-issue evidence at the time of remediation.
- Separate documentation fixes from settings/configuration changes, independent review, ethics review, and policy decisions.
- For each failure, document action, evidence, reviewer, and retest; do not mark closed until the stated criterion is met.
- For unavailable evidence or reviews, retain `INDETERMINATE`.

**Exit condition:** a new, versioned re-audit explains what changed and what remains unresolved. The ToG draft itself is not audit closure.

### Stage 6 — Validate the governance repository tooling

- Verify manifest-generation and hash-validation behavior.
- Verify claim-source and crosswalk checks.
- Verify that CI tests the intended files and fails on the intended errors.
- Test branch protection, required review, blocked force-push/deletion, least privilege, and emergency recovery through evidence from actual settings and safe tests.
- Test template/schema validation with bounded fixtures and negative cases.
- Keep test fixture results separate from proof that broader claims are true.

**Exit condition:** execution records, results, known limits, and rollback instructions exist.

### Stage 7 — Pilot the Good and Greater Good Framework

Use a bounded, non-sensitive, rights-cleared fixture. Test at least:

- competing benefits and rights;
- missing or contradictory evidence;
- harmful output and refusal boundaries;
- transformation versus immediate containment;
- proportionate temporary access restriction with expiry/review;
- false positives and successful appeal/correction;
- culturally restricted material and lack of authorization;
- high-risk changes that must not proceed without explicit approval;
- transparency without revealing secrets or private data; and
- recovery and record preservation after a simulated incident.

**Exit condition:** test report, failures, adjustments, independent challenge, and an unresolved-risk statement. No public deployment follows automatically.

### Stage 8 — Adopt, enforce, and maintain

- Owner records adoption of the exact version and scope.
- Required domain standards are approved separately or explicitly marked pending.
- Enforcement settings and workflows are implemented and tested.
- Every product and project maps relevant controls and gates.
- A cadence is established for governance review and public status updates.

**Exit condition:** no claim that the whole system is implemented unless the complete relevant control set has evidence of implementation, tests, independent review where required, and recorded approval.

## 6. Required evidence for completion claims

Use the following minimum meanings:

- **Drafted:** text exists.
- **Reviewed:** a named reviewer examined the specified revision and recorded findings.
- **Approved:** the decision authority explicitly approved the specified scope/version.
- **Documented:** the rule/process is recorded.
- **Implemented:** the control is configured or operational in the stated environment.
- **Tested:** an actual test was run with recorded criteria and results.
- **Verified:** available evidence meets a bounded acceptance criterion; independence must be stated separately.
- **Independently verified:** an appropriate reviewer not responsible for producing the work confirmed the specified evidence.
- **Adopted:** formally placed under the project's approved governance process.
- **Operational:** required controls are operating in the specified environment, with monitoring and response arrangements.
- **Complete:** only within a defined scope, version and acceptance criterion; never unqualified if material blockers remain.

## 7. Go / no-go conditions

### Do not proceed with adoption or release if

- authority or precedence conflicts are unresolved;
- required evidence is missing or incompatible;
- a gate is `INDETERMINATE` but downstream action requires `PASS`;
- a required independent, ethics, privacy, security, or legal review is absent;
- there is no rollback, suspension, or containment path for a material risk;
- affected communities' authority or restrictions have been ignored;
- the action exceeds approved scope or permissions;
- the proposed benefit is used to hide severe, non-consensual harm or erase rights; or
- a claim of conformance, adoption, implementation, or verification exceeds the evidence.

### A bounded pilot may proceed only when

- its purpose, fixture, scope, and non-sensitive data are defined;
- its operators and decision authority are explicit;
- acceptance and failure criteria are written before execution;
- safeguards and containment controls are tested to the degree appropriate to the pilot;
- no external deployment or irreversible operation is implied; and
- the owner has explicitly approved the pilot.
